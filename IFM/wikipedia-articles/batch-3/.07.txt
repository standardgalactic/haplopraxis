   The [84]NBC [85]superhero [86]serial [87]drama series [88]Heroes
   follows the lives of people across the globe who possess various
   [89]superhuman powers as they struggle to cope with their everyday
   lives and prevent foreseen disasters from occurring. The series
   premiered on American and [90]Canadian television on September 25,
   2006.^[91][1] The first season, which finished 21st of 142 American
   primetime television programs in [92]Nielsen ratings,^[93][2] was
   released on DVD and [94]HD DVD on August 28, 2007.^[95][3] The Blu-ray
   was released on August 26, 2008.^[96][4] Within the seasons of the show
   are "volumes", which allow the writers to focus on shorter story
   arcs.^[97][5] The first season comprises a single volume of 23 episodes
   called Genesis, which is also the same title of the show's [98]pilot
   episode.

Plot

   [[99]edit]

   The plot of Heroes is designed to be similar to the stories of comic
   books with small story arcs built into the series' larger story arc
   plot. Each season of Heroes is designed to involve ordinary people who
   discover extraordinary super powers, and how these abilities take
   effect in the character's daily lives.

   The [100]first season, known as "Genesis", begins as a seemingly
   ordinary group of people gradually becomes aware that they have special
   abilities. Events illustrate their reactions to these powers, and how
   the discovery affects their personal and professional lives. At the
   same time, several ordinary individuals are investigating the origins
   and extent of these abilities. Mohinder continues his late father's
   research into the biological source of the change, while Noah
   represents a secret organization known only as "The Company". While
   coping with these new abilities, each of the characters is drawn,
   willingly or unwillingly, into the Company's conspiracy to control
   superpowered people and into a race to stop an explosion from
   destroying New York City. Meanwhile, focus is placed on Peter Petrelli
   as he tries to maintain his volatile power, and Sylar, a serial killer
   hunting superhumans.

Cast and characters

   [[101]edit]
     * [102]Milo Ventimiglia as [103]Peter Petrelli, the protagonist; a
       nurse and Nathan's brother, gains the abilities of evolved humans
       who come near him.
     * [104]Hayden Panettiere as [105]Claire Bennet, a high school
       cheerleader with the power of rapid cellular regeneration. Marc
       Hirschfeld, executive vice president of casting for NBC Universal
       Television stated, "When they were trying to decide who the
       cheerleader should be, I literally picked up the phone and said to
       the producers, 'You've got to meet Hayden Panettiere.'"^[106][6]
     * [107]Jack Coleman as [108]Noah Bennet, or "Horn-Rimmed Glasses"
       (recurring episodes 1 to 10), Claire's father and employee of the
       Company.
     * [109]Tawny Cypress as [110]Simone Deveaux, Peter's friend and
       Isaac's girlfriend.
     * [111]Leonard Roberts as [112]D. L. Hawkins, a convict who can walk
       through solid objects.
     * [113]Santiago Cabrera as [114]Isaac Mendez, a painter and drug
       addict with the ability to paint the future.
     * [115]Masi Oka as [116]Hiro Nakamura, an office worker who possesses
       the ability of space-time manipulation. This allows him to
       teleport, stop time, or travel through time, but recent events in
       the series have prevented him from regaining his full abilities.
       Hiro was one of the last main characters to be created by Tim
       Kring; he was added to the pilot episode after Kring's wife noticed
       none of the existing main characters were happy about their
       powers.^[117][7]
     * [118]Greg Grunberg as [119]Matt Parkman is a police officer with
       the power to read minds.
     * [120]Adrian Pasdar as [121]Nathan Petrelli, an anti-hero and New
       York senator who can fly.
     * [122]Noah Gray-Cabey as [123]Micah Sanders, Niki and D.L.'s son and
       a technopath.

     * [124]Ali Larter as [125]Niki Sanders, D.L. Hawkins' wife and Micah
       Sanders' mother. She is a single mother with [126]a dangerous
       alter-ego and super-strength.
          + Larter also plays Jessica Sanders, Niki's [127]split
            personality.

     * [128]Sendhil Ramamurthy as [129]Mohinder Suresh, a scientist who
       continues his father's research on super-powered humans.

Production

   [[247]edit]

Conception

   [[248]edit]

   Heroes began development early in 2006, when [249]Tim Kring, creator of
   NBC's [250]Crossing Jordan, came up with the show's concept. Kring
   wanted to create a "large [251]ensemble saga" that would connect with
   the audience. He began thinking about how big, scary and complicated he
   felt the world is, and wanted to create a character-driven series about
   people who could do something about it. Kring felt that a cop or
   medical drama did not have characters that were big enough to save the
   world. He came up with the thought of [252]superheroes; ordinary people
   who would discover extraordinary abilities, while still rooted in the
   real world and in reality. Casting directors Jason La Padura and
   Natalie Hart brought forth a cast of new faces such as [253]Milo
   Ventimiglia who described the pilot as a "character drama about
   everyday people with a heightened reality." A heightened reality that
   was brought to light through the work of production designer Ruth
   Ammon. Kring wanted the series to have touchstones that involved the
   characters and the world they lived in.^[254][31]^[255][32]

   Before he began putting his ideas together, he spoke with [256]Lost
   executive producer Damon Lindelof, with whom he had worked for three
   years on Crossing Jordan. Kring credits Lindelof for giving him ideas
   on how to pitch the series to the network and advice on the lessons he
   (Lindelof) learned about working on a [257]serialized drama. The two
   still speak and support each other's
   projects.^[258][33]^[259][34]^[260][35] When Kring pitched the idea for
   Heroes to the NBC network, he described the network's reaction as
   "excited...very supportive."^[261][36] He comments that he has been
   partners with NBC for some time based on his six-year run as
   [262]showrunner for Crossing Jordan.^[263][36] When he pitched the
   pilot, he described every detail, including the [264]cliffhanger
   ending. When NBC executives asked him what was going to happen next,
   Kring responded, "Well, you'll just have to wait and find
   out."^[265][37] After the project was [266]greenlit, a special
   73-minute version of the pilot was first screened to a large audience
   at the 2006 [267]San Diego Comic-Con.^[268][38] It was initially
   reported that this unaired pilot would not be released, however it was
   included on the first season DVD set.^[269][39]

Reception

   [[270]edit]

Critical reception

   [[271]edit]

   During the series' first season, the [272]American Film Institute named
   Heroes one of the ten "best television programs of the year."^[273][40]
   Doug Elfman of the [274]Chicago Sun-Times stated, "the show's super
   strengths are its well-developed filmmaking, smooth pacing and a
   perfect cast. It views like the first hour of a fun, thoughtful movie."
   Barry Garron at [275]The Hollywood Reporter also stated, "Heroes is one
   of TV's most imaginative creations and might, with luck, become this
   year's Lost."^[276][41] Less favorable reviews included the
   Philadelphia Inquirer, who commented that although the show had many
   "cool effects," it "lands, splat, in a pile of nonsense and dim
   dialogue." In response to the first pod of season one episodes, The
   Chicago Tribune went as far as saying, "you could watch the first few
   episodes of Heroes, or you could repeatedly hit yourself on the head
   with a brick. The effect is surprisingly similar."^[277][42]

   On [278]Rotten Tomatoes, the first season received an 83% approval
   rating, with average rating of 8.04/10, based on 18 reviews. The
   website's consensus reads, "The first season of Heroes promises a fresh
   take on the superhero genre, with enough style and foreshadowed
   intrigue to attract a following."^[279][43] At [280]Metacritic, the
   Heroes pilot received a 67/100, with generally favorable reviews from
   critics.^[281][44]

Ratings

   [[282]edit]
   [283][440px-Heroes_U.S._viewers.svg.png] U.S. viewers for each episode
   in the series, broken down into its five volumes.

   The pilot episode generated 14.3 million viewers,^[284][45] with the
   season high topping out at 16.03 million viewers for [285]episode
   9.^[286][46] When the series returned from hiatus on January 22, 2007,
   the ratings averaged about the same as the pilot with 14.9 million
   viewers.^[287][47] When the show went on a second hiatus during the
   first season, from March 4, 2007 to April 23, 2007 (7 weeks), ratings
   hit a new low; the lowest being 11.14 million viewers during part one
   of the three part finale, "[288]The Hard Part."^[289][48]

Awards and nominations

   [[290]edit]

   By the time Heroes completed the first half of the first season, the
   show had already collected an assortment of honors and accolades. On
   December 13, 2006, the [291]Writers Guild of America nominated the
   program for "best new series" of 2007.^[292][49] On December 14, the
   [293]Hollywood Foreign Press Association nominated the program for a
   [294]Golden Globe Award for "best television drama", and nominated
   [295]Masi Oka ([296]Hiro Nakamura) for Best Supporting Actor on a TV
   Series.^[297][50] On January 9, 2007, Heroes won the award for Favorite
   New TV Drama at the [298]33rd People's Choice Awards.^[299][51] The
   [300]National Association for the Advancement of Colored People
   nominated Heroes on January 9, 2007 for an Image Award in the
   "Outstanding Drama Series" category.^[301][52] On February 21, 2007, it
   was announced that Heroes was nominated for five [302]Saturn Awards.
   The nominations included "Best Network Television Series", "Best
   Supporting Actor in a Television Series" for both [303]Greg Grunberg
   and [304]Masi Oka, and "Best Supporting Actress in a Television Series"
   for [305]Hayden Panettiere and [306]Ali Larter.^[307][53] On February
   22, 2008, it was announced that Heroes was again nominated for five
   Saturn Awards. In 2008 it was nominated for "Best Television Series on
   DVD."^[308][53]

   On July 19, 2007, the [309]Academy of Television Arts & Sciences
   announced their nominations for the 2007 Primetime [310]Emmy awards.
   Heroes was nominated in eight categories, including Outstanding Drama
   Series. The first episode, "[311]Genesis", earned six nominations:
   Outstanding Directing ([312]David Semel), Outstanding Art Direction for
   a Single-Camera Series, Outstanding Single-Camera Picture Editing for a
   Drama Series, Outstanding Sound Mixing for a Comedy or Drama Series,
   and Outstanding Stunt Coordination. The episode "[313]Five Years Gone"
   also received a nomination for Outstanding Visual Effects for a Series.
   [314]Masi Oka was nominated for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Drama
   Series.^[315][54] On September 16, 2007, the [316]59th Primetime Emmy
   Awards were held and Heroes failed to win a single Emmy award despite
   the eight nominations. On July 21, 2007, the [317]Television Critics
   Association awarded Heroes with the prestigious Outstanding Program of
   the Year title during their 23rd Annual [318]TCA Awards
   ceremony.^[319][55] The cast of Heroes was named in the 2006 Time
   Magazine's [320]Person of the Year issue under "People Who
   Mattered".^[321][56]

DVDs

   [[322]edit]

   The first DVD release of Heroes was a sampler disc, containing only the
   first episode, and was released in [323]Ireland and the [324]UK on
   September 3, 2007.^[325][57] UK Region 2 split Heroes into two halves
   on its initial release; part one being released on October 1, 2007 and
   part two on December 10, 2007.^[326][58]^[327][59] When the second part
   was released, a complete first season boxset was also released on the
   same day on both DVD and HD DVD formats^[328][60] The complete first
   season DVD includes nearly 3 hours of bonus features including: an
   extended 73-minute version of the pilot episode with audio commentary;
   50 deleted and extended scenes; behind the scenes featurettes,
   including the making of Heroes, stunts, a profile of artist Tim Sale,
   and the score; and audio commentaries with cast, crew and show creator
   Tim Kring.^[329][61] On February 22, 2008, the Heroes Season One DVD
   was nominated for a 2008 [330]Saturn Award, in the category of "Best
   Television Series on DVD."^[331][53] The complete first season was
   released in USA and Canada on August 28, 2007.^[332][62] It was
   released in Australia and New Zealand on September 17, 2007.^[333][61]

   [334]Universal Studios Home Entertainment announced that the first and
   second seasons would be released on Blu-ray on August 26, 2008, the
   same date as the DVD release of the second season.^[335][63]
