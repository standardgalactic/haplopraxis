   Chemical compound

   1-Aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid (ACC) is a disubstituted cyclic
   α-[106]amino acid in which a [107]cyclopropane ring is fused to the
   C[α]atom of the amino acid. It is a white solid. Many
   cyclopropane-substituted amino acids are known, but this one occurs
   naturally.^[108][2]^[[109]verification needed] Like glycine, but unlike
   most α-amino acids, ACC is not chiral.

Biochemistry

   [[110]edit]

   ACC is the precursor to [111]the plant hormone
   ethylene.^[112][3]^[113][4] It is synthesized by the enzyme [114]ACC
   synthase ([115]EC [116]4.4.1.14) from [117]methionine and converted to
   ethylene by [118]ACC oxidase ([119]EC [120]1.14.17.4).^[121][5]

   ACC also exhibits ethylene-independent signaling that plays a critical
   role in [122]pollination and seed production by activating proteins
   similar to those involved in nervous system responses in humans and
   animals. More specifically, ACC signaling promotes secretion of the
   [123]pollen tube [124]chemoattractant LURE1.2 in [125]ovular
   [126]sporophytic tissue thus enhancing pollen tube attraction.
   Additionally, ACC activates [127]Ca^2+-containing [128]ion currents via
   [129]glutamate receptor-like (GLR) channels in root
   [130]protoplasts.^[131][6]

   ACC can be used by soil [132]microorganisms (both [133]bacteria and
   [134]fungi) as a source of [135]nitrogen and [136]carbon.^[137][7] As
   such, using ACC to incubate soils has been proven to induce the gene
   abundance encoding ACC-[138]deaminases, which may have positive
   consequences on [139]plant growth and [140]stress
   tolerance.^[141][7]^[142][8]

   ACC has also been extracted from [143]kelp.^[144][9]

   ACC is also an exogenous [145]partial agonist of the [146]mammalian
   [147]NMDA receptor.^[148][10]

   In 2019, the [149]United States Environmental Protection Agency issued
   notice of an application for an [150]experimental use permit to be
   issued for use of ACC as a [151]pesticide.^[152][11]
