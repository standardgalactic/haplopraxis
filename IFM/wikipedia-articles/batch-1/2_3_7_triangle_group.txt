   From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

   In the theory of [59]Riemann surfaces and [60]hyperbolic geometry, the
   [61]triangle group (2,3,7) is particularly important for its connection
   to [62]Hurwitz surfaces, namely Riemann surfaces of genus g with the
   largest possible order, 84(g − 1), of its [63]automorphism group.

   The term "(2,3,7) triangle group" most often refers not to the full
   triangle group Δ(2,3,7) (the Coxeter group with [64]Schwarz triangle
   (2,3,7) or a realization as a hyperbolic [65]reflection group), but
   rather to the ordinary triangle group (the [66]von Dyck group) D(2,3,7)
   of orientation-preserving maps (the rotation group), which is index 2.

   Torsion-free [67]normal subgroups of the (2,3,7) triangle group are
   [68]Fuchsian groups associated with [69]Hurwitz surfaces, such as the
   [70]Klein quartic, [71]Macbeath surface and [72]First Hurwitz triplet.

Constructions

   [[73]edit]

Hyperbolic construction

   [[74]edit]
   [75][220px-H2checkers_237.png] The (2,3,7) triangle group is the group
   of orientation-preserving isometries of the tiling by the (2,3,7)
   [76]Schwarz triangle, shown here in a [77]Poincaré disk model
   projection.

   To construct the triangle group, start with a hyperbolic triangle with
   angles π/2, π/3, and π/7. This triangle, the smallest hyperbolic
   [78]Schwarz triangle, tiles the plane by reflections in its sides.
   Consider then the group generated by reflections in the sides of the
   triangle, which (since the triangle tiles) is a [79]non-Euclidean
   crystallographic group (a discrete subgroup of hyperbolic isometries)
   with this triangle for [80]fundamental domain; the associated tiling is
   the [81]order-3 bisected heptagonal tiling. The (2,3,7) triangle group
   is defined as the [82]index 2 subgroups consisting of the
   orientation-preserving isometries, which is a [83]Fuchsian group
   (orientation-preserving NEC group).

Quaternion algebra

   [[117]edit]

   The (2,3,7) triangle group admits a presentation in terms of the group
   of quaternions of norm 1 in a suitable [118]order in a [119]quaternion
   algebra. More specifically, the triangle group is the quotient of the
   group of quaternions by its center ±1.

Relation to SL(2,R)

   [[122]edit]

   [123][220px-Morphing_of_modular_tiling_to_2_3_7_triangle_tiling.gif]
   Visualization of the map (2,3,∞) → (2,3,7) by morphing the associated
   tilings.^[124][1]

   Extending the scalars from Q(η) to R (via the standard imbedding), one
   obtains an isomorphism between the quaternion algebra and the algebra
   M(2,R) of real 2 by 2 matrices. Choosing a concrete isomorphism allows
   one to exhibit the (2,3,7) triangle group as a specific [125]Fuchsian
   group in [126]SL(2,R), specifically as a quotient of the [127]modular
   group. This can be visualized by the associated tilings, as depicted at
   right: the (2,3,7) tiling on the Poincaré disc is a quotient of the
   modular tiling on the upper half-plane.

   For many purposes, explicit isomorphisms are unnecessary. Thus, traces
   of group elements (and hence also translation lengths of hyperbolic
   elements acting in the [128]upper half-plane, as well as [129]systoles
   of Fuchsian subgroups) can be calculated by means of the reduced trace
   in the quaternion algebra, and the formula
