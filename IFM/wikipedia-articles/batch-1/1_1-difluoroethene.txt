   Chemical compound

   1,1-Difluoroethylene, also known as vinylidene fluoride, is a
   [141]hydrofluoroolefin. This colorless, flammable gas is a
   difluorinated derivative of [142]ethylene. Global production in 1999
   was approximately 33,000 metric tons.^[143][3] It is primarily used in
   the production of [144]fluoropolymers such as [145]polyvinylidene
   fluoride and [146]FKM.

Preparation

   [[147]edit]

   1,1-Difluoroethylene can be prepared by [148]elimination reaction from
   a 1,1,1-trihaloethane compound, for example, loss of [149]hydrogen
   chloride from [150]1-chloro-1,1-difluoroethane:^[151][5]

          [152]Preparation of 1,1-difluoroethylene from
          1-chloro-1,1-difluoroethane

   or loss of [153]hydrogen fluoride from
   [154]1,1,1-trifluoroethane:^[155][6]

          [156]Preparation of 1,1-difluoroethylene from
          1,1,1-trifluoroethane
