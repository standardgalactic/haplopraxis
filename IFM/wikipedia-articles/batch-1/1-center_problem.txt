   Combinatorial optimization problem

   The 1-center problem, also known as minimax problem or minmax location
   problem, is a classical [54]combinatorial optimization problem in
   [55]operations research of [56]facilities location type. In its most
   general case the problem is stated as follows: given a set of n demand
   points, a space of feasible locations of a facility and a function to
   calculate the transportation cost between a facility and any demand
   point, find a location of the facility which minimizes the maximum
   facility-demand point transportation cost.

   There are numerous particular cases of the problem, depending on the
   choice of the locations both of demand points and facilities, as well
   as the distance function.

   A simple special case is when the feasible locations and demand points
   are in the plane with Euclidean distance as transportation cost (planar
   minmax Euclidean facility location problem, Euclidean 1-center problem
   in the plane, etc.). It is also known as the [57]smallest circle
   problem. Its generalization to n-dimensional [58]Euclidean spaces is
   known as the [59]smallest enclosing ball problem. A further
   generalization (weighted Euclidean facility location) is when the set
   of weights is assigned to demand points and the transportation cost is
   the sum of the products of distances by the corresponding weights.
   Another special case, the [60]closest string problem, arises when the
   inputs are [61]strings and their distance is measured using [62]Hamming
   distance.

   The 1-center problem can be restated as finding a [63]star in a
   weighted [64]complete graph that minimizes the maximum weight of the
   selected edges. The corresponding problem of minimizing the maximum
   weight of a [65]path between two selected vertices, in place of a star,
   is called the [66]minimax path problem.
