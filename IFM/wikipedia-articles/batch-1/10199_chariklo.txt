   10199 Chariklo [203]/ˈkærəkloʊ/ is the largest confirmed [204]centaur,
   a class of [205]minor planet in the outer [206]Solar System. It orbits
   the Sun between [207]Saturn and [208]Uranus, [209]grazing the orbit of
   Uranus. On 26 March 2014, astronomers announced the discovery of two
   [210]rings (nicknamed [211]Oiapoque and [212]Chuí after the rivers that
   define Brazil's borders)^[213][22] around Chariklo by observing a
   stellar [214]occultation,^[215][23]^[216][24] making it the first minor
   planet known to have rings.^[217][25]^[218][26]

   A photometric study in 2001 was unable to find a definite period of
   rotation.^[219][27] Infrared observations of Chariklo indicate the
   presence of water ice,^[220][28] which may in fact be located in its
   rings.^[221][7]

Discovery and naming

   [[222]edit]

   Chariklo was discovered by [223]James V. Scotti of the [224]Spacewatch
   program on February 15, 1997. Chariklo is named after the [225]nymph
   [226]Chariclo (Χαρικλώ), the wife of [227]Chiron and the daughter of
   [228]Apollo.^[229][2]^[230][4]

   A symbol derived from that for [231]2060 Chiron,
   [232][14px-Chariklo_symbol.svg.png] , was devised in the late 1990s by
   German astrologer Robert von Heeren. It replaces Chiron's K with a C
   for Chariklo.^[233][29]

Size and shape

   [[234]edit]

   Chariklo is currently the largest known [235]centaur, with a
   volume-equivalent diameter of about 250 km.^[236][10] Its shape is
   probably elongated, with dimensions of 287.6 × 270.4 ×
   198.2 km.^[237][10] [238](523727) 2014 NW65 is likely to be the second
   largest with 225 km (140 mi) and [239]2060 Chiron is likely to be the
   third largest with 220 km (140 mi).^[240][8]

Orbit

   [[241]edit]

   Centaurs originated in the [242]Kuiper belt and are in dynamically
   unstable orbits that will lead to ejection from the Solar System, an
   [243]impact with a planet or the Sun, or transition into a short-period
   [244]comet.^[245][30]

   The orbit of Chariklo is more stable than those of [246]Nessus,
   [247]Chiron, and [248]Pholus. Chariklo lies within 0.09 [249]AU of the
   4:3 [250]resonance of Uranus and is estimated to have a relatively long
   orbital half-life of about 10.3 [251]Myr.^[252][31] Orbital simulations
   of twenty clones of Chariklo suggest that Chariklo will not start to
   regularly come within 3 AU (450 Gm) of Uranus for about thirty thousand
   years.^[253][32]

   During the [254]perihelic [255]oppositions of 2003–04, Chariklo had an
   [256]apparent magnitude of +17.7.^[257][33] As of 2014^[258][update],
   Chariklo was 14.8 [259]AU from the Sun.^[260][15]

Rings

   [[262]edit]
   Main article: [263]Rings of Chariklo

   A stellar [264]occultation in 2013^[265][7]^[266][22] revealed that
   Chariklo has two [267]rings with radii 386 and 400 km and widths of
   about 6.9 km and 0.12 km respectively.^[268][10] The rings are
   approximately 14 km apart.^[269][10] This makes Chariklo the smallest
   known object to have rings. These rings are consistent with an edge-on
   orientation in 2008, which can explain Chariklo's dimming before 2008
   and brightening since. Nonetheless, the elongated shape of Chariklo
   explains most of the brightness variability resulting in darker rings
   than previously determined.^[270][9] Furthermore, the rings can explain
   the gradual disappearance of the water-ice features in Chariklo's
   spectrum before 2008 and their reappearance thereafter if the water ice
   is in Chariklo's rings.^[271][7]^[272][23]^[273][34]
   [274][400px-Centaur_10199_Chariklo_%28NIRCam_Occultation_Light_Curve%29
   _%2801GQJ8KESB02G5QGRBZ64AFFAF%29.png] An infographic showing the
   apparent brightness measurement during Chariklo's occultation

   The existence of a ring system around a minor planet was unexpected
   because it had been thought that rings could only be stable around much
   more massive bodies. Ring systems around minor bodies had not
   previously been discovered despite the search for them through direct
   imaging and stellar occultation techniques.^[275][7] Chariklo's rings
   should disperse over a period of at most a few million years, so either
   they are very young, or they are actively contained by [276]shepherd
   moons with a mass comparable to that of the
   rings.^[277][35]^[278][7]^[279][23]^[280][34] However, other research
   suggests that Chariklo's elongated shape combined with its fast
   rotation can clear material in an equatorial disk through [281]Lindblad
   resonances and explain the survival and location of the rings, a
   mechanism valid also for the ring of [282]Haumea.^[283][36]

   The team nicknamed the rings [284]Oiapoque (the inner, more substantial
   ring) and [285]Chuí (the outer ring), after the two rivers that form
   the northern and southern coastal borders of Brazil. A request for
   formal names will be submitted to the [286]IAU at a later
   date.^[287][23]

   It has been confirmed that [288]2060 Chiron may have [289]a similar
   pair of rings.^[290][37]

Exploration

   [[291]edit]

   Main article: [292]Camilla (spacecraft)

   [293]Camilla is a mission concept published in June 2018 that would
   launch a robotic probe to perform a single [294]flyby of Chariklo and
   drop off a 100 kg (220 lb) impactor made of [295]tungsten to excavate a
   crater approximately 10 m (33 ft) deep for remote compositional
   analysis during the flyby.^[296][38] The mission would be designed to
   fit under the cost cap of NASA's [297]New Frontiers program, although
   it has not been formally proposed to compete for funding. The
   spacecraft would be launched in September 2026, using one gravity
   assist from Venus in February 2027 and Earth in December 2027 and 2029
   to accelerate it out toward Jupiter.
