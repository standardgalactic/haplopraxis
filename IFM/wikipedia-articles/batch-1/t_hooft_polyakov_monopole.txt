   Yang–Mills–Higgs magnetic monopole

   In [52]theoretical physics, the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole is a
   [53]topological soliton similar to the [54]Dirac monopole but without
   the Dirac string. It arises in the case of a [55]Yang–Mills theory with
   a [56]gauge group
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>G</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle G}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle G} , coupled to a [57]Higgs field which
   [58]spontaneously breaks it down to a smaller group
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>H</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle H}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle H} via the [59]Higgs mechanism. It was first found
   independently by [60]Gerard 't Hooft and [61]Alexander
   Polyakov.^[62][1]^[63][2]

   Unlike the Dirac monopole, the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole is a smooth
   solution with a finite total [64]energy. The solution is localized
   around
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>r</mi> <mo>=</mo> <mn>0</mn>
   </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle r=0}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle r=0} . Very far from the origin, the gauge group
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>G</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle G}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle G} is broken to
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>H</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle H}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle H} , and the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole reduces to the
   Dirac monopole.

   However, at the origin itself, the
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>G</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle G}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle G} [65]gauge symmetry is unbroken and the solution is
   non-singular also near the origin. The Higgs field
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msub> <mi>H</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mi>i</mi> </mrow> </msub> <mo
   stretchy="false">(</mo> <mi>i</mi> <mo>=</mo> <mn>1</mn> <mo>,</mo>
   <mn>2</mn> <mo>,</mo> <mn>3</mn> <mo stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle>
   </mrow> <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   H_{i}(i=1,2,3)}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle H_{i}(i=1,2,3)} , is proportional to
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msub> <mi>x</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mi>i</mi> </mrow> </msub> <mi>f</mi> <mo
   stretchy="false">(</mo> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mo
   stretchy="false">|</mo> </mrow> <mi>x</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mo stretchy="false">|</mo> </mrow> <mo
   stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle x_{i}f(|x|)}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle x_{i}f(|x|)} , where the adjoint indices are identified
   with the three-dimensional spatial indices. The gauge field at infinity
   is such that the Higgs field's dependence on the angular directions is
   pure gauge. The precise configuration for the Higgs field and the gauge
   field near the origin is such that it satisfies the full
   [66]Yang–Mills–Higgs equations of motion.

Mathematical details

   [[67]edit]

   Suppose the vacuum is the [68]vacuum manifold
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi mathvariant="normal">Σ</mi>
   </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \Sigma }</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \Sigma } . Then, for finite energies, as we move along
   each direction towards spatial infinity, the state along the path
   approaches a point on the vacuum manifold
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi mathvariant="normal">Σ</mi>
   </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \Sigma }</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \Sigma } . Otherwise, we would not have a finite energy.
   In topologically trivial 3 + 1 dimensions, this means spatial infinity
   is homotopically equivalent to the [69]topological sphere
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msup> <mi>S</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mn>2</mn> </mrow> </msup> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   S^{2}}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle S^{2}} . So, the [70]superselection sectors are
   classified by the second homotopy group of
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi mathvariant="normal">Σ</mi>
   </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \Sigma }</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \Sigma } ,
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msub> <mi>π</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mn>2</mn> </mrow> </msub> <mo
   stretchy="false">(</mo> <mi mathvariant="normal">Σ</mi> <mo
   stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \pi _{2}(\Sigma
   )}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \pi _{2}(\Sigma )} .

   In the special case of a Yang–Mills–Higgs theory, the vacuum manifold
   is isomorphic to the quotient space
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>G</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mo>/</mo> </mrow> <mi>H</mi> </mstyle>
   </mrow> <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   G/H}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle G/H} and the relevant homotopy group is
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msub> <mi>π</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mn>2</mn> </mrow> </msub> <mo
   stretchy="false">(</mo> <mi>G</mi> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD">
   <mo>/</mo> </mrow> <mi>H</mi> <mo stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle>
   </mrow> <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \pi
   _{2}(G/H)}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \pi _{2}(G/H)} . This does not actually require the
   existence of a scalar Higgs field. Most symmetry breaking mechanisms
   (e.g. technicolor) would also give rise to a 't Hooft–Polyakov
   monopole.

   It is easy to generalize to the case of
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>d</mi> <mo>+</mo> <mn>1</mn>
   </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle d+1}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle d+1} dimensions. We have
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msub> <mi>π</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mi>d</mi> <mo>−</mo> <mn>1</mn> </mrow>
   </msub> <mo stretchy="false">(</mo> <mi mathvariant="normal">Σ</mi> <mo
   stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \pi _{d-1}(\Sigma
   )}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \pi _{d-1}(\Sigma )} .

Monopole problem

   [[71]edit]

   The "monopole problem" refers to the cosmological implications of
   [72]grand unification theories (GUT). Since monopoles are generically
   produced in GUT during the cooling of the universe, and since they are
   expected to be quite massive, their existence threatens to overclose
   it^[[73]clarification needed]. This is considered a "problem" within
   the standard [74]Big Bang theory. [75]Cosmic inflation remedies the
   situation by diluting any primordial abundance of magnetic monopoles.
