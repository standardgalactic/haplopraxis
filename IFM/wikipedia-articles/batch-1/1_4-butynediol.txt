   Chemical compound

   1,4-Butynediol is an [131]organic compound that is an [132]alkyne and a
   [133]diol. It is a colourless, hygroscopic solid that is soluble in
   water and polar [134]organic solvents. It is a commercially significant
   compound in its own right and as a precursor to other products.

Synthesis

   [[135]edit]

   1,4-Butynediol can be produced in the [136]Reppe synthesis, where
   [137]formaldehyde and [138]acetylene are the reactants:^[139][3]

          2 CH[2]O + HC≡CH → HOCH[2]CCCH[2]OH

   Several patented production methods use [140]copper [141]bismuth
   [142]catalysts coated on an inert material. The normal temperature
   range for the reaction is 90 °C up to 150 °C, depending on the pressure
   used for the reaction which can range from 1 to 20 bar.^[143][4]

Applications

   [[144]edit]

   1,4-Butynediol is a precursor to [145]1,4-butanediol and
   [146]2-butene-1,4-diol by [147]hydrogenation. It is also used in the
   manufacture of certain herbicides, textile additives, corrosion
   inhibitors, plasticizers, synthetic resins, and [148]polyurethanes. It
   is the major raw material used in the synthesis of [149]vitamin
   B[6].^[150][5] It is also used for brightening, preserving, and
   inhibiting nickel plating.^[151][3]

   It reacts with a mixture of chlorine and hydrochloric acid to give
   mucochloric acid, HO[2]CC(Cl)=C(Cl)CHO (see [152]mucobromic acid).

Safety

   [[153]edit]

   1,4-Butynediol is corrosive and irritates the skin, eyes, and
   respiratory tract.
