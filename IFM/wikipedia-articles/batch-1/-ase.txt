   From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
   Suffix for enzymes in biochemistry
   Look up [56]-ase in Wiktionary, the free dictionary.

   The [57]suffix -ase is used in [58]biochemistry to form names of
   [59]enzymes. The most common way to name enzymes is to add this suffix
   onto the end of the [60]substrate, e.g. an enzyme that breaks down
   peroxides may be called [61]peroxidase; the enzyme that produces
   [62]telomeres is called [63]telomerase. Sometimes enzymes are named for
   the function they perform, rather than substrate, e.g. the enzyme that
   [64]polymerizes (assembles) [65]DNA into strands is called
   [66]polymerase; see also [67]reverse transcriptase.^[68][1]

Etymology

   [[69]edit]

   The -ase suffix is a [70]libfix derived from "[71]diastase", the first
   recognized enzyme.^[72][2] Its usage in subsequently discovered enzymes
   was proposed by [73]Émile Duclaux, with the intention of honoring the
   first scientists to isolate diastase.^[74][3]
