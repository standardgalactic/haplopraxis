   Chemical compound

   1-Diazidocarbamoyl-5-azidotetrazole, often jokingly referred to as
   azidoazide azide,^[108][5] is a [109]heterocyclic [110]inorganic
   compound with the formula C[2]N[14].^[111][6] It is a highly reactive
   and [112]extremely sensitive [113]explosive.

Synthesis

   [[114]edit]

   1-Diazidocarbamoyl-5-azidotetrazole was produced by [115]diazotizing
   triaminoguanidinium chloride with [116]sodium nitrite in ultra-purified
   [117]water.^[118][6] Another synthesis uses a metathesis reaction
   between [119]isocyanogen tetrabromide in [120]acetone and aqueous
   [121]sodium azide.^[122][3] This first forms isocyanogen tetraazide,
   the "open" isomer of C[2]N[14], which at room temperature quickly
   undergoes an irreversible cyclization reaction to form a [123]tetrazole
   ring.^[124][7]

          [125][300px-Isocyanogen_tetraazide_closure.png]

Properties

   [[126]edit]

   The C[2]N[14] molecule is a monocyclic tetrazole with three [127]azide
   groups. This ring form is in [128]equilibrium with isocyanogen
   tetraazide, an [129]isomeric acyclic structure that has long been known
   to cyclize quickly to the tetrazole.^[130][7]

   It is one of a family of high energy nitrogen compounds in which the
   nitrogen atoms do not have strong triple bonds. This instability makes
   many such compounds liable to explosive decomposition, releasing
   [131]nitrogen gas.

   This tetrazole explosive has a decomposition temperature of 124 °C. It
   is very sensitive, with an impact sensitivity lower than 0.25 joules.
   It is, however, less sensitive than [132]nitrogen triiodide.
   Decomposition can be initiated by only using contact or using a laser
   beam.^[133][8] For these reasons, it is often erroneously claimed to be
   the world's most sensitive compound.^[134][9]^[135][5]
