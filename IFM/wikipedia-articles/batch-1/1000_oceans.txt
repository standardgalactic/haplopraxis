   Song

   "1000 Oceans"
   [59][220px-1000_Oceans.jpg]
   [60]Single by [61]Tori Amos
   from the album [62]To Venus and Back
   Released September 9, 1999 (US); January 10, 2000 (AUS)^[63][1]
   Recorded May 1999
   Length 4:19
   [64]Label [65]Atlantic
   [66]Songwriter(s) Tori Amos
   [67]Producer(s) [68]Tori Amos
   [69]Tori Amos singles chronology
   "[70]Bliss"
   (1999) "1000 Oceans"
   (1999) "[71]Glory of the 80's"
   (1999)

   "1000 Oceans" is a song by [72]Tori Amos, released as the second single
   from her 1999 album [73]To Venus and Back. It reached number 22 on the
   [74]Hot 100 Singles Sales, though it did not chart on the [75]Hot
   100.^[76][2] The song deals with issues of love and loss, and is based
   on the singer's personal experiences. It was released on September 7,
   1999, and was generally well received by critics.

Background

   [[77]edit]

   Amos has explained that the idea for the song came to her in a dream.
   An old African woman was humming the melody to her, and she got up
   around 5:30 in the morning to record it.^[78][3]^[79][4] She describes
   writing the transition from the melody line around the words "I can't
   believe that I would keep, keep you from flying" to "and I would cry
   1000 more" as particularly difficult, and what took the longest
   time.^[80][3] The inspiration for the lyrics came when her
   father-in-law died, and she claims the song helped her husband deal
   with the grieving process.^[81][4] The lyrics contain reference to
   [82]Silbury Hill, an ancient mound in Wiltshire, England. This is a
   place often visited by Amos and her husband.^[83][5]

Video

   [[84]edit]

   The video for the song was directed by Erick Ifergan and filmed by Toby
   Irwin, and it was shot in a downtown Los Angeles parking lot.^[85][6]
   It shows Amos singing inside a glass booth. The booth is in a busy
   street, and as people walk by some stop and stare. Others are engaged
   in everyday activities, and at one point a full-scale riot breaks out
   in the street, while Amos behaves like a mere distant observer.^[86][7]
   The video had its TV debut on [87]MTV's [88]120 Minutes on October 24,
   1999.^[89][6]

Reviews

   [[90]edit]

   Reviewers generally had a positive attitude towards "1000 Oceans", and
   many mentioned it among the better tracks on the album. [91]VH1 said
   the song was "one of the most billowing songs she's written in a
   while".^[92][8] [93]The Tech called the melody of this song and "Lust"
   "powerful ballads" and "some of Tori's finest".^[94][9] Others were
   less impressed though; [95]Spin magazine called the album track
   "perhaps the most disappointing", and claimed Amos came across as a
   "[96]Celine Dion-[97]LeAnn Rimes rip-off".^[98][10]
