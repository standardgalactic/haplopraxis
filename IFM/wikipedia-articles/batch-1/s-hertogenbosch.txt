   's-Hertogenbosch (
   Dutch: [236][ˌsɛrtoːɣə(m)ˈbɔs] ^[237]ⓘ),^[238][b] colloquially known as
   Den Bosch (
   pronounced [239][dɛm ˈbɔs] ^[240]ⓘ), is a [241]city and
   [242]municipality in the [243]Netherlands with a population of 160,783.
   It is the capital of the [244]province of [245]North Brabant and its
   fourth largest city by population. The city is south of the [246]Maas
   river and near the [247]Waal.

History

   [[248]edit]
   For a chronological guide, see [249]Timeline of 's-Hertogenbosch.

   The city's official name is a contraction of the (archaic) [250]Dutch
   des Hertogen bosch — 'the forest of the duke'. The duke in question was
   [251]Henry I, Duke of Brabant, whose family had owned a large estate at
   nearby Orthen for at least four centuries. He founded a new town
   located on some forested dunes in the middle of a marsh. At age 26, he
   granted 's-Hertogenbosch [252]city rights and the corresponding trade
   privileges in 1185. This is the traditional date given by later
   chroniclers; the first mention in contemporaneous sources is 1196. The
   original [253]charter has been lost. His reason for founding the city
   was to protect his own interests against encroachment from [254]Gelre
   and [255]Holland; from its first days, he conceived of the city as a
   fortress. It was destroyed in 1203 in a joint expedition of Gelre and
   Holland, but was soon rebuilt. Some remnants of the original city walls
   remain.
   [256][300px-Braun_s-Hertogenbosch_UBHD.jpg] 's-Hertogenbosch in the
   16th century

   In the late 14th century, a much larger wall was erected to protect the
   greatly expanded settled area. Artificial waterways were dug to serve
   as a city moat, through which the rivers [257]Dommel and [258]Aa were
   diverted. 's-Hertogenbosch became the birthplace and home of one of the
   greatest painters of the [259]northern Renaissance period,
   [260]Hieronymus Bosch.

   Until 1520, the city flourished, becoming the second largest population
   centre in the territory of the present Netherlands, after [261]Utrecht.
   The city was also a center of music, and composers, such as
   [262]Jheronimus Clibano, received their training at its churches.
   Others held positions there: [263]Matthaeus Pipelare was musical
   director at the Confraternity of Our Lady; and renowned Habsburg
   copyist and composer [264]Pierre Alamire did much of his work at
   's-Hertogenbosch.

Eighty Years' War

   [[265]edit]

   The wars of the [266]Reformation changed the course of the city's
   history. It became an independent [267]bishopric. During the
   [268]Eighty Years' War, the city took the side of the [269]Habsburg
   ([270]Catholic) authorities and thwarted a [271]Calvinist coup. It was
   besieged several times by Prince [272]Maurice of [273]Orange,
   [274]stadtholder of most of the [275]Dutch Republic, who wanted to
   bring 's-Hertogenbosch under the rule of the rebel United Provinces.
   The city was successfully defended against Prince Maurice in [276]1601
   and again in 1603,^[277][7] but it eventually fell in the [278]1629
   siege led by his brother [279]Frederick Henry.^[280][8]

   In the [283]years of Truce, before the renewed fighting after 1618, the
   fortifications were greatly expanded. The surrounding marshes made a
   siege of the conventional type impossible, and the fortress, deemed
   impregnable, was nicknamed moerasdraak, or the Swamp Dragon.^[284][9]
   The town was nevertheless finally conquered by [285]Frederik Hendrik of
   Orange in 1629 in a typically Dutch stratagem: he diverted the rivers
   Dommel and Aa, created a [286]polder by constructing a forty-kilometre
   (25 mile) [287]dyke and then pumped out the water by mills. After a
   siege of three months, the city had to surrender—an enormous blow to
   Habsburg geo-political strategy during the [288]Thirty Years' War. This
   surrender cut the town off from the rest of the duchy and the area was
   treated by the Republic as an [289]occupation zone without political
   liberties (see also [290]Generality Lands).

Louis XIV to Bonaparte

   [[291]edit]

   After the [292]Peace of Westphalia, the fortifications were again
   expanded. In 1672, the Dutch [293]rampjaar, the city held against the
   army of [294]Louis XIV of France. In 1794 French revolutionary troops
   under the command of [295]Charles Pichegru attacked the city. It was
   only weakly defended, and fell after a short siege. Pichegru then
   crossed the rivers and put an end to the Dutch Republic.

   Under the new [296]Batavian Republic, established in 1795, both
   Catholics and Brabanders at last gained equal rights. From 1806, the
   city became part of the [297]Kingdom of Holland and from 1810, it was
   incorporated into the [298]First French Empire. It was captured by the
   [299]Prussians in 1814.

Kingdom of the Netherlands

   [[300]edit]

   The next year, 1815, when the [301]United Kingdom of the Netherlands
   was established, it became the capital of [302]North Brabant. Many
   newer and more modern fortresses were created in the vicinity of the
   city. A new canal was built, the 'Zuid-Willemsvaart', which gave the
   city an economic impulse. Trade, manufacturing and industry grew. Until
   1878, it was forbidden to build outside the ramparts. That led to
   overcrowding and the highest [303]infant mortality in the kingdom.

   At the end of the 19th century, the very conservative city government
   prevented industrial investment to avoid an increase in the number of
   workers and the establishment of educational institutions: students
   were regarded as disorderly. As a result, the relative importance of
   the city diminished.

World War II and after

   [[304]edit]
   [305][170px-Voorgevel_-_%27s-Hertogenbosch_-_20110416_-_RCE.jpg]
   [306]De Moriaan [[307]nl]

   One of the few official [308]Nazi [309]concentration camp complexes in
   [310]Western Europe outside Germany and [311]Austria was named after
   's-Hertogenbosch. It operated from January 1943, to September 1944 and
   was known to the Germans as [312]Herzogenbusch (see List of subcamps of
   Herzogenbusch). About 30,000 inmates were interned in the complex
   during this time, of whom about 12,000 were [313]Jews. In the
   Netherlands, this camp is known as 'Kamp [314]Vught', because the
   concentration camp was actually located at a heath near Vught, a
   village a few kilometres south of 's-Hertogenbosch.

   The city was occupied by German forces during [315]World War II from
   1940 to 1944. The railway station was bombed by planes of the
   [316]Royal Air Force on 16 September 1944. The city was liberated
   between 24 and 27 October 1944 during [317]Operation Pheasant by
   [318]British soldiers of [319]Major-General [320]Robert Knox Ross's
   [321]53rd (Welsh) Infantry Division following the victory of the 1st
   Battalion, [322]East Lancashire Regiment, of [323]158th Infantry
   Brigade over the enemy on 23–24 October.^[324][10]

   After the war, 's-Hertogenbosch was modernized, like many other cities
   in the Netherlands. It was possible that it was only the geography that
   shielded the old town from rigorous reconstruction in those early
   years. Just in time, the pendulum swung back to protecting the history
   of the city. In 1956, the council wanted to demolish the Moriaan, the
   oldest brick building in the Netherlands, to give traffic better access
   to the market square. The permit was refused by the government and
   instead the building was restored, starting in 1963. Later, city
   councils became much more aware of the value of historic buildings and
   from about the turn of the millennium, the historic fortifications are
   also given much attention by the authorities.

Economy

   [[349]edit]

   The city of 's-Hertogenbosch has become a center of industry,
   education, administration and culture. It is currently the fourth city
   of [350]North Brabant. It is home to many national and international
   businesses such as [351]Heineken, [352]Epic, [353]Tyco International,
   [354]SAP and many others. The [355]Jeroen Bosch Hospital is the biggest
   employer in the area, with over 4,000 employees.^[356][13]

Culture

   [[357]edit]
   [358][170px-Overzicht_-_%27s-Hertogenbosch_-_20110195_-_RCE.jpg]
   Typical street in 's-Hertogenbosch

   's-Hertogenbosch is home to a variety of events such as the theatre
   festival Boulevard, Jazz in Duketown, and hip hop in duketown, the
   start of the [359]Tour de France (1996), [360]Tour Feminin (1997), the
   [361]International Vocal Competition, [362]November Music (a
   contemporary music festival) and the UNICEF Open (formerly the
   [363]Ordina Open) grass court tennis tournament (in the nearby town of
   [364]Rosmalen). There are also over 350 restaurants, pubs and cafés to
   be found in the city.^[[365]citation needed]

   's-Hertogenbosch is also home to the European Ceramic Work Centre. This
   is a juried international ceramic residency where they invite artists,
   designers and architects from around to the world to explore the medium
   of Ceramics. This program was initially started in 1991 and continues
   to this day.

   The city has its own food speciality, the [366]Bossche Bol —
   effectively a giant [367]profiterole, somewhat larger than a tennis
   ball, which is filled with [368]whipped cream and coated with
   [369]chocolate.

   The spoken language is [370]Maaslands [[371]nl] (the variant spoken in
   's-Hertogenbosch is called Bosch which is placed among the Central
   North [372]Brabantian dialects, although other classification systems
   also describe it as [373]East Brabantian), which is very similar to
   colloquial [374]Dutch.^[375][14]

   [376]De Toonzaal is a [377]music venue for [378]chamber music,
   [379]improvised music, and [380]experimental music. For popular music
   there is the venue [381]W2 (or Willem II).

Museums

   [[382]edit]
   [383][220px-Brabants_Museum_main_entrance_building_Den_Bosch_at_21_May_
   2015_-_panoramio.jpg] [384]Noordbrabants Museum

   The [385]Noordbrabants Museum is a provincial museum with an overview
   of works that [386]Vincent van Gogh made in Brabant. The [387]Design
   Museum Den Bosch is a modern art museum. The [388]Jheronimus Bosch Art
   Center, is dedicated to the work of [389]Hieronymus Bosch. Other
   museums include the [390]Swan Brothers' House and [391]Museum Slager.
   Also the National (Dutch) Carnavalsmuseum Oeteldonks gemintemuzejum is
   located in the city. In the near future a new museum will be opened
   about the fortresses of the town and in general in
   Europe.^[[392]citation needed] The house where the famous painter
   Hieronymus produced his paintings can be visited on the market square.

Carnival celebrations

   [[393]edit]
   [394][220px-PeervaandenMuggenheuvel.jpg] Mayor Ton Rombouts, the Mayor
   of Oeteldonk, and the prince at City Hall in 2007

   's-Hertogenbosch has a strong [395]carnival tradition. In its current
   form the story and symbolism dates from 1881 to 1883. In these years
   some citizens created the legend of "Oeteldonk", whereby the city was
   renamed to Oeteldonk for the three day carnival. "Donk" is a reference
   to a dry place in the [396]marsh. The frog is widely used as a symbol
   during the 's-Hertogenbosch Carnival. It is also a symbol of the
   Oeteldonk [397]marsh.^[398][15] It was also a remark aimed at Bishop
   Godschalk from Den Dungen, where 'Van den Oetelaar' was a common family
   name. He had wanted to forbid the traditional festivities of
   [399]Shrove Tuesday that often led to excesses.

   Oeteldonk is a village and therefore every inhabitant is a farmer or a
   'durske' (a girl or young woman), eliminating class
   differences.^[400][16] The village is headed by the Mayor "Peer vaan
   den Muggenheuvel tot den Bobberd". Each year the mayor of
   's-Hertogenbosch hands over his authority to the Mayor of Oeteldonk. On
   Sunday at 11:11 AM the Mayor of Oeteldonk then receives Prince Carnaval
   "Prince Amadeiro XXVI" at Oeteldonk central station. From there a
   parade of all carnival clubs escorts the company to the town hall.

   The citizens of 's-Hertogenbosch wear traditional outfits throughout
   these days. A so-called boerenkiel is worn and every year patches are
   designed according to that years theme which can then be stitched onto
   the outfit. The boerenkiel is often combined with a traditional farmers
   bandana and a long scarf in the colors of Oeteldonk. The tradition of
   the Boerenkiel and / or Bandana is very different from the carnival
   traditions in the rest of the Netherlands. Other aspects like the
   parade, the temporary name and the temporary flag (for Oeteldonk red,
   white and yellow) are very similar.

   [402][220px-De_grootste_kathedraal_van_Nederland%2C_de_Sint_Janskathedr
   aal_in_%27s-Hertogenbosch.jpg] [403]Saint John's Cathedral

   's-Hertogenbosch was founded as a fortified city and that heritage can
   still be seen today. After World War II, plans were made to modernise
   the old city, by filling in the canals, removing or modifying some
   ramparts and redeveloping historic neighbourhoods. Before these plans
   could come to effect, the central government declared the city a
   protected townscape. Most historic elements have been preserved. In
   contrast to cities like [404]Rotterdam, 's-Hertogenbosch also survived
   the [405]Second World War relatively unscathed. Much of its historic
   heritage remains intact, and today there are always renovations going
   on in the city to preserve the many old buildings, fortifications,
   churches and statues for later generations.^[[406]citation needed]

City center

   [[407]edit]
   [408][220px-%27s-Hertogenbosch_Rijksmonument_21719_Stadhuis%2C_Markt_1.
   JPG] City Hall of
   's-Hertogenbosch[409][220px-%27s-Hertogenbosch_Marktplein_-_Den_Bosch_M
   arkt.jpg] Market square

   The city center has a cosy atmosphere because of the almost continuous
   ramparts that still surround it. It has been molded by the multiple
   rivers that convene on 's-Hertogenbosch, giving the center its strange
   street plan so different from the usual [410]grid plan where streets
   meet at right angles. The center is dominated by [411]Saint John's
   Cathedral (Sint-Janskathedraal in [412]Dutch), which dates from c. 1220
   and is best known for its [413]Brabantine Gothic design and the many
   sculptures of craftsmen that are sitting on almost every arc and rim
   along the outside of the cathedral. In 2010 an extensive restoration
   was completed, undoing the damage of many years of wear-and-tear and
   [414]acid rain.

   On the central square is the oldest remaining brick house of the
   Netherlands, 'de Moriaan',^[415][17] which was built at the beginning
   of the 13th century. In the 1960s, de Moriaan was renovated to its
   former glory based on a famous 16th-century Dutch painting called 'De
   Lakenmarkt van 's-Hertogenbosch' ('The fabric market of
   's-Hertogenbosch').^[416][18] The town hall is an original 14th-century
   Gothic building. After the town was conquered by the Dutch Republic in
   1629, it received a new facade in the style of [417]Dutch Baroque
   architecture. It showcased the authority of the new masters, just like
   the new town hall in Maastricht would.

   Hidden below the old city is a [418]canal network called the
   [419]Binnendieze, which once spanned 22 km (14 mi). It started out as a
   regular river, the Dommel, running through the city in medieval times.
   Due to a lack of space in the city, people started building their
   houses and roads over the river. Later, the Binnendieze functioned as a
   sewer and fell into disrepair. In recent decades, the remaining sixth
   part of the old waterway system has been renovated, and it is possible
   to take several guided subterranean boat trips through it.

Fortifications

   [[420]edit]
   [421][220px-Stadsmuurdenboschnl.jpg] City
   rampart[422][220px-%27s-Hertogenbosch_-_Citadellaan_-_Vestingwerken.jpg
   ] [423]Citadel of 's-Hertogenbosch[424][220px-Boze_Griet.JPG] Boze
   Griet, a forged cannon from 1510 in the Bastionder

   's-Hertogenbosch has an extensive and almost complete
   fifteenth-to-seventeenth-century city fortification. It was made to
   profit from the city's strong defensive position, lying on a sandy hill
   in the center of a large swamp fed by many rivers. This also caused the
   main ramparts to be preserved, because they were crucial in keeping out
   the water. In 2004 the city was awarded the title European Fortress
   City of the year. In the years that followed it restored many of the
   city defenses to much of their old glory.

   Apart from small sections of medieval walls, the main structure of the
   fortification is a late-medieval (fifteenth-century) wall. The upper
   sections were removed when cannon became more powerful, and polygonal
   [425]bastions were added, some after the conquest by the republic. Most
   of these have not been restored to their original height, but do
   maintain their brick walls. The [426]citadel in the north west of the
   city does retain its original height. Around the city itself many other
   fortresses can still be seen.

   In the north east of the old city, the hexagonal [427]gunpowder
   magazine, called Kruithuis^[428][19] is located close to the citadel.
   It is one of only a handful that still exist in the Netherlands, and
   was built when the city was still part of the [429]Spanish Netherlands.
   It is planned to become the museum of fortress 's-Hertogenbosch. One of
   the bastions of the fortress now houses the mini museum
   [430]Bastionder. It has been dug out in a bastion of the south side. On
   the inside it shows a unique wrought iron cannon, and an older bastion
   that was walled in by the current one.

Nature

   [[431]edit]
   [432][220px-Sint-Janskathedraal%2C_Het_Bossche_Broek%2C_%27s-Hertogenbo
   sch.jpg] View on the St. Jan from Bossche Broek

   On the south side of the city, the city center and walls still border
   the Bossche Broek, an old [433]polder that could never be made dry. In
   1995 the dyke of the Dommel broke and an enormous amount of water
   entered the polder. It also flooded and blocked the main Dutch highway
   A2. In order to prevent this in the future, the area was rearranged to
   store excess water in case of emergencies. In 2006 the area had been
   furnished with higher dikes and locks that allowed a controlled
   flooding of the polder and some adjacent areas in case of emergency.

   The Bossche Broek is now a 22-hectare (54-acre) [434]nature reserve,
   that stretches all the way to [435]Vught. It is connected to the
   Moerputten and Vlijmens Ven, with which it forms a [436]Natura 2000
   area. Rare species in the area are the [437]scarce large blue and the
   [438]European weather loach. The Moerputten sports the [439]Moerputten
   Bridge, a 600 metre (650 yard) long nineteenth century railway bridge
   and engineering feat. What is unique about the area is its close
   proximity to the city center.

Sport

   [[444]edit]

   The city has one professional football club, [445]FC Den Bosch. It is
   the 1967 successor of the professional branches of BVV (Bossche Voetbal
   Vereniging) and Wilhelmina. Both of them still exist as amateur
   football clubs. As a successor of BVV FC Den Bosch can claim the
   national championship of 1948. This championship led to the
   construction of stadium [446]De Vliert, which at one time had a
   capacity of 30,000. Due to the less successful years that followed, the
   capacity is now only 8,500 visitors. FC Den Bosch was the first club of
   Dutch international player [447]Ruud van Nistelrooy.

   's-Hertogenbosch is more successful in field hockey. It is home to top
   club [448]HC Den Bosch. The women's team in particular is a dominant
   force in the Dutch field hockey competition. The professional
   basketball club [449]New Heroes Den Bosch is also very successful. The
   city's rugby club is called The Dukes and dates from 1974. It is
   located at a very scenic location at the foot of the city walls.
   Because of the limited space, the club plays on [450]artificial turf
   and part of the accommodation is subterranean. The Dukes has the most
   junior members. It became the national rugby champion in
   2008.^[451][21]

   As regards events the city is host to the [452]Rosmalen Grass Court
   Championships, a combined [453]ATP Tour and [454]WTA Tour grass court
   tennis event played two weeks before the [455]Wimbledon Championships.
   The [456]World Archery Championships and [457]World Para Archery
   Championship were held here in June 2019. During these combined World
   Championships two separate venues were used: the Parade and the rugby
   fields of The Dukes. All finals took place in the arena at the Parade.
   The Parade is a historic square surrounded by high trees, situated at
   the foot of the nearly seven-hundred-year-old Saint John's Cathedral in
   the attractive center of 's-Hertogenbosch.

   The [471]Zuid-Willemsvaart runs from the Meuse just north of the city
   towards Maastricht via Helmond and Weert. In 's-Hertogenbosch it runs
   through the city proper, south east from where a bastion has been cut
   off from the citadel. Because of this route it was impossible to widen
   it further than for ships of [472]CEMT class II. Therefore, the
   [473]Máxima Canal of 8 km (5 miles) was dug just east of the city,
   creating a shortcut from the canal to the Meuse suitable for ships of
   CEMT class IV. On the remaining part of the Zuid-Willemsvaart west of
   the city is the industrial harbor of 's-Hertogenbosch. A marina is
   located in the center.

   's-Hertogenbosch is situated on the busy [474]A2 motorway, the most
   important north–south connection of the Netherlands. This connection
   was established with the opening of the [475]Dieze Bridge in 1942. From
   1961 the Utrecht-'s-Hertogenbosch section was 2 times 2 lanes. In 1970
   the A2 was rerouted to the east of the city. In 1989 it finally became
   a [476]controlled-access highway. In 1996 the section between
   's-Hertogenbosch and [477]Eindhoven became a controlled-access highway.
   The situation in [478]Maastricht was only solved in 2016, when the
   Koning Willem-Alexandertunnel was opened. On the east–west axis
   's-Hertogenbosch is on the [479]A59 motorway. The [480]A65 motorway
   between 's-Hertogenbosch and [481]Tilburg is a regional highway, but is
   not completely access-controlled.

   [482]'s-Hertogenbosch railway station is on the [483]Utrecht–Boxtel
   part of the railway stretch between Amsterdam and the Dutch
   industrial/tech center near Eindhoven. As a consequence north–south
   trains depart every ten minutes. On the [484]Tilburg–Nijmegen railway
   trains run on a more modest schedule. 's-Hertogenbosch railway station
   is also a major station for [485]Arriva buslines that serve the city
   and most of its suburbs. Other stations within the limits of the
   municipality are [486]'s-Hertogenbosch Oost railway station and
   [487]Rosmalen railway station. [488]Vught railway station is actually
   closer to the city center than that in Rosmalen.

   's-Hertogenbosch has attempted to adapt to the growing popularity of
   the bicycle in Dutch cities. A reasonable amount of bike paths has so
   far been constructed in the town. In 2011, the city was chosen as
   [489]Fietsstad 2011—the top bike city of the Netherlands for 2011. The
   details of the report were less jubilant and showed that it was really
   a prize meant to stimulate 's-Hertogenbosch to take further action;
   Hugo van der Steenhoven of the Fietsersbond: "In the past years Den
   Bosch has spent much energy, ambition, creativity and money to give
   cycling an enormous boost. This is a big achievement for a city where
   bicycle use is lower than in the rest of the Netherlands" (cyclist
   union).^[490][22]

Education

   [[491]edit]

   's-Hertogenbosch has multiple [492]vocational universities called
   Hogeschool in Dutch. The [493]HAS Hogeschool of about 3,500 students is
   focused on agricultural and food technology. [494]Avans Hogeschool is
   located in 's-Hertogenbosch and two nearby cities. The [495]AKV St.
   Joost is an art academy that is now part of Avans and dates back to
   1812.^[496][23] [497]Fontys Hogeschool also offers some education in
   the city. The [498]Jheronimus Academy of Data Science (JADS), located
   at the Mariënburg Campus in the center of 's-Hertogenbosch, and
   provides a number of [499]data science programs at graduate ([500]MSc)
   and post-graduate level ([501]PhD).^[502][24] It is a department of the
   [503]Eindhoven University of Technology and [504]Tilburg University.

   In [505]secondary education the [506]City Gymnasium is a [507]gymnasium
   (school) that originated from the Latin school of the city. It is
   comparable to a [508]grammar school and can trace its origin back 1274.
   The same type of education and all other types of secondary education
   are offered by a number of large institutes.

Religion

   [[509]edit]

   Religions in 's-Hertogenbosch (2013)^[510][25]
     [511]No affiliation (44.8%)
     [512]Roman Catholic (43.3%)
     [513]Protestant (4.1%)
     Other Christian denominations (2.1%)
     [514]Islam (5%)

     [515]Hinduism (0.3%)

     [516]Buddhism (0.4%)

   Roman Catholicism is the dominant religion in 's-Hertogenbosch, with
   somewhat more than 40% of the population counting themselves as
   belonging to it. Even so, attendance at mass is significantly lower
   than 40%. Three churches in the city center are still in use by the
   Catholic church: Saint John's Cathedral, Saint Catherine and the
   Monastery Church of the Franciscans nearby the railway station. Smaller
   churches in use by the Roman Catholic church are: Saint Anne's Church
   in Hintham, Saint Landoline Church in Empel, Saint Willibrord Church in
   Maaspoort, Saint Lambert Church (Rosmalen), etc.

   The Protestant religion has seen its share of believers in the city
   fall from 20% to about 4%. It is based in the [517]Great Church. The
   [518]Eastern Orthodox Church is a new church in town. It is based at
   [519]Saint Catherine's Church where Catholic worship services have been
   held again since 2021.

   The Arrahma Mosque has been built by the Moroccan community. The
   Turkish community has the Orhan Gazi Mosque.
