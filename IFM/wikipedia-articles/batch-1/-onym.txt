   The [137]suffix -onym (from [138]Ancient Greek: ὄνυμα, [139]lit.
   'name') is a [140]bound morpheme, that is attached to the end of a
   [141]root word, thus forming a new [142]compound word that designates a
   particular class of [143]names. In [144]linguistic terminology,
   compound words that are formed with suffix -onym are most commonly used
   as designations for various [145]onomastic classes. Most onomastic
   terms that are formed with suffix -onym are [146]classical compounds,
   whose word roots are taken from [147]classical languages (Greek and
   Latin).^[148][1]^[149][2]

   For example, onomastic terms like [150]toponym and [151]linguonym are
   typical classical (or neoclassical) compounds, formed from suffix -onym
   and classical (Greek and Latin) root words ([152]Ancient Greek: τόπος /
   place; [153]Latin: lingua / language). In some compounds, the -onym
   morpheme has been modified by replacing (or dropping) the "o". In the
   compounds like [154]ananym and [155]metanym, the correct forms
   ([156]anonym and [157]metonym) were pre-occupied by other meanings.
   Other, late 20th century examples, such as [158]hypernym and
   [159]characternym, are typically redundant [160]neologisms, for which
   there are more traditional words formed with the full -onym
   ([161]hyperonym and [162]charactonym).

   The English suffix -onym is from the [163]Ancient Greek suffix -ώνυμον
   (ōnymon), neuter of the suffix ώνυμος (ōnymos), having a specified kind
   of name, from the Greek ὄνομα (ónoma), [164]Aeolic Greek ὄνυμα (ónyma),
   "name". The form -ōnymos is that taken by ónoma when it is the end
   component of a [165]bahuvrihi compound, but in English its use is
   extended to [166]tatpuruṣa compounds.

   The suffix is found in many modern languages with various spellings.
   Examples are: [167]Dutch synoniem, [168]German Synonym, [169]Portuguese
   sinónimo, [170]Russian синоним (sinonim), [171]Polish synonim,
   [172]Finnish synonyymi, [173]Indonesian sinonim, [174]Czech synonymum.

   According to a 1988 study^[175][3] of words ending in -onym, there are
   four discernible classes of -onym words: (1) historic, classic, or, for
   want of better terms, naturally occurring or common words; (2)
   scientific terminology, occurring in particular in linguistics,
   onomastics, etc.; (3) language games; and (4) [176]nonce words. Older
   terms are known to gain new, sometimes contradictory, meanings (e.g.,
   eponym and cryptonym). In many cases, two or more words describe the
   same phenomenon, but no precedence is discernible (e.g., necronym and
   penthonym). New words are sometimes created, the meaning of which
   duplicating existing terms. On occasion, new words are formed with
   little regard to historical principles.

Words that end in -onym

   [[177]edit]
   Main articles: [178]Onomastics and [179]Nomenclature
     * [180]acronym: considered to be a "word" in its original sense
       formed from the initials of one or more words that is pronounceable
       like a normal word, such as [181]NATO, sometimes in distinction to
       [182]initialism; reflecting a historical development from its
       component word initials
     * agoronym: a name of a square or a marketplace.^[183][4]
     * agronym: a name of a field or a plain.^[184][4]
     * [185]allonym: an author's name of another person - often, a
       well-known person's name; an alternative term for a pseudonym
     * [186]anacronym: an acronym so well-established that its origin as
       an abbreviation is no longer widely known and its component
       initials are in danger of no longer being recognized (a blend of
       [187]anachronism and acronym)
     * andronym: a male name,^[188][5] or a man's name adopted by a woman
       as a pseudonym.
     * [189]anonym: something created anonymously, or its creator; an
       unknown author; this term now generally replaced by pseudonym
     * anemonym: name of a hurricane or violent
       wind^[190][1]^[[191]page needed]
     * [192]anepronym: a portmanteau of anacronym and [193]eponym; an
       original eponym of a trademark term that becomes so well
       established that it is used to define other objects that share its
       own definition (e.g., [194]aspirin)
     * [195]anthroponym: a proper name of a human being, individual or
       collective.^[196][6]
     * [197]anthropotoponym: a type of [198]toponym (place name) that is
       derived from an anthroponym
     * [199]antonym: a word with the exact opposite meaning of another
       word; an [200]antithesis: often shown in opposite word pairs such
       as "high" and "low" (compare with "synonym")
     * apronym: a word which, as an acronym or backronym, has a meaning
       related to the meaning of the words constituting the acronym or
       backronym; such as [201]PLATO for "Programmed Logic for Automated
       Teaching" alluding to [202]Plato, the philosopher and teacher
     * [203]aptronym: a name appropriate to its owner's [204]occupation or
       physical properties, such as "Goldsmith" or "Longman" (compare with
       "charactonym") — coined by Franklin P. Adams
     * asteroidonym: a proper name of an asteroid.^[205][7]
     * astionym: a name of a town or city.^[206][7]
     * astronym: a name of a star (or more loosely of a constellation, or
       other heavenly body).^[207][7]
     * [208]autoethnonym: an ethnonym of endonymic (native) origin,
       created and used by an ethnic group as a self-designation (see
       also: endoethnonym).
     * [209]autoglossonym or [210]autoglottonym: a glossonym / glottonym
       (language name) of endonymic (native) origin, created and used by
       native speakers as a designation for their language.
     * autolinguonym: a linguonym (language name) of endonymic (native)
       origin, same as autoglossonym / autoglottonym (see also:
       endolinguonym).^[211][8]
     * [212]autonym: Botanical nomenclature for an automatically created
       name. Not to be confused with onomastic [213]autonym, formerly used
       as a variant term for [214]endonym.
     * [215]backronym: an ordinary word understood as an (usually amusing
       or ironic) acronym (a portmanteau of back + acronym), such as
       [216]Fiat understood as "Fix It Again Tomorrow"
     * [217]basionym: the first name published for a biological taxon
       (species, genus, etc.), which remains the defining name for the
       taxon even when the taxon has been transferred to a new name
     * caconym: a bad name, either from poor formation (as through mixing
       Greek and Latin) or unpleasantness (as through lengthiness or
       cacophony)^[218][9]
     * [219]capitonym: a word that changes its meaning (and sometimes
       pronunciation) when it is capitalized, such as March and march or
       Polish and polish.
     * [220]charactonym: a name of a fictional character reflected in his
       personality traits, such as Shakespeare's [221]Pistol or
       [222]Bottom (compare with "aptronym")
     * [223]choronym: a proper name of a region or a country.^[224][10]
     * chrematonym: a proper name of a particular (unique) object, natural
       or artificially made. For example: [225]Hope Diamond (proper name
       of a famous diamond), [226]Bayeux Tapestry (proper name of a famous
       tapestry), or [227]Wanamaker Organ (proper name of a famous musical
       instrument).^[228][10]
     * [229]chresonym: Biol. use of a taxonomic name; historically
       sometimes referred to as a synonym. Sometimes divided into
       orthochresonyms (correct usages) and heterochresonyms (names
       incorrectly applied).
     * chrononym: a proper name of a time period, like the [230]Bronze
       Age, or the [231]Middle Ages.^[232][11]
     * cometonym: a proper name of a comet.^[233][12]
     * comonym: a name of a village.^[234][13]
     * [235]contronym or antagonym or autoantonym: a word that may have
       opposite meanings in different contexts, such as cleave meaning
       "stick together" or "split apart"
     * cosmonym: a proper name of a [236]cosmic feature, outside
       Earth.^[237][14]
     * [238]cryptonym: a [239]code name; a word or name used clandestinely
       to refer to another name or word
     * [240]demonym: a name, derived from a place name, for residents of
       that place (e.g., Utahn, from Utah, or Sioux Cityan, from Sioux
       City) — coined by George H. Scheetz, according to Paul Dickson in
       What Do You Call a Person From...? A Dictionary of Resident
       Names.^[241][15] The term first appeared in print in 1988 in Names'
       Names: A Descriptive and Prescriptive Onymicon by George H.
       Scheetz.^[242][16] See also [243]taxonym.
     * dromonym: a name of a road or any other communication or transport
       route by land, water or air.^[244][17]
     * drymonym: a proper name of a wood or forest.^[245][18]
     * ecclesiastonym: a name referring to members of a religious entity,
       e.g. Methodist, Protestant, Rastafarian, Wiccan (from Greek
       ekklisiastís 'churchgoer')
     * ecclesionym: a name of a church.^[246][18]
     * endochoronym: a choronym (regional or country name) of endonymic
       (native) origin, created and used by native population as a
       designation for their region or country.^[247][19]
     * [248]endoethnonym: an ethnonym of endonymic (native) origin,
       created and used by an ethnic group as a self-designation (see
       also: autoethnonym).^[249][20]
     * endolinguonym: a linguonym (language name) of endonymic (native)
       origin, created and used by native speakers as a designation for
       their language (see also: autolinguonym).^[250][21]^[251][8]
     * [252]endonym: a self-assigned name by locals of a place, or a group
       of people; formerly also known as [253]autonym, but that term is
       [254]polysemic (not to be confused with the [255]autonym in
       botany).
     * endotoponym: a type of [256]toponym (place name) of endonymic
       (native) origin, created and used by native population as a
       designation for some toponymic feature in their homeland.^[257][20]
     * [258]eponym: a botanical, zoological, artwork, or place name that
       derives from a real or legendary person; a name for a real or
       hypothetical person from whom a botanical, geographical, artwork or
       zoological name is derived; a person after whom a [259]medical
       condition is named, or the condition so named. A type of taxonym.
     * [260]ergonym: a name of an incorporated work-oriented entity, like
       a workshop, company or any firm in general.^[261][22]
     * ethnochoronym: a choronym derived from an ethnonym.^[262][23]
     * ethnohydronym: a hydronym that is formed from an
       ethnonym.^[263][24]
     * [264]ethnonym: a name of an ethnic group.^[265][25]
     * ethnotoponym: a type of [266]toponym that is formed from an
       ethnonym.^[267][26]
     * exochoronym: a choronym (regional or country name) of exonymic
       (foreign) origin, created and used by those who don't belong to the
       native population of a referred territory.^[268][23]
     * [269]exoethnonym: an ethnonym of exonymic (foreign) origin, created
       and used as a designation for an ethnic group by those who do not
       belong to it.^[270][20]
     * exolinguonym: a linguonym (language name) of exonymic (foreign)
       origin, created and used by those who are not native speakers of
       that language.^[271][21]
     * [272]exonym: a name used by one group of people for another group,
       but who call themselves by a different name, such as "Germans" for
       "Deutsche"; a place name used by one group that differs from the
       name used by the people who live there, such as "[273]Cologne" for
       "Köln".^[274][27]
     * exotoponym: a type of [275]toponym (place name) of exonymic
       (foreign) origin, created and used by those who don't belong to the
       native population of a region in which the referred toponymic
       feature is located.^[276][20]
     * gamonym: a name bestowed as a consequence of marriage. Judy Jones
       married Count Stephen Smith: her gamonyms include Mrs. Stephen
       Smith, Judy Smith, and Countess Smith.
     * [277]geonym: a name of a geographic feature, on Earth.^[278][28]
     * glacionym: a name of a glacier.^[279][29]
     * glossonym or glottonym: a name of a language
     * gynonym: a female name,^[280][30] or a woman's name adopted by a
       man as a pseudonym.
     * [281]hagionym: a name of a saint.^[282][29]
     * [283]hagiotoponym: a type of [284]toponym (place name) derived from
       a hagionym (name of a saint).^[285][31]
     * helonym: a name of a swamp, marsh, or bog.^[286][31]
     * heterochresonym: (biological taxonomy) see chresonym.
     * [287]heteronym: a word that is spelled in the same way as another
       but that has a different sound and meaning, for example "bow" as in
       "bow of a ship" or "bow and arrow" (compare "homonym")
     * hodonym: a name of a street or road (also [288]odonym).^[289][32]
     * [290]holonym: a word for the whole of which other words are part,
       in the way house contains roof, door and window; or car contains
       steering-wheel and engine (compare "meronym")
     * [291]homonym: 1: a: a word pronounced like another, but differing
       in meaning or derivation or spelling—also known as a [292]homophone
       (e.g. to, too, two). b: a word spelled like another, but differing
       in derivation or meaning or pronunciation—also known as a
       [293]homograph or heteronym (lead, to conduct, and lead, the
       metal). Compare autantonym, contronym, and heteronym. c: a word
       spelled and pronounced like another, but differing in meaning (pool
       of water, and pool, the game). 2: a namesake. 3: Biol. a taxonomic
       designation that is identical to another one of the same rank, but
       based on a different type; only one of the homonyms is considered a
       valid designation (see [294]homonym (biology)). Compare to synonym.
     * [295]hydronym: a name of river, lake, sea or any other body of
       water.^[296][33]
     * [297]hypernym: sometimes spelled hyperonym, a generic word that
       stands for a class or group of equally ranked items, such as "tree"
       for "beech" or "elm," or "house" for "chalet" or "bungalow." A
       hypernym is said to be "superordinate" to a hyponym.
     * hypocoronym, [298]hypocorism, or hypocoristic: a colloquial,
       usually unofficial, name of an entity; a pet-name or "nickname"
     * [299]hyponym: an item that belongs to and is equally ranked in a
       generic class or group, for example "lily" or "violet" in the class
       of "flowers"; or "limousine" or "hatchback" in the class of
       "automobiles." A hyponym is said to be "subordinate" to a
       hyperonym.
     * insulonym: a name of an island.^[300][34]
     * [301]isonym: 1: a word having the same root or stem as another —
       also known as paronym. Compare exonym, heteronym, paronym, and
       synonym. 2: one person's surname that is the same as another
       person's surname.^[302][35]^[303][36] 3: Biol. a taxonomic
       designation that is identical to another designation, and based on
       the same type, but published at a different time by the same or
       other authors (see [304]isonym (taxonomy)).
     * limnonym: a name of a lake or a pond.^[305][37]
     * [306]logonym: a polysemic term, with several meanings.^[307][38]
     * [308]linguonym: a name of a language
     * macrotoponym: a type of [309]toponym that designates an important
       toponymic feature, that has a wider (regional, national,
       continental, global) significance.^[310][39]
     * [311]meronym: a word that names a part that belongs to and is
       therefore subordinate to a larger entity; a part-whole
       relationship, such as "door" or "window" in "house", or "engine" or
       "steering-wheel" in "car" (compare "holonym")
     * meteoronym: a proper name of a meteor.^[312][40]
     * [313]metonym: a word that substitutes a part for the whole it is
       associated with, for example "crown" for "monarch"; [314]metonymy
       is the [315]figure of speech incorporating a metonym
     * matronym or [316]matronymic: a name of a human being making
       reference to that person's mother (contrast "patronym")
     * [317]mononym: a word indicating the "single name" as generally
       applied to people e.g. Madonna or Plato.
     * morphonym: a name of a taxonomic species.^[318][41]^[319][42]
     * microtoponym: a type of [320]toponym that is used locally, as
       designation for some toponymic feature that has only a local
       significance.^[321][43]
     * [322]necronym: a reference to or name of a person who has died.
     * [323]numeronym: is a number-based word.
     * oceanonym: a name of an ocean.^[324][44]
     * [325]odonym: a name of a street or road (also hodonym).^[326][32]
     * [327]oikonym, also (Latinized) [328]oeconym or [329]econym: a name
       of a house or other building.^[330][45]
     * [331]oronym: 1: a name of a hill, mountain, or
       mountain-range;^[332][46] 2: a neologism for same-sounding
       (homophonic) words or phrases.
     * [333]orthonym: The correct word for a concept in a specified
       language. By extension in some religious and ideological cults, the
       one that appears in the database of the ideological or religious
       registry of name (such as the church or nationalist civil
       register).
     * orthochresonym: (biological taxonomy) see chresonym.
     * [334]paedonymic: a name adopted from one's child's name, as in the
       [335]kunya of [336]Islamic names or when one is identified by means
       of one's child's name ("Tim's dad").^[337][47]
     * [338]paronym: a word that is related to another word and derives
       from the same root; a [339]cognate word, such as dubious and
       doubtful
     * [340]patronym or [341]patronymic: a name adopted from the father's
       or ancestor's name, for example "Johnson" from "John," "MacDonald"
       from "Donald," "O'Brien" from "Brien," or "Ivanovich" from "Ivan"
     * pelagonym: a name of a sea.^[342][48]
     * phaleronym: a name of a medal, or any other honorary
       decoration.^[343][48]
     * [344]phantonym: a word that looks like it would mean one thing,
       when in reality it means something completely different. Such as
       "noisome" meaning "smelly" or "unhealthy" and not "noisy".
     * [345]phytonym: a name of an individual [346]plant.^[347][49]
     * planetonym: a proper name of a [348]planet.^[349][49]
     * [350]plesionym or [351]near-synonym: word that is almost synonymous
       with another word, but not quite
     * poetonym: a fictional or literary name^[352][1]^[[353]page needed]
     * politonym: a name referring to members of a [354]political entity
     * potamonym: a name of a river or a stream.^[355][50]
     * [356]prosoponym: a personal name; full name of an individual
       person.^[357][51]
     * [358]pseudonym: a false and fictitious name, especially one adopted
       by an author; a [359]pen name
     * [360]retronym: a compound or modified noun that replaces an
       original simple noun, for example "analog watch" now means what
       "watch" used to mean before the invention of the digital watch, and
       motorcycles became "solo motorcycles" when others were built with
       sidecars
     * speleonym: a name of a cave or some other subterranean
       feature.^[361][52]
     * [362]synonym: 1: a word equivalent in meaning or nearly so to
       another word; a word that may be substituted for another word that
       has the same or a similar meaning, such as near and close (compare
       "antonym"). 2: In Biology, one or more names given to the same
       taxon, and so considered equivalent. Usually, only one of them is
       considered as correct (senior synonym in animal taxonomy, accepted
       name in plant taxonomy), while the other are considered deprecated
       (see [363]synonym (taxonomy)).
     * [364]tautonym: a [365]binomial or [366]scientific name in the
       [367]taxonomy of living things in which the [368]generic and
       [369]specific names are the same, such as [370]Gorilla gorilla; a
       scientific name in which the specific name is repeated, such as
       [371]Homo sapiens sapiens as distinct from [372]Homo sapiens
       neanderthalensis; a noun component that is repeated, such as
       [373]aye-aye or tom-tom; a [374]personal name where both
       [375]forename and [376]surname are identical, such as Francis
       Francis
     * taxonym: a name used for classification or identification purposes,
       usually signifying a relationship to something. Taxonyms include
       [377]binomens, names of [378]clades or [379]taxas, [380]demonyms,
       ethnonyms, and [381]eponyms. Examples include [382]canine,
       [383]hominid, and [384]Dryad.
     * [385]teknonym: a name that refers to a parent by the name of one of
       their children.
     * [386]textonym: a word that is generated by a single sequence of
       numerals keyed in to a mobile telephone; for example, 726 produces
       pam, ram, sam, and ran. Also called homonumeric words.^[387][53]
     * [388]theonym: a name of a god or a goddess.^[389][54] The names
       societies give their gods at times is useful in understanding the
       origin of their language as well as their view of a particular
       deity. Analysis of theonyms has been useful in understanding the
       connections of [390]Indo-European languages, and possibly their
       religions, in particular. In [391]Abrahamic faiths the origin and
       meaning of the [392]Tetragrammaton is sometimes deemed to have
       important historical or even metaphysical meaning.
     * theronym: a name — especially a product name — that has been
       derived from the name of an animal.^[393][55]
     * topoanthroponym: an anthroponym that is derived from a
       toponym.^[394][56]
     * topoethnonym: an ethnonym that is derived from a toponym.^[395][57]
     * [396]toponym: a [397]place or [398]geographical name; the name of
       an area of the [399]body, as distinguished from the name of an
       [400]organ
     * [401]troponym: a verb conveying a meaning that is a particular case
       of the meaning of another verb. For example, to duel is a troponym
       of to fight; to write is a troponym of to communicate; etc. The
       concept of troponym is to verbs as that of hyponym is to nouns.
     * urbanonym: a name of an urban element (street, square etc.) in
       towns and cities.^[402][58]
     * zoonym: a name of an animal.^[403][59]
