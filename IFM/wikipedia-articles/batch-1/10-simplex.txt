   In [90]geometry, a 10-[91]simplex is a self-dual [92]regular
   [93]10-polytope. It has 11 [94]vertices, 55 [95]edges, 165 triangle
   [96]faces, 330 tetrahedral [97]cells, 462 [98]5-cell 4-faces, 462
   [99]5-simplex 5-faces, 330 [100]6-simplex 6-faces, 165 [101]7-simplex
   7-faces, 55 [102]8-simplex 8-faces, and 11 [103]9-simplex 9-faces. Its
   [104]dihedral angle is cos^−1(1/10), or approximately 84.26°.

   It can also be called a hendecaxennon, or hendeca-10-tope, as an
   11-[105]facetted polytope in 10-dimensions. The [106]name hendecaxennon
   is derived from hendeca for 11 [107]facets in [108]Greek and [109]-xenn
   (variation of ennea for nine), having 9-dimensional facets, and -on.

Related polytopes

   [[127]edit]

   The [128]2-skeleton of the 10-simplex is topologically related to the
   [129]11-cell [130]abstract regular polychoron which has the same 11
   vertices, 55 edges, but only 1/3 the faces (55).
