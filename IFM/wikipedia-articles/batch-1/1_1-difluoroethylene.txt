   Chemical compound

   1,1-Difluoroethylene, also known as vinylidene fluoride, is a
   [140]hydrofluoroolefin. This colorless, flammable gas is a
   difluorinated derivative of [141]ethylene. Global production in 1999
   was approximately 33,000 metric tons.^[142][3] It is primarily used in
   the production of [143]fluoropolymers such as [144]polyvinylidene
   fluoride and [145]FKM.

Preparation

   [[146]edit]

   1,1-Difluoroethylene can be prepared by [147]elimination reaction from
   a 1,1,1-trihaloethane compound, for example, loss of [148]hydrogen
   chloride from [149]1-chloro-1,1-difluoroethane:^[150][5]

          [151]Preparation of 1,1-difluoroethylene from
          1-chloro-1,1-difluoroethane

   or loss of [152]hydrogen fluoride from
   [153]1,1,1-trifluoroethane:^[154][6]

          [155]Preparation of 1,1-difluoroethylene from
          1,1,1-trifluoroethane
