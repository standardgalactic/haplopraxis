   10 (ten) is the [277]even [278]natural number following [279]9 and
   preceding [280]11. Ten is the base of the [281]decimal [282]numeral
   system, the most common system of denoting [283]numbers in both spoken
   and written language.

Linguistics

   [[284]edit]

     * A collection of ten items (most often ten years) is called a
       [285]decade.
     * The [286]ordinal adjective is decimal; the distributive adjective
       is denary.
     * Increasing a quantity by one [287]order of magnitude is most widely
       understood to mean multiplying the quantity by ten.
     * To reduce something by one tenth is to [288]decimate. (In ancient
       Rome, the killing of one in ten soldiers in a cohort was the
       punishment for cowardice or mutiny; or, one-tenth of the
       able-bodied men in a village as a form of retribution, thus causing
       a labor shortage and threat of starvation in agrarian societies.)

Mathematics

   [[289]edit]

   Ten is the smallest [290]noncototient number.^[291][1] There are
   exactly 10 [292]small Pisot numbers that do not exceed the [293]golden
   ratio.^[294][2]

Decagon

   [[295]edit]

   Main article: [296]Decagon

   A ten sided polygon is called a [297]decagon.

Science

   [[298]edit]

   The [299]metric system is based on the number 10, so converting units
   is done by adding or removing zeros (e.g. 1 centimetre = 10
   millimetres, 1 decimetre = 10 centimetres, 1 meter = 100 centimetres, 1
   dekametre = 10 meters, 1 kilometre = 1,000 meters).

Mysticism

   [[300]edit]

   [301][200px-Tetractys.svg.png] The [302]tetractys
     * In [303]Pythagoreanism, the number 10 played an important role and
       was symbolized by the [304]tetractys.
