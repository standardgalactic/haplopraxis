   10 Hygiea is a major [226]asteroid located in the [227]main asteroid
   belt. With a [228]mean diameter of between 425 and 440 km and a mass
   estimated to be 3% of the total mass of the belt,^[229][11] it is the
   [230]fourth-largest asteroid in the [231]Solar System by both volume
   and mass, and is the largest of the [232]C-type asteroids (dark
   asteroids with a [233]carbonaceous surface) in classifications that use
   [234]G type for [235]1 Ceres. It is very close to spherical, apparently
   because it had re-accreted after the disruptive impact that produced
   the large [236]Hygiean family of asteroids.

Observation

   [[237]edit]
   [238][170px-10_Hygiea_2MASS.jpg] Image of 10 Hygiea taken by the
   [239]2MASS survey

   Despite its size, Hygiea appears very dim when observed from
   [240]Earth. This is due to its dark surface and its position in the
   [241]outer main belt. For this reason, six smaller asteroids were
   observed before [242]Annibale de Gasparis discovered Hygiea on 12 April
   1849. At most [243]oppositions, Hygiea has a [244]magnitude that is
   four magnitudes dimmer than [245]Vesta's, and observing it typically
   requires at least a 100-millimetre (4 in) [246]telescope. However,
   while at a [247]perihelic opposition, it can be observed just with
   10×50 [248]binoculars as Hygiea would have a magnitude of
   +9.1.^[249][12]

Discovery and name

   [[250]edit]

   On 12 April 1849, in [251]Naples, Italy, astronomer [252]Annibale de
   Gasparis (age 29) discovered Hygiea.^[253][13] It was the first of his
   nine asteroid discoveries. The director of the Naples observatory,
   [254]Ernesto Capocci, named the asteroid. He chose to call it Igea
   Borbonica ("[255]Bourbon Hygieia"), after the [256]Greek goddess of
   health, daughter of [257]Asclepius, and in honor of the ruling family
   of the [258]Kingdom of the Two Sicilies where Naples was
   located.^[259][14]

   In 1852, [260]John Russell Hind wrote that "it is universally termed
   Hygiea, the unnecessary appendage 'Borbonica' being dropped."^[261][14]
   The English form is an irregular spelling of Greek Hygieia or Hygeia
   (Latin Hygea or Hygia).^[262][15]

Symbol

   [[263]edit]

   The intended astronomical symbol for Hygiea was a [264]zeta-shaped
   serpent crowned with a star, in the pipeline for [265]Unicode 17.0 as
   U+1F779 🝹 ([266] A zeta-shaped serpent crowned with a star
   );^[267][16]^[268][17] the serpent and serpent [269]drinking from a
   bowl are traditional symbols of the goddess Hygieia (cf. U+1F54F 🕏). In
   later years it was substituted with a [270]rod of Asclepius: [271]rod
   of Asclepius (a serpent twined around a staff, U+2695 ⚕), confusing
   Hygieia with her masculine counterpart. These symbols are now both
   largely obsolete. In this century, 10 Hygiea has seen some minor
   astrological use, and its symbol was confused once again, with
   Asclepsius's rod replaced by Mercury's [272]caduceus: [273]Astrological
   symbol for Hygiea ,^[274][18] though in a more elaborate form (U+2BDA
   ⯚) than the symbol of the planet Mercury. The caduceus has [275]long
   been mistaken for the rod of Asclepius.

Physical characteristics

   [[276]edit]
   [277][220px-The_Four_Largest_Asteroids.jpg] Relative sizes of the four
   largest asteroids. Hygiea is furthest
   right.[278][220px-Hygiea_imaging_SPHERE.png] VLT-SPHERE images of
   asteroid 10 Hygiea, taken in June/July 2017. A bright surface feature
   and several dark craters are visible on the surface. At the time, the
   southern hemisphere of Hygiea was facing
   Earth.[279][220px-Iau_dozen.jpg] The [280]IAU 2006 draft proposal
   listed Hygiea as a potential planet.^[281][19]

   Observations taken with the [282]Very Large Telescope's [283]SPHERE
   imager in 2017 and 2018 revealed that Hygiea is nearly spherical and is
   close to a [284]hydrostatic equilibrium shape.^[285][7] Based on
   spectral evidence, Hygiea's surface is thought to consist of primitive
   [286]carbonaceous materials similar to those found in [287]carbonaceous
   chondrite [288]meteorites. [289]Aqueous alteration products have been
   detected on its surface, which could indicate the presence of water ice
   in the past which was heated sufficiently to melt.^[290][15] The
   primitive present surface composition indicates that Hygiea had not
   melted during the early period of Solar System formation.^[291][15]
   However, observations suggest Hygiea suffered a major collision early
   in its history that completely disrupted it, with its present spherical
   shape due to re-accretion of the disrupted material.^[292][6] No deep
   basins are visible in VLT images, indicating that any large craters
   that formed after re-accretion must have flat floors, consistent with
   an icy C-type composition.^[293][7]

   In images taken with the VLT in 2017, a bright surface feature is
   visible, as well as at least two dark craters, which have been
   informally^[294][b] named Serpens and Calix after the Latin words for
   'snake' and 'cup', respectively.^[295][8] Serpens has a diameter of
   180 km, Calix of 90 km.^[296][8]^[297][7]

   Hygiea is the largest of the class of dark [298]C-type asteroids that
   are dominant in the outer asteroid belt, beyond the [299]Kirkwood gap
   at 2.82 AU.^[300][20] Its mean diameter 433±8 km.^[301][6] Hygiea is
   close to spherical, with an axis ratio of 0.94±0.05 that is consistent
   with a [302]MacLaurin ellipsoid.^[303][6] Aside from being the smallest
   of the "big four", Hygiea has a relatively low density of
   2.06±0.20,^[304][6] comparable to Ceres (2.16) and the larger icy
   satellites of the Solar System (Ganymede 1.94, Callisto 1.83, Titan
   1.88, Triton 2.06) rather than to Pallas (2.9±0.1) or Vesta (3.45).

   Although it is the largest body in its region, due to its dark surface
   and farther-than-average distance from the Sun, Hygiea appears very dim
   when observed from Earth. In fact, it is the third dimmest of the first
   twenty-three asteroids discovered, with only [305]13 Egeria and [306]17
   Thetis having lower mean opposition [307]magnitudes.^[308][21] At most
   oppositions, Hygiea has a [309]magnitude of around +10.2,^[310][21]
   which is as much as four orders fainter than Vesta, and observation
   calls for at least a 4-inch (100 mm) [311]telescope to
   resolve.^[312][22] However, at a perihelic opposition, Hygiea can reach
   +9.1 magnitude and may just be resolvable with 10 × 50 [313]binoculars,
   unlike the next two largest asteroids in the asteroid belt, [314]704
   Interamnia and [315]511 Davida, which are always beyond binocular
   visibility.^[316][12]

   A total of 17 [317]stellar [318]occultations by Hygiea have been
   tracked by Earth-based astronomers,^[319][23]^[320][24] including two
   (in 2002 and 2014) that were seen by a large number of observers. The
   observations have been used to constrain Hygiea's size, shape and
   rotation axis.^[321][25] The [322]Hubble Space Telescope has resolved
   the asteroid and ruled out the presence of any orbiting companions
   larger than about 16 kilometres (9.9 mi) in diameter.^[323][26]

Orbit and rotation

   [[324]edit]
   [325][220px-AnimatedOrbitOf10Hygiea.gif] Animated orbit of Hygiea
   relative to the orbits of the terrestrial planets and Jupiter

   Orbiting at an average of 3.14 AU from the Sun, Hygiea is the most
   distant of the "big four" asteroids. It lies closer to the
   [326]ecliptic as well, with an orbital inclination of 4°.^[327][15] Its
   orbit is less circular than those of Ceres or Vesta, with an
   eccentricity of around 0.12.^[328][1] Its [329]perihelion is at a quite
   similar longitude to those of Vesta and Ceres, though its ascending and
   descending nodes are opposite to the corresponding ones for those
   objects. Although its perihelion is extremely close to the mean
   distance of Ceres and Pallas, a collision between Hygiea and its larger
   companions is impossible because at that distance they are always on
   opposite sides of the ecliptic.^[[330]citation needed] In 2056, Hygiea
   will pass 0.025 AU from Ceres, and then in 2063, Hygiea will pass
   0.020 AU from Pallas.^[331][27]^[[332]failed verification] At
   [333]aphelion Hygiea reaches out to the extreme edge of the asteroid
   belt at the perihelia of the [334]Hilda family, which is in a 3:2
   [335]orbital resonance with Jupiter.^[336][28]

   As one of the most massive asteroids, Hygiea is used by the [337]Minor
   Planet Center to calculate [338]perturbations.^[339][29]

   Hygiea is in an unstable three-body [340]mean motion resonance with
   Jupiter and Saturn.^[341][30] The computed [342]Lyapunov time for this
   asteroid is 30,000 years, indicating that it occupies a chaotic orbit
   that will change randomly over time because of [343]gravitational
   perturbations by the planets.^[344][30] It is the lowest numbered
   asteroid in such a resonance (the next lowest numbered being [345]70
   Panopaea).^[346][30]^[347][31]

   Hygiea has a rotation period of 13.83 hours.^[348][7] Its single-peaked
   light curve has an amplitude of 0.27 mag,^[349][8] which is largely
   attributed to [350]albedo variations.^[351][7] Hygiea's north pole
   points towards [352]ecliptic longitude 306°±3° and ecliptic latitude
   −29°±3°,^[353][6] which gives an [354]axial tilt of 119° with respect
   to the [355]ecliptic.^[356][6]

Hygiea family

   [[357]edit]
   [358][330px-Hygiea_family.png] Location and structure of the Hygiea
   family
   Main article: [359]Hygiea family

   Hygiea is the main member of the Hygiean [360]asteroid family that
   constitutes about 1% of asteroids in the main belt.^[[361]citation
   needed] The family was formed when an object with a diameter of about
   100 km collided with proto-Hygiea about 2 billion years ago. Because
   the impact craters on Hygiea today are too small to contain the volume
   of ejected material, it is thought that Hygiea was completely disrupted
   by the impact and that the majority of the debris recoalesced after the
   pieces that formed the rest of the family had escaped. Hygiea contains
   almost all the mass (over 98%) of the family.^[362][7]
