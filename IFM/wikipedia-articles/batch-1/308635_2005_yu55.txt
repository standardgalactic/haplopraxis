   (308635) 2005 YU[55], [154]provisionally named 2005 YU[55], is a
   [155]potentially hazardous asteroid^[156][2] 360±40 [157]meters in
   diameter, as measured after its Earth flyby.^[158][8] Previously it was
   estimated to be 310 meters^[159][5] or about 400 m (1,300 [160]feet) in
   diameter.^[161][6]^[162][7] It was discovered on 28 December 2005 by
   [163]Robert S. McMillan at [164]Steward Observatory, Kitt
   Peak.^[165][1] On 8 November 2011 it passed 0.85 [166]lunar distances
   (324,900 [167]kilometers; 201,900 [168]miles)^[169][11] from Earth.

8 November 2011 flyby

   [[170]edit]
   [171]Animation of the trajectory of asteroid 2005 YU55 compared with
   the orbits of Earth and the Moon on 8–9 November 2011. Trajectory of
   asteroid 2005 YU[55] compared with the orbits of [172]Earth and the
   [173]Moon on 8–9 November 2011.[174]Skymap showing apparent trajectory
   of 2005 YU55. Apparent trajectory on 8–9 November 2011.

   In February 2010, (308635) 2005 YU[55] was rated 1 on the [175]Torino
   Scale for a potential pass near Earth on 10 November 2103,^[176][12]
   that posed no unusual level of danger. On 19 April 2010, [177]radar
   ranging by the [178]Arecibo radio telescope reduced uncertainties about
   the orbit by 50%.^[179][7] This improvement eliminated any possibility
   of an impact with Earth within the next 100 years.^[180][7] It was
   removed from the [181]Sentry Risk Table on 22 April 2010 and as such it
   now has a rating of 0 on the Torino Scale.^[182][13] It is now known
   that on 10 November 2103 (308635) 2005 YU[55] will be roughly 2 [183]AU
   from Earth.^[184][14]

   On 8 November 2011 at 23:28 [185]UT the asteroid passed 0.85 [186]LD
   (330,000 [187]km; 200,000 [188]mi) from Earth.^[189][11] On 9 November
   2011 at 07:13 UT the asteroid passed 0.6231 LD (239,500 km; 148,800 mi)
   from the Moon.^[190][15] During the close approach the asteroid reached
   about [191]apparent magnitude 11,^[192][6]^[193][16] and would have
   been visible to experienced observers using high-end [194]binoculars
   with an [195]objective lens of 80^+ [196]mm if it were not for bright
   moonlight preventing a true [197]dark sky. Since the [198]gibbous moon
   did interfere with the viewing,^[199][3] observers trying to visually
   locate the asteroid required a telescope with an aperture of 6 inches
   (15 centimeters) or larger.^[200][3]^[201][11]

   The next few times a known asteroid this large will come this close to
   Earth will be in 2028^[202][3] when [203](153814) 2001 WN5 passes
   0.65 LD from Earth,^[204][17] and in 2029 when the 325-meter [205]99942
   Apophis comes even closer at just 0.10 LD.^[206][18]

   According to [207]Jay Melosh, if an asteroid the size of (308635) 2005
   YU[55] (~400 m across) were to hit land, it would create a [208]crater
   6.3 km (3.9 mi) across, 518 m (1,699 ft) deep and generate a
   seven-[209]magnitude-equivalent-earthquake.^[210][19] The chances of an
   actual collision with an asteroid like (308635) 2005 YU[55] is about
   1 percent in the next thousand years.^[211][19]

Study

   [[281]edit]
   [282]Swift captures flyby of asteroid 2005 YU[55].

   During the 2011 passage (308635) 2005 YU[55] was studied with radar
   using [283]Goldstone, [284]Arecibo, the [285]Very Long Baseline Array,
   and the [286]Green Bank Telescope.^[287][3] The [288]Herschel Space
   Observatory has made far-[289]infrared measurements of (308635) 2005
   YU[55] on 10 November, helping determine its temperature and
   composition.^[290][20]

   Radar analysis has also helped to pin down the asteroid's [291]albedo,
   or [292]diffuse reflectivity. Although radar measurements do not detect
   visible light they can determine the distance and size of an object
   with a high degree of accuracy. This information, coupled with visible
   light measurements, provides a more accurate measure of an object's
   [293]absolute magnitude, and therefore its albedo.

   On 8 November 2011, NASA released a statement mentioning a number of
   structures on the surface of the asteroid, which were detected as it
   passed near Earth.^[294][21] On 11 November 2011, higher-resolution
   images showed concavities, a ridge near the asteroid's equator, and
   numerous features interpreted as decameter-scale
   boulders.^[295][8]^[296][22] Shape modeling based on the radar images
   shows that YU55's shape is close to spheroidal, with maximum dimensions
   of 360±40 m, and an equator-aligned ridge. A 150–200 meter-long,
   ~20 meter-high rise forms a portion of the ridge-line, and the number
   of boulders on the surface is comparable to that seen on the asteroid
   [297]25143 Itokawa by the Hayabusa spacecraft.^[298][8]

   Optical lightcurve measurements during the flyby provided a more
   accurate estimate of the asteroid's spin period – about
   19.3 hours.^[299][9] Because (308635) 2005 YU[55] is so nearly
   spheroidal, it was not significantly torqued by [300]Earth's tides
   during the flyby, and there is no evidence of [301]non-principal-axis
   rotation. Optical, near-infrared,^[302][10] and ultraviolet^[303][23]
   spectroscopy confirmed that 2005 YU[55] is a [304]C-type asteroid.

Future trajectory

   [[305]edit]

   On 19 January 2029, (308635) 2005 YU[55] will pass 0.0023 AU
   (340,000 km; 210,000 mi) from [306]Venus.^[307][15] The close approach
   distance to Venus in 2029 will determine how close the asteroid will
   pass to Earth in 2041.^[308][3] Before the November 2011 observations,
   the uncertainties in the post-2029 trajectory showed that the asteroid
   would pass somewhere between 0.002 AU (300,000 km; 190,000 mi) and
   0.3 AU (45,000,000 km; 28,000,000 mi) of Earth in 2041.^[309][3] Radar
   astrometry in November 2011 clarified the Earth passage in 2041 and
   beyond.^[310][3] As a result of the November 2011 radar observations,
   it is now known that (308635) 2005 YU[55] will pass between 0.1017 AU
   (15,210,000 km; 9,450,000 mi) and 0.1020 AU of Earth on 12 November
   2041.^[311][15] Using the current uncertainty region [312]integrated
   until the future, the 2075 approach will be between 0.0013 AU
   (190,000 km; 120,000 mi) and 0.0021 AU (310,000 km;
   200,000 mi).^[313][15]
