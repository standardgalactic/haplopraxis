   "Baby Got Back" is a song written, co-produced and recorded by American
   rapper and songwriter [92]Sir Mix-a-Lot. Released in May 1992 by
   [93]Def American and [94]Reprise as the second single from his third
   album, [95]Mack Daddy (1992), the song samples the 1986 [96]Detroit
   techno single "Technicolor" by [97]Channel One. At the time of its
   original release, the song caused controversy because of its outspoken
   and blatantly sexual lyrics objectifying women, as well as specific
   references to the [98]buttocks, which some people found objectionable.
   The song's accompanying music video was briefly banned by
   [99]MTV.^[100][1] Mix-a-Lot defended the song as being empowering to
   curvaceous women who were being shown skinny models as an ideal for
   beauty.

   "Baby Got Back" topped the US [101]Billboard Hot 100, and spent five
   weeks atop the chart. It was the second best-selling song in the US in
   1992. The song was ranked number 17 on [102]VH1's "100 Greatest Songs
   of Hip Hop" in 2008.^[103][2] In October 2023, [104]Billboard ranked it
   among the "500 Best Pop Songs of All Time".^[105][3]

Synopsis

   [[106]edit]

   In the song's prelude there is a conversation between two (presumably)
   thin, white [107]valley girls, similar to girl talk in [108]Frank
   Zappa's "[109]Valley Girl". One girl (dubbed Linda by Amylia
   Dorsey)^[110][4] remarks to her friend, "Oh, my, God Becky, look at her
   butt! It is so big... She's just so black!", at which point Sir
   Mix-a-Lot begins rapping.

   The first verse begins with "I like big butts and I cannot lie" and
   most of the song is about the rapper's attraction to women with large
   buttocks. The second and third verse challenge mainstream norms of
   beauty: "I ain't talkin' 'bout [111]Playboy. Cause silicone parts are
   made for toys." and "So [112]Cosmo says you're fat. Well I ain't down
   with that!"

   The song came from a meeting between Sir Mix-a-Lot and Amylia Dorsey,
   who saw little representation of full-figured women in media. The idea
   came from a 1980s-era Budweiser commercial^[113][5] featuring thin,
   valley girl-esque models of varying ethnicities. They decided to
   dedicate a song to the opposite, featuring curvy women of color. Mix
   and Dorsey sought to "broaden the definition of beauty."^[114][6]

   Sir Mix-a-Lot commented in a 1992 interview: "The song doesn't just say
   I like large butts, you know? The song is talking about women who damn
   near kill themselves to try to look like these beanpole models that you
   see in [115]Vogue magazine." He explains that most women respond
   positively to the song's message, especially black women: "They all
   say, 'About time.'"^[116][7]

   The dialogue of actress [117]Papillon Soo Soo saying "Me so horny" is
   sampled from the 1987 film [118]Full Metal Jacket to complete Sir
   Mix-a-Lot's lyric, "That butt you got makes..."

   In 2014, according to TMZ, Sir Mix-a-Lot says it was [119]Jennifer
   Lopez's moves as a Fly Girl on the 90s show [120]In Living Color that
   first inspired him to write "Baby Got Back".^[121][8]

Critical reception

   [[122]edit]

   [123]Larry Flick from [124]Billboard wrote, "First offering from
   rapper's major-label debut, [125]Mack Daddy, cheekily rhapsodizes about
   the joys of women with prominent backsides. Cute rhymes and slammin'
   beats add up to a potential smash at several formats."^[126][9]

   [127]J.D. Considine from [128]The Baltimore Sun commented, "In some
   cases, what's said can be as simple as Sir Mix-a-Lot's assertion 'I
   like big butts!' in the single 'Baby Got Back'. On the surface, it may
   seem that all he's doing is expressing an opinion, but there's more to
   it than Mix-a-Lot's personal preferences. At root, 'Baby Got Back'
   challenges the dominant standard for physical beauty in our culture, a
   standard that stresses long legs, slim hips, small buttocks and has no
   room for women with wide hips or protuberant posteriors. And the fact
   that 'Baby Got Back' spent five weeks at No. 1 suggests that there are
   millions who agree with his assessment."^[129][10]

   James Bernard from [130]Entertainment Weekly remarked that the song
   "alternates deftly between a critique of the [131]Cosmo/[132]Playboy
   narrow-minded – and narrow-hipped – standard of female beauty and a
   bawdy appreciation of, er, generous rear ends."^[133][11]

   In [134]Melody Maker's review of the album, "Baby Got Back" was named
   "worst of all" and "a hip hop 'Fatty Bum Bum' and – Warning! Warning! –
   could be a novelty hit."^[135][12]

   Mark Coleman from [136]Rolling Stone said the song "celebrates a
   section of the anatomy long revered by rappers ("beggin' for a piece of
   that bubble" is a new twist)."^[137][13]

   In 2020, [138]Cleveland.com ranked "Baby Got Back" number 24 in their
   list of the best [139]Billboard [140]Hot 100 No. 1 song of the 1990s.
   They described it as "the novelty song that never went away", adding,
   "You could put this on at a wedding today and women will recite the
   opening word for word before the rap breaks in and everyone (and I mean
   everyone) joins in. Sir Mix-a-Lot was never shy about playing up the
   song's "playful" nature, rapping on top of a giant butt in the
   video."^[141][14]

   The song has been cited in demonstrating the limitations of the
   [142]Bechdel test.^[[143]clarification needed] It has been described as
   passing the test because it begins with a [144]valley girl saying to
   another "Oh my god, Becky, look at her
   butt!".^[145][15]^[146][16]^[147][17]

Chart performance and awards

   [[148]edit]

   "Baby Got Back" reached number one on the US Billboard Hot 100 chart
   for five weeks in the summer of 1992, and won a [149]1993 [150]Grammy
   Award for Best Rap Solo Performance. In the years following the song's
   release on the album [151]Mack Daddy, it has continued to appear in
   many movies, television shows, and commercials, as detailed below. It
   was ranked number six on [152]VH1's "Greatest Songs of the '90s", and
   number one on VH1's "Greatest One Hit Wonders of the '90s". In October
   2023, [153]Billboard magazine ranked it number 327 in their "500 Best
   Pop Songs of All Time".^[154][3] Same year, [155]Time Out ranked "Baby
   Got Back" number 23 in their "The 100 Best Party Songs Ever
   Made".^[156][18]

In popular culture

   [[207]edit]

   In 2019, former governor of Alaska and Republican vice-presidential
   candidate [208]Sarah Palin performed the song on [209]Fox's [210]The
   Masked Singer while dressed as a bear.^[211][40]^[212][41]

Jonathan Coulton/Glee cover

   [[213]edit]

   [214]Jonathan Coulton released a cover of "Baby Got Back" during his
   [215]Thing a Week project in October 2005, with the song being released
   as part of the first Thing A Week compilation album the next
   year.^[216][42] Coulton performs the song on acoustic guitar with a
   gentle and tender melody at odds with the lascivious nature of the
   lyrics.

   Coulton's arrangement of the song was used in a 2013 [217]episode of
   the musical television series [218]Glee without Coulton's knowledge or
   permission.^[219][43] Coulton publicly lambasted the show's producers,
   and alleged that they had even lifted elements from his
   recording,^[220][44] but claimed that Fox responded that "they're
   within their legal rights to do this, and that [Coulton] should be
   happy for the exposure", though Coulton pointed out that he was not
   credited within the episode.^[221][44] Coulton released his cover of
   "Baby Got Back" to iTunes, labelling it "a cover of Glee's cover of my
   cover of Sir Mix-a-Lot's song", with proceeds going to
   charity.^[222][45] Coulton pursued legal charges against the show's
   producers, but ultimately dropped them, deciding a seeming victory in
   the court of public opinion was "a good enough win." The controversy
   was referenced in a season 5 episode of [223]The Good Wife entitled
   [224]"Goliath and David".^[225][46]

Related songs

   [[226]edit]

   In a 2000 interview, Sir Mix-a-Lot reflected, "There's always butt
   songs. Hell, I got the idea sitting up here listening to old
   [227]Parliament records: [228]Motor Booty Affair. Black men like butts.
   That's the bottom line."^[229][47] The song is part of a tradition of
   1970s–90s African-American music celebrating the female posterior,
   including "[230]Da Butt", "[231]Rump Shaker", and "[232]Shake Your
   Groove Thing".^[233][48]

   In 2014, Trinidadian-American rapper [234]Nicki Minaj sampled "Baby Got
   Back", including some of its verses, for her hit "[235]Anaconda", from
   the album [236]The Pinkprint.^[237][49] The song has been viewed by
   some as a [238]diss track, in [239]answer to "Baby Got Back". Whereas
   Sir Mix-a-Lot focuses on a woman's body and the pleasure it gives him,
   Minaj raps from the perspective of the unnamed woman, and shows how she
   uses her [240]callipygian physique to profit and empower
   herself.^[241][50]
