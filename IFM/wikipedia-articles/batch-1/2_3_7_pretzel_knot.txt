   In [69]geometric topology, a branch of [70]mathematics, the (−2, 3, 7)
   pretzel knot, sometimes called the Fintushel–Stern knot (after [71]Ron
   Fintushel and [72]Ronald J. Stern), is an important example of a
   [73]pretzel knot which exhibits various interesting phenomena under
   three-dimensional and four-dimensional [74]surgery constructions.

Mathematical properties

   [[75]edit]

   The (−2, 3, 7) pretzel knot has 7 exceptional slopes, [76]Dehn surgery
   slopes which give non-[77]hyperbolic 3-manifolds. Among the enumerated
   knots, the only other hyperbolic knot with 7 or more is the
   [78]figure-eight knot, which has 10. All other hyperbolic knots are
   conjectured to have at most 6 exceptional slopes.
   [79][220px-PretzelKnot.jpg] A [80]pretzel (−2,3,7) pretzel knot.
