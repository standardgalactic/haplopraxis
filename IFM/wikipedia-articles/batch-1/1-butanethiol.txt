   Chemical compound

   Butane-1-thiol, also known as butyl mercaptan, is a [131]volatile,
   clear to yellowish [132]liquid with a fetid (extremely foul-smelling)
   odor, commonly described as "[133]skunk" odor. In fact, 1-butanethiol
   is structurally similar to several major constituents of a skunk's
   defensive spray but is not actually present in the spray.^[134][4] The
   scent of 1-butanethiol is so strong that the human nose can easily
   detect it in the air at concentrations as low as 10 parts per billion.
   The threshold level for 1-butanethiol is reported as 1.4 ppb^[135][5]

Chemistry

   [[136]edit]

   Butane-1-thiol is chemically classified among the [137]thiols, which
   are [138]organic compounds with [139]molecular formulas and
   [140]structural formulas similar to [141]alcohols, except that the
   [142]sulfur-containing [143]sulfhydryl group (-SH) replaces the
   [144]oxygen-containing [145]hydroxyl group (-OH) in the molecule.
   1-Butanethiol's basic molecular formula is C[4]H[9]SH, and its
   structural formula is similar to that of the alcohol [146]n-butanol.
   1-Butanethiol is prepared by the free radical catalyzed addition of
   hydrogen sulfide to 1-butene. Commercially, this is performed using
   ultraviolet light. 1-Butanethiol is a thiol of low [147]molecular
   weight, and it is highly [148]flammable.

Uses

   [[149]edit]

   Butane-1-thiol is used as an industrial solvent,^[[150]citation needed]
   and as an intermediate for cotton defoliants.^[151][6] It is sometimes
   placed in "[152]stink bombs" and "stink perfumes" for pranksters.

Safety

   [[153]edit]

   Butane-1-thiol is a very noxious and caustic chemical compound, and at
   sufficiently high concentrations, it produces serious health effects in
   both humans and animals, especially as a result of prolonged exposure.
   Higher concentrations can lead to unconsciousness and coma after
   prolonged exposure. Contact with the skin and mucous membranes causes
   burns, and contact with the eyes can lead to blurred vision or complete
   blindness.^[[154]citation needed]

   Inhalation may cause weakness, confusion, cough, dizziness, drowsiness,
   headache, nausea, vomiting, and shortness of breath. The substance
   irritates the eyes, the skin, and the respiratory tract. It may cause
   effects on the thyroid and the nervous system and could cause lowering
   of consciousness.^[155][7]
