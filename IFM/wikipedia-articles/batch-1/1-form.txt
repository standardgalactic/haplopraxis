   Differential form of degree one or section of a cotangent bundle
   "One-form" redirects here. Not to be confused with [69]One-form (linear
   algebra).

   In [70]differential geometry, a one-form (or covector field) on a
   [71]differentiable manifold is a [72]differential form of degree one,
   that is, a [73]smooth [74]section of the [75]cotangent bundle.^[76][1]
   Equivalently, a one-form on a manifold

   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle M} is a smooth mapping of the [77]total space of the
   [78]tangent bundle of
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>M</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle M}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle M} to
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mi

   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \mathbb {R}
   }</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} } whose restriction to each fibre is a
   linear functional on the tangent space.^[79][2] Symbolically,

   coordinates. In a local coordinate system, a one-form is a linear
   combination of the [82]differentials of the coordinates:

Examples

   [[85]edit]

   The most basic non-trivial differential one-form is the "change in
   angle" form
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>d</mi> <mi>θ</mi> <mo>.</mo>
   </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle d\theta .}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle d\theta .} This is defined as the derivative of the
   angle "function"
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>θ</mi> <mo
   stretchy="false">(</mo> <mi>x</mi> <mo>,</mo> <mi>y</mi> <mo
   stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
   encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \theta (x,y)}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \theta (x,y)} (which is only defined up to an additive
   constant), which can be explicitly defined in terms of the [86]atan2
   function. Taking the derivative yields the following formula for the
   [87]total derivative:
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD">

   </mrow> <mi>d</mi> <mi>x</mi> <mo>+</mo> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD">

   {y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}}dx+{\frac
   {x}{x^{2}+y^{2}}}dy\end{aligned}}}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}d\theta &=\partial
   _{x}\left(\operatorname {atan2} (y,x)\right)dx+\partial

   angle "function" cannot be continuously defined – the function atan2 is
   discontinuous along the negative
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle

   the origin, reflecting the fact that infinitesimal (and indeed local)
   changes in angle can be defined everywhere except the origin.
   Integrating this derivative along a path gives the total change in
   angle over the path, and integrating over a closed loop gives the
   [88]winding number times
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle

   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle 2\pi .}

   In the language of [89]differential geometry, this derivative is a
   one-form on the [90]punctured plane. It is [91]closed (its [92]exterior
   derivative is zero) but not [93]exact, meaning that it is not the
   derivative of a 0-form (that is, a function): the angle
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>θ</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \theta
   }</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \theta } is not a globally defined smooth function on
   the entire punctured plane. In fact, this form generates the first
   [94]de Rham cohomology of the punctured plane. This is the most basic
   example of such a form, and it is fundamental in differential geometry.
