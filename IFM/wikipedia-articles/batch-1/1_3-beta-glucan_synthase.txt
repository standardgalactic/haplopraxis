   1,3-Beta-glucan synthase is a [104]glucosyltransferase [105]enzyme
   involved in the generation of [106]beta-glucan in [107]fungi. It serves
   as a pharmacological target for [108]antifungal drugs such as
   [109]caspofungin, [110]anidulafungin, and [111]micafungin, deemed
   1,3-Beta-glucan synthase inhibitors. Under the [112]CAZy classification
   system, fungi and plant members fall in the glycosyltransferase 48
   family (GT48).^[113][2] Some members of the [114]glycosyltransferase 2
   family ([115]Pfam [116]PF13632), such as the [117]curdlan synthase CrdS
   ([118]Q9X2V0), also has a similar activity.^[119][3]

   The [120]biosynthesis of disaccharides, [121]oligosaccharides, and
   [122]polysaccharides involves the action of hundreds of different
   [123]glycosyltransferases. These [124]enzymes [125]catalyse the
   transfer of [126]sugar [127]moieties from activated donor
   [128]molecules to specific acceptor molecules, forming [129]glycosidic
   bonds.

   The family consists of various 1,3-beta-glucan [130]synthase components
   including Gls1, Gls2, and Gls3 from [131]yeast. 1,3-Beta-glucan
   [132]synthase ([133]EC [134]2.4.1.34.) also known as callose synthase
   [135]catalyses the formation of a beta-1,3-glucan [136]polymer that is
   a major component of the [137]fungal [138]cell wall.^[139][4] The
   reaction catalysed is:

          UDP-glucose + {(1,3)-beta-D-glucosyl}(N) = UDP +
          {(1,3)-beta-D-glucosyl}(N+1).
