   1,000,000 (one million), or one [205]thousand thousand, is the
   [206]natural number following [207]999,999 and preceding 1,000,001. The
   word is derived from the early Italian millione (milione in modern
   Italian), from mille, "thousand", plus the [208]augmentative suffix
   -one.^[209][1]

   It is commonly abbreviated:
     * in British English as m^[210][2]^[211][3]^[212][4] (not to be
       confused with the [213]metric prefix "m" [214]milli, for 10^−3, or
       with [215]metre),
     * M,^[216][5]^[217][6]
     * MM ("thousand thousands", from Latin "Mille"; not to be confused
       with the [218]Roman numeral

   MM = 2,000),

     mm (not to be confused with [219]millimetre), or

     mn, mln, or mio can be found in financial contexts.^[220][7]^[221][8]

   In [222]scientific notation, it is written as 1×10^6 or 10^6.^[223][9]
   [224]Physical quantities can also be expressed using the [225]SI prefix
   [226]mega (M), when dealing with [227]SI units; for example, 1
   [228]megawatt (1 MW) equals 1,000,000 [229]watts.

   The meaning of the word "million" is common to the [230]short scale and
   long scale numbering systems, unlike the larger numbers, which have
   different names in the two systems.

   The million is sometimes used in the English language as a
   [231]metaphor for a very large number, as in "Not in a million years"
   and "You're one in a million", or a [232]hyperbole, as in "I've walked
   a million miles" and "You've asked a million-dollar question".

   1,000,000 is also the [233]square of [234]1000 and also the [235]cube
   of [236]100.

Visualizing one million

   [[238]edit]

   Even though it is often stressed that [239]counting to precisely a
   million would be an exceedingly tedious task due to the time and
   concentration required, there are many ways to bring the number "down
   to size" in approximate quantities, ignoring irregularities or packing
   effects.
     * Information: Not counting spaces, the text printed on 136 pages of
       an [240]Encyclopædia Britannica, or 600 pages of [241]pulp
       paperback fiction contains approximately one million characters.
     * Length: There are one million [242]millimetres in a [243]kilometre,
       and roughly a million sixteenths of an [244]inch in a [245]mile (1
       sixteenth = 0.0625). A typical [246]car [247]tire might rotate a
       million times in a 1,900-kilometre (1,200 mi) trip, while the
       engine would do several times that number of [248]revolutions.
     * Fingers: If the [249]width of a human finger is 22 mm (7⁄8 in),
       then a million fingers lined up would cover a distance of 22 km
       (14 mi). If a person walks at a speed of 4 km/h (2.5 mph), it would
       take them approximately five and a half hours to reach the end of
       the fingers.
     * Area: A square a thousand objects or units on a side contains a
       million such objects or square units, so a million holes might be
       found in less than three square yards of window screen, or
       similarly, in about one half square foot (400–500 cm^2) of bed
       sheet cloth. A city lot 70 by 100 feet is about a million square
       inches.
     * Volume: The cube root of one million is one hundred, so a million
       objects or cubic units is contained in a cube a hundred objects or
       linear units on a side. A million grains of [250]table salt or
       granulated [251]sugar occupies about 64 mL (2.3 imp fl oz;
       2.2 US fl oz), the volume of a cube one hundred grains on a side.
       One million cubic inches would be the volume of a small room

   8+1⁄3 feet long by 8+1⁄3 feet wide by 8+1⁄3 feet high.

     Mass: A million cubic millimetres (small droplets) of water would
   have a volume of one [252]litre and a mass of one [253]kilogram. A
   million millilitres or [254]cubic centimetres (one [255]cubic metre) of
   water has a mass of a million [256]grams or one [257]tonne.

     Weight: A million 80-milligram (1.2 gr) [258]honey bees would weigh
   the same as an 80 kg (180 lb) person.

     Landscape: A [259]pyramidal hill 600 feet (180 m) wide at the base
   and 100 feet (30 m) high would weigh about a million short tons.

     Computer: A [260]display resolution of 1,280 by 800 [261]pixels
   contains 1,024,000 pixels.

     Money: A [262]U.S. dollar bill of any denomination weighs 1 gram
   (0.035 oz). There are 454 grams in a pound. One million dollar bills
   would weigh 1 [263]megagram (1,000 kg; 2,200 lb) or 1 tonne (just over
   1 [264]short ton).

     Time: A million [265]seconds, 1 megasecond, is 11.57 [266]days.

   In [267]Indian English and [268]Pakistani English, it is also expressed
   as 10 [269]lakh. Lakh is derived from lakṣa for 100,000 in
   [270]Sanskrit.

   – each tile with white or grey background contains 1000 dots [272](full
   image)

Prime numbers

   [[462]edit]

   There are 78,498 primes less than 10^6, where 999,983 is the largest
   prime number smaller than 1,000,000.

   Increments of 10^6 from 1 million through a 10 million have the
   following prime counts:
     * 70,435 primes between 1,000,000 and 2,000,000.
     * 67,883 primes between 2,000,000 and 3,000,000.
     * 66,330 primes between 3,000,000 and 4,000,000.
     * 65,367 primes between 4,000,000 and 5,000,000.
     * 64,336 primes between 5,000,000 and 6,000,000.
     * 63,799 primes between 6,000,000 and 7,000,000.
     * 63,129 primes between 7,000,000 and 8,000,000.
     * 62,712 primes between 8,000,000 and 9,000,000.
     * 62,090 primes between 9,000,000 and 10,000,000.

   In total, there are 586,081 prime numbers between 1,000,000 and
   10,000,000.^[463][65]
