   10,000 (ten thousand) is the [167]natural number following [168]9,999
   and preceding 10,001.

Name

   [[169]edit]

   See also: [170]Orders of magnitude (numbers)

   Many languages have a specific word for this number: in [171]Ancient
   Greek it is μύριοι (the etymological root of the word [172]myriad in
   [173]English), in [174]Aramaic ܪܒܘܬܐ, in [175]Hebrew רבבה [revava], in
   [176]Chinese 萬/万 (Mandarin wàn, [177]Cantonese maan6, Hokkien bān), in
   [178]Japanese 万/萬 [man], in [179]Khmer ម៉ឺន [meun], in [180]Korean 만/萬
   [man], in [181]Russian тьма [t'ma], in [182]Vietnamese vạn, in
   [183]Sanskrit अयुत [ayuta], in [184]Thai หมื่น [meun], in
   [185]Malayalam പതിനായിരം [patinayiram], and in [186]Malagasy
   alina.^[187][1] In many of these languages, it often denotes a
   [188]very large but indefinite number.^[189][2]

   The classical [190]Greeks used letters of the [191]Greek alphabet to
   represent [192]Greek numerals: they used a capital letter [193]mu (Μ)
   to represent ten thousand.^[[194]citation needed] This Greek root was
   used in early versions of the [195]metric system in the form of the
   decimal prefix [196]myria-.^[197][3]

   Depending on the country, the number ten thousand is usually written as
   10,000 (including in the UK and US), 10.000, or 10 000.^[198][4]

In mathematics

   [[199]edit]

   In [200]scientific notation it is written as 10^4 or 1 E+4
   (equivalently 1 E4) in [201]E notation.

   It is the [202]square of [203]100 and the [204]square root of
   [205]100,000,000.

   The value of a [206]myriad to the [207]power of itself, 10000^10000 =
   10^40000.

   It has a total of 25 [208]divisors, whose [209]geometric mean is a
   [210]whole number, 100 (the number of primes below this value is
   25).^[211][5]

   It has a [212]reduced totient of [213]500, and a [214]totient of
   [215]4,000, with a total of 16 [216]integers having a totient value of
   10,000.^[217][6]^[218][7]

   There are a total of [219]1,229 prime numbers less than ten thousand, a
   count that is itself prime.^[220][5]^[221][8]

   A [222]myriagon is a [223]polygon with ten thousand edges and a total
   of 25 [224]dihedral symmetry groups when including the myriagon itself,
   alongside 25 [225]cyclic groups as [226]subgroups.^[227][9]
