   Chemical compound

   (Z)-Stilbene is a [122]diarylethene, that is, a [123]hydrocarbon
   consisting of a cis [124]ethene double bond substituted with a
   [125]phenyl group on both carbon atoms of the double bond. The name
   stilbene was derived from the [126]Greek word stilbos, which means
   shining.

Isomers

   [[127]edit]

   Stilbene exists as two possible isomers known as [128](E)-stilbene and
   (Z)-stilbene. (Z)-Stilbene is [129]sterically hindered and less stable
   because the steric interactions force the aromatic rings 43°
   out-of-plane and prevent [130]conjugation.^[131][2] (Z)-Stilbene has a
   melting point of 5–6 °C (41–43 °F), while (E)-stilbene melts around
   125 °C (257 °F), illustrating that the two compounds are quite
   different.

Uses

   [[132]edit]

     * Stilbene is used in manufacture of [133]dyes and [134]optical
       brighteners, and also as a [135]phosphor and a [136]scintillator.
     * Stilbene is one of the [137]gain mediums used in [138]dye lasers.

Properties

   [[139]edit]

     * Stilbene will typically have the chemistry of a [140]diarylethene,
       a conjugated [141]alkene.
     * Stilbene can undergo [142]photoisomerization under the influence of
       UV light.
     * Stilbene can undergo [143]stilbene photocyclization, an
       [144]intramolecular reaction.
     * (Z)-Stilbene can undergo [145]electrocyclic reactions.

Natural occurrence

   [[146]edit]

   Many stilbene derivatives ([147]stilbenoids) are present naturally in
   plants. An example is [148]resveratrol and its cousin,
   [149]pterostilbene.
