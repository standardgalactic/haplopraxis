   Asir,^[147][a]^[148][b] officially the Asir Province,^[149][c] is a
   [150]province in the southwest of [151]Saudi Arabia, which is named
   after the [152]ʿAsīr tribe. It has an area of 76,693 square kilometres
   (29,611 sq mi), and an estimated population of 2,024,285 (in
   2022).^[153][1] ʿAsīr is surrounded by [154]Mecca Province to the north
   and west, [155]Al-Bahah Province to the northwest, [156]Riyadh Province
   to the northeast, [157]Najran Province to the southeast, and [158]Jazan
   Province and the [159]Yemeni [160]Muhafazah ([161]Governorate) of
   [162]Saada to the south.

   [163]Abha is the provincial capital, and other towns include
   [164]Khamis Mushait, [165]Bisha and [166]Bareq. The regional governor
   is [167]Turki [168]ibn [169]Talal bin Abdulaziz Al Saud. He replaced
   his cousin, [170]Faisal bin Khalid bin Abdulaziz Al Saud, after being
   appointed on 27 December 2018.^[171][2]

Etymology

   [[172]edit]

   The origin of the name "Asir" remains a subject of debate among
   historians. One theory, proposed by [173]Al-Masudi in his work [174]The
   Meadows of Gold, suggests that the region was originally known as the
   "land of Azd".^[175][3]

   Another popular hypothesis suggests that the name "Asir" derives from
   the Arabic term ʿUsrah ([176]Arabic: عُسرة, [177]lit. 'Hardship'). This
   theory comes from the fact that the region's terrain is rugged, which
   can be challenging to navigate.^[178][4]

   A third perspective proposed by [179]Fuad Hamza in his book Fi bilad
   ʿAseer, links the name to the historical prominence of [180]Banu
   Asir.^[181][5] As the tribe gained power and influence, its name came
   to encompass the surrounding territories and tribes.^[182][5] This
   association was reinforced by the close ties between the Asir tribe and
   many of the ruling emirs in the region, including those from the Al
   Yazid, Al Muthami, and Al A'ad families.^[183][5]

History

   [[184]edit]

Ancient history

   [[185]edit]

   In 25 B.C., [186]Aelius Gallus marched his legions south from
   [187]Egypt on an expedition of 1,300 miles (2,100 kilometres), to take
   control of the ancient overland trade routes between the
   [188]Mediterranean Sea and what is now [189]Hadhramaut in [190]Yemen.
   The [191]Romans wanted control of those routes because they were
   desperate for money and hoped to raise some by capturing [192]Ma'rib,
   capital of [193]Sabaʾ, and taking control of the trade in [194]incense
   – then a priceless commodity – and other valuable aromatics. As it
   turned out, however, the expedition was a disaster and little
   information about ʿAsir emerged.^[195][6]

Modern history

   [[196]edit]
   See also: [197]Idrisid Emirate of Asir and [198]Saudi–Yemeni war (1934)

   By 1920, however, [199]Ibn Saud, the founder of the modern Saudi state,
   had begun to recoup the losses of the [200]House of Saud, and to unify
   most of the [201]peninsula under his rule. As part of this campaign, he
   sent his [202]Bedouin warriors also known as the [203]Ikhwan to occupy
   ʿAsir, and the ruler of the region, Hasan Al Idrissi, had to
   leave.^[204][7] Therefore, he asked for protection from [205]Imam
   Yahya, the ruler of Yemen and went there.^[206][7] From then on ʿAsir
   has been controlled by the House of Saud, a situation formalized in
   1934 with the signing of the [207]Treaty of Taʾif between Saudi Arabia
   and Yemen. Even then the region was still largely unknown to the West.
   In 1932, [208]St John Philby, one of the first Europeans to explore and
   map the peninsula, did enter ʿAsir, but as he did not publish his
   observations until 1952, the area remained one of the blank spots on
   the world's map.^[209][8] In 1935 ʿAsir was made a separate
   governorate.^[210][9]

Mass shootings of Ethiopian refugees

   [[211]edit]

   Since 2014 refugees are killed after crossing the Saudi border by the
   [212]Saudi Border Guard. Since 2023 at least several hundred Ethiopian
   refugees have been killed at the border between Yemen and the provinces
   [213]Jazan, Asir and [214]Najran. The Saudis have also used
   [215]explosive weapons.^[216][10]^[217][11]

Economy

   [[218]edit]

   Historically, ʿAsir was known for producing [219]coffee, [220]wheat,
   [221]alfalfa, [222]barley, [223]senna, and
   [224]frankincense.^[225][12]^[226][13]^[227][14] Wheat was grown in the
   summer and [228]sesame has been grown in wetter areas of the
   region.^[229][13]^[230][15] [231]Straw was used to make mats, hats, and
   [232]baskets. Tribes in the area also wove tents from straw.^[233][16]

Modern development projects

   [[234]edit]

   In 2019, the Saudi government launched an infrastructure development
   project in ʿAsir Region. The project is expected to cost more than 1
   billion Saudi Riyals. The provided projects will include health care,
   transportation and municipal services.^[235][17] The project is in line
   with the [236]Saudi Vision 2030 to diversify non-petroleum income and
   activate new resources in Saudi Arabia.^[237][17]

   In 2023, [238]Crown Prince [239]Mohammed bin Salman launched
   [240]Soudah Peaks Project to develop a tourism destination in Saudi
   Arabia's highest peak, [241]Jabal Soudah.^[242][18] Soudah Peaks is a
   planned luxury mountain tourism destination located at 3,015 meters
   above sea level.

Geography

   [[243]edit]
   See also: [244]Asir Mountains

   The ʿAsir Region is situated on a high plateau that receives more
   rainfall than the rest of the country and contains the country's
   highest [245]peaks, which rise to almost 3,000 metres (9,800 ft) at
   [246]Jabal Sawda near Abha. Though data is exceedingly sparse and
   unreliable, the average annual rainfall in the highlands probably
   ranges from 300–500 millimetres (12–20 in). It falls in two rainy
   seasons, the chief one being in March and April, with some rain in the
   summer. Temperatures are very extreme, with diurnal temperature ranges
   in the highlands the greatest in the world. It is
   common^[[247]clarification needed] for afternoon temperatures to be
   over 30 °C (86 °F), yet mornings can be extremely frosty and [248]fog
   can cut visibility to near zero percent. As a result, there is much
   more natural vegetation in ʿAsir than in any other part of Saudi
   Arabia, with sheltered areas even containing areas of dense coniferous
   forests, though more exposed ridges still are very dry. ʿAsir is home
   to many farmers who chiefly grow wheat and fruit crops. Irrigation has
   greatly expanded production in modern times. [249]Asir National Park
   was established in 1981, and extends from the Red Sea coast through the
   western foothills to the Asir escarpment.^[250][19]
