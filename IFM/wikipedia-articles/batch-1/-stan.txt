   Persian-language suffix used for place names
   For other uses, see [107]Stan (disambiguation).

   [108][229px-Stansuffixmap.png]
     Countries whose name in English has the suffix -stan
     Other countries that have first-level divisions whose names in
   English have the suffix -stan.
     Countries whose name in their native language has the suffix -stan.

   stan ([109]Persian: ستان stân,^[110][n 1] estân or istân^[111][n 2])
   has the meaning of "a place abounding in"^[112][1] or "a place where
   anything abounds" as a [113]suffix.^[114][2] It is widely used by
   [115]Iranian languages as well as the [116]common Turkish languages
   (excluding [117]Siberian Turkic) and other languages. The suffix
   appears in the names of many regions throughout [118]West, [119]Central
   and [120]South Asia, and parts of the [121]Caucasus and
   [122]Russia.^[123][3]

Etymology and cognates

   [[124]edit]
   See also: [125]Oikonyms in Western and South Asia
   This article may require [126]copy editing for grammar, style,
   cohesion, tone, or spelling. You can assist by [127]editing it. (May
   2024) ([128]Learn how and when to remove this message)

   The suffix -stan is analogous to the suffix [129]-land, present in many
   country and location names. The suffix is also used more generally, as
   in Persian rigestân (ریگستان), "place of sand, desert"; golestân
   (گلستان), "place of flowers, garden"; gurestân (گورستان), "graveyard,
   cemetery";^[130][4] and [131]Hendostân (هندوستان), "Land of the
   [132]Indus ([133]India)".^[134][5]

   Originally an independent noun, this morpheme evolved into a suffix by
   virtue of appearing frequently as the last part in [135]nominal
   compounds. It is of [136]Indo-Iranian and ultimately [137]Indo-European
   origin. It is [138]cognate with the English word [139]state, Polish
   [140]stan (estate, or [141]New World first-level subdivisions), and
   with [142]Sanskrit sthā́na ([143]Devanagari: [144]Sanskrit: स्थान
   [145][stʰaːnɐ]), meaning "the act of standing", from which many further
   meanings derive, including "place, location; abode, dwelling". This
   meaning ultimately descends from [146]Proto-Indo-Iranian
   *sthāna-,^[147][6] partly loaned into [148]Ancient Greek as
   [149]Ancient Greek: -στήνη (-stēnē).

Countries

   [[150]edit]
   This article should specify the language of its non-English content,
   using {{[151]lang}}, {{[152]transliteration}} for transliterated
   languages, and {{[153]IPA}} for phonetic transcriptions, with an
   appropriate [154]ISO 639 code. Wikipedia's [155]multilingual support
   templates may also be used. [156]See why. (January 2024)

   Countries adopting the -stan suffix in both English and these
   countries' national languages include:
     *  [157]Afghanistan - افغانستان (Afghānistān)
     *  [158]Kazakhstan - Қазақстан (Qazaqstan)
     *  [159]Kyrgyzstan - Кыргызстан (Kyrgyzstan)
     *  [160]Pakistan - پاکستان (Pākistān)
     *  [161]Tajikistan - Тоҷикистон (Tojikiston)
     *  [162]Turkmenistan - Türkmenistan
     *  [163]Uzbekistan - Ўзбекистон (Oʻzbekiston)

   Some of these nations were also known with the Latinate suffix [164]-ia
   during their time as [165]Soviet republics: Turkmenistan was frequently
   Turkmenia, Kyrgyzstan often Kirghizia, and even Uzbekistan and
   Tajikistan were very occasionally Uzbekia and
   Tajikia.^[166][7]^[167][8] In addition, the native name of [168]Armenia
   is Hayastan, hay being the [169]endonym of Armenians. The largest -stan
   country by area is [170]Kazakhstan.

Historical regions

   [[380]edit]

     * [381]Frangistan – a historical term used (by Muslims and Persians
       in particular) to refer to Western or Christian Europe.
     * [382]Funjistan (Funj) - a historical empire ubicated in modern-day
       Sudan
     * [383]Kabulistan – a historical name of the territory centered
       around present-day [384]Kabul Province of Afghanistan.
     * [385]Lazistan – a historical and cultural region of the Caucasus
       and Anatolia, traditionally inhabited by the [386]Laz people.
     * [387]Moghulistan (Mughalistan) – a historical area in Central Asia
       that included parts of modern-day Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and
       [388]Xinjiang
     * [389]Tabaristan – a historical mountainous region located on the
       Caspian coast of [390]Northern Iran.
     * [391]Zabulistan – a historical region roughly corresponding to
       today's [392]Zabul Province in southern Afghanistan.

Other

   [[498]edit]

     * [499]Absurdistan – sometimes used to satirically describe a country
       where everything goes wrong
     * [500]Angyalistan – a [501]micronation that claims garbage patches
       in [502]international waters
     * Autistan – the "metaphorical country" of the autistic people
     * Bailoutistan (or Bailoutistan 2.0) - sarcastic term for Greece
       following the European Union bail out packages, coined by Yanis
       Varoufakis in his book 'Adults in the Room: My Battle With the
       European and American Deep Establishment'
     * [503]Bimaristan – a kind of hospital in medieval Persia and the
       medieval Islamic world
     * [504]Bradistan – a moniker for [505]Bradford, England, owing to its
       large population of Pakistani worker migrants
     * [506]Canuckistan (full name being The People's Republic of Soviet
       Canuckistan) – [507]epithet for Canada, used by [508]Pat Buchanan
       on 31 October 2002, on his television show on [509]MSNBC in which
       he denounced Canadians as anti-American and the country as a haven
       for terrorists. He was reacting to Canadian criticisms of US
       security measures regarding [510]Arab Canadians^[511][15]
     * Cavaquistan (Cavaquistão in [512]Portuguese) – a name coined after
       the former [513]Portuguese President and Prime-Minister [514]Aníbal
       Cavaco Silva, referring to the regions of Portugal where he
       achieved landslide victories in the elections held in the late
       1980s and early 1990s (especially in the [515]Viseu
       District^[516][16]); intended pun with Kazakhstan (Cazaquistão in
       Portuguese^[517][17])
     * [518]Dalitstan.org – a [519]Dalit advocacy website active until
       mid-2006,^[520][18] one of 18 websites that were blocked by the
       Indian government to check for hate messages following the
       [521]2006 Mumbai train bombings.^[522][19]
     * Extremistan and Mediocristan – used by author [523]Nassim Nicholas
       Taleb to illustrate concepts of [524]black swan theory in [525]The
       Black Swan: The Impact of the Highly Improbable
     * [526]Filmistan – a film-production company
     * [527]Fondukistan or Fondoqestān – an early medieval settlement and
       Buddhist monastery in Afghanistan
     * [528]Gazimestan – name of a monument commemorating the historical
       [529]Battle of Kosovo
     * Hookturnistan – satirical name of [530]Melbourne, Victoria, due to
       the large number of [531]hook turns on city roads
     * [532]Hamastan – a concept of a Palestinian Islamic government with
       [533]Sharia as law
     * [534]Iranistan – a pseudo-orientalist mansion built for P. T.
       Barnum in 1848 in Connecticut
     * [535]Islamistan – means 'Land of Islam', used in various contexts
     * [536]Londonistan – French counter-terrorism agents gave the
       British/English capital of [537]London this [538]sobriquet.
       Sometimes used derogatorily to refer to the large immigrant,
       especially Muslim, population in London.
     * [539]Muristan – a complex of streets and shops in the Christian
       Quarter of the Old City of Jerusalem
     * [540]New Yorkistan – the title of the cover art for the 10 December
       2001, edition of [541]The New Yorker magazine
          + The New Yorkistan map itself included various districts ending
            in -stan, e.g., Bronxistan, Cold Turkeystan,
            Fuhgeddabouditstan, Gaymenistan, Taxistan,
            Youdontunderstandistan, etc.
     * Orbánistan – a derogatory term for [542]Hungary under the rule of
       [543]Viktor Orbán^[544][20]
     * [545]Paganistan – the pagan/neo-pagan community of
       [546]Minneapolis-Saint Paul in [547]Minnesota^[548][21]
     * [549]Quebecistan – a term coined by [550]Barbara Kay in 2006 in
       reference to [551]Quebec, [552]Canada.
     * [553]Sarvestan – a Sasanian-era palace in the Iranian province of
       Sarvestan
     * [554]Shabestan – an underground space, usually found in the
       traditional architecture of mosques, houses, and schools in ancient
       Persia
     * [555]Shahrestan (several meanings)
     * [556]Skateistan – a skateboarding/educational organization based in
       Kabul, Afghanistan
     * Swedistan – a derogatory term for [557]Sweden due to its growing
       immigration from Islamic countries. It is sometimes used by
       [558]internet users to attack or offend Swedish Muslims.
     * Talibanistan – a name for the government of [559]Afghanistan under
       the [560]Taliban
     * [561]Tristan da Cunha - Not etymologically a '-stan', often
       shortened as 'Tristan'
     * [562]Zaqistan – a [563]micronation currently in [564]Nevada
