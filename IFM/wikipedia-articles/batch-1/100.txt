   100 or one hundred ([241]Roman numeral: C)^[242][1] is the [243]natural
   number following [244]99 and preceding [245]101.

In mathematics

   [[246]edit]
   [247][Cube-sum-100.png] 100 as the sum of the first positive cubes

   100 is the square of [248]10 (in [249]scientific notation it is written
   as 10^2). The standard [250]SI prefix for a hundred is "[251]hecto-".

   100 is the basis of [252]percentages (per centum meaning "by the
   hundred" in Latin), with 100% being a full amount.

   100 is a [253]Harshad number in [254]decimal, and also in base-four, a
   base in-which it is also a [255]self-descriptive
   number.^[256][2]^[257][3]

   100 is the sum of the first nine [258]prime numbers, from [259]2
   through [260]23.^[261][4] It is also divisible by the number of primes
   below it, [262]25.^[263][5]

   100 cannot be expressed as the difference between any integer and the
   total of [264]coprimes below it, making it a
   [265]noncototient.^[266][6]

   100 has a [267]reduced totient of 20, and an [268]Euler totient of
   40.^[269][7]^[270][8] A totient value of 100 is obtained from four
   numbers: [271]101, [272]125, [273]202, and [274]250.

   100 can be expressed as a sum of some of its divisors, making it a
   [275]semiperfect number.^[276][9] The [277]geometric mean of its nine
   divisors is [278]10.

   100 is the sum of the [279]cubes of the first four positive
   [280]integers (100 = 1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + 4^3).^[281][10] This is related
   by [282]Nicomachus's theorem to the fact that 100 also equals the
   square of the sum of the first four positive integers: 100 = 10^2 = (1
   + 2 + 3 + 4)^2.^[283][11]

   100 = 2^6 + 6^2, thus 100 is the seventh [284]Leyland number.^[285][12]
   100 is also the seventeenth [286]Erdős–Woods number, and the fourth
   18-[287]gonal number.^[288][13]^[289][14]

   The 100th prime number is [290]541, which returns
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mn>0</mn> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle 0}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle 0} for the [291]Mertens function.^[292][15] It is the
   10th [293]star number^[294][16] (whose [295]digit sum also adds to 10
   in [296]decimal).

   There are exactly 100 prime numbers in base-ten whose digits are in
   strictly ascending order (e.g. 239, 2357, etc.).^[297][17] The last
   such prime number is 23456789, which contains eight consecutive
   [298]integers as digits.

In science

   [[299]edit]

   One hundred is the atomic number of [300]fermium, an [301]actinide, and
   the last of the [302]heavy metals that can be created through neutron
   bombardment.

   On the [303]Celsius scale, 100 degrees is the boiling temperature of
   pure [304]water at [305]sea level.

   The [306]Kármán line lies at an altitude of 100 kilometres (62 mi)
   above the Earth's sea level and is commonly used to define the boundary
   between Earth's atmosphere and outer space.

In history

   [[307]edit]
     * In medieval contexts, it may be described as the short hundred or
       five [308]score in order to differentiate the [309]English and
       [310]Germanic use of "hundred" to describe the [311]long
       hundred^[312][18] of six [313]score or [314]120.
     * The [315]Hundred Years' War, a significant conflict in the middle
       ages.

In religion

   [[316]edit]
     * There are 100 blasts of the [317]Shofar heard in the service of
       [318]Rosh Hashana, the Jewish New Year.^[319][19]
     * A religious Jew is expected to utter at least 100 blessings
       daily.^[320][20]
     * In the [321]Hindu epic of the [322]Mahabharata, King
       [323]Dhritarashtra of Kuru had 100 sons, known as the
       [324]Kauravas.

In politics

   [[325]edit]
     * The [326]Hundred (county division) is a largely historical division
       of a county or similar larger administrative unit.
     * The [327]United States Senate has 100 [328]Senators.

In money

   [[329]edit]
   [330][220px-Hundred_rupee_note_India.jpg] Hundred rupee note India

   Most of the world's [331]currencies are divided into 100 subunits; for
   example, one [332]euro is one hundred cents and one [333]pound sterling
   is one hundred pence.

   By specification, [334]100 euro notes feature a picture of a Rococo
   gateway on the obverse and a Baroque bridge on the reverse.
   [335][250px-Obverse_of_the_series_2009_%24100_Federal_Reserve_Note.jpg]
   The [336]U.S. hundred-dollar bill, Series 2009

   The [337]U.S. hundred-dollar bill has [338]Benjamin Franklin's
   portrait; the "Benjamin" is the largest U.S. bill in print. American
   savings bonds of $100 have [339]Thomas Jefferson's portrait, while
   American $100 treasury bonds have [340]Andrew Jackson's portrait.

In sports

   [[341]edit]
     * The number of yards in an [342]American football field (not
       including the end zones).^[343][21]
     * The number of points required for a [344]snooker player to score a
       [345]century break, a significant milestone.
     * The record number of points scored in one [346]NBA [347]game by a
       single player, set by [348]Wilt Chamberlain of the
       [349]Philadelphia Warriors on March 2, 1962.^[350][22]
     * The [351]100-yard dash and the [352]100-metre dash are sprint track
       events.
     * The [353]100-kilometre walk is a racewalking event.
     * [354]The Hundred (cricket), a 100-ball cricket competition.

In other fields

   [[355]edit]

   One hundred is also:
     * The number of years in a [356]century.
     * The number of [357]centimeters in a [358]meter.
     * The number of pounds in an American short [359]hundredweight.
     * In [360]Greece, [361]India, [362]Israel and [363]Nepal, 100 is the
       police telephone number.
     * In [364]Belgium, 100 is the ambulance and firefighter telephone
       number.
     * In [365]United Kingdom, 100 is the [366]operator telephone number.
     * The [367]HTTP status code indicating that the client should
       continue with its request.
     * The age at which a person becomes a [368]centenarian.
