   From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
   (Redirected from [65](G,X)-structure)

   In [66]geometry, if X is a manifold with an action of a [67]topological
   group G by analytical diffeomorphisms, the notion of a (G, X)-structure
   on a [68]topological space is a way to formalise it being locally
   isomorphic to X with its G-invariant structure; spaces with a (G,
   X)-structure are always [69]manifolds and are called (G, X)-manifolds.
   This notion is often used with G being a [70]Lie group and X a
   [71]homogeneous space for G. Foundational examples are [72]hyperbolic
   manifolds and [73]affine manifolds.

Riemannian examples

   [[84]edit]

Complete (G,X)-structures

   [[111]edit]

   A
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mo stretchy="false">(</mo>
   <mi>G</mi> <mo>,</mo> <mi>X</mi> <mo stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle>
   </mrow> <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   (G,X)}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle (G,X)} structure is said to be complete if it has a
   developing map which is also a [112]covering map (this does not depend
   on the choice of developing map since they differ by a diffeomorphism).
   For example, if
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>X</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle X}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle X} is simply connected the structure is complete if and
   only if the developing map is a diffeomorphism.

Examples

   [[113]edit]

Riemannian (G,X)-structures

   [[114]edit]

   If
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>X</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle X}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle X} is a Riemannian manifold and
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>G</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle G}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle G} its full group of isometry, then a
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mo stretchy="false">(</mo>
   <mi>G</mi> <mo>,</mo> <mi>X</mi> <mo stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle>
   </mrow> <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   (G,X)}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle (G,X)} -structure is complete if and only if the
   underlying Riemannian manifold is [115]geodesically complete
   (equivalently metrically complete). In particular, in this case if the
   underlying space of a
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mo stretchy="false">(</mo>
   <mi>G</mi> <mo>,</mo> <mi>X</mi> <mo stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle>
   </mrow> <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   (G,X)}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle (G,X)} -manifold is compact then the latter is
   automatically complete.

   In the case where
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>X</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle X}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle X} is the hyperbolic plane the developing map is the
   same map as given by the [116]Uniformisation Theorem.

Other cases

   [[117]edit]

   In general compactness of the space does not imply completeness of a
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mo stretchy="false">(</mo>
   <mi>G</mi> <mo>,</mo> <mi>X</mi> <mo stretchy="false">)</mo> </mstyle>
   </mrow> <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   (G,X)}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle (G,X)} -structure. For example, an affine structure on
   the torus is complete if and only if the monodromy map has its image
   inside the [118]translations. But there are many affine tori which do
   not satisfy this condition, for example any quadrilateral with its
   opposite sides glued by an affine map yields an affine structure on the
   torus, which is complete if and only if the quadrilateral is a
   parallelogram.

   Interesting examples of complete, noncompact affine manifolds are given
   by the Margulis spacetimes.
