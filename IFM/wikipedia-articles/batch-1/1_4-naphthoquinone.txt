   Chemical compound

   1,4-Naphthoquinone or para-naphthoquinone is a [109]quinone derived
   from [110]naphthalene. It forms volatile yellow triclinic crystals and
   has a sharp odor similar to [111]benzoquinone. It is almost insoluble
   in cold water, slightly soluble in [112]petroleum ether, and more
   soluble in polar organic solvents. In [113]alkaline solutions it
   produces a reddish-brown color. Vitamin K is a derivative of
   1,4-naphthoquinone. It is a planar molecule with one aromatic ring
   fused to a quinone subunit.^[114][2] It is an [115]isomer of
   [116]1,2-naphthoquinone.

Preparation

   [[117]edit]

   The industrial synthesis involves aerobic oxidation of naphthalene over
   a vanadium oxide catalyst:^[118][3]

          C[10]H[8] + 3/2 O[2] → C[10]H[6]O[2] + H[2]O

   In the laboratory, naphthoquinone can be produced by the oxidation of a
   variety of naphthalene compounds. An inexpensive route involves
   oxidation of naphthalene with [119]chromium trioxide.^[120][4]

Reactions

   [[121]edit]

   1,4-Naphthoquinone acts as strong [122]dienophile in [123]Diels-Alder
   reaction. Its adduct with [124]1,3-butadiene can be prepared by two
   methods: 1) long (45 days) exposure of naphthoquinone in neat liquid
   butadiene taken in huge excess at room temperature in a thick-wall
   glass tube or 2) fast catalyzed [125]cycloaddition at low temperature
   in the presence of 1 equivalent of [126]tin(IV) chloride:^[127][5]
   [128]Diels-Alder reaction of 1,4-naphthoquinone with 1,3-butadiene
   Diels-Alder reaction of 1,4-naphthoquinone with 1,3-butadiene

Uses

   [[129]edit]

   1,4-Naphthoquinone is mainly used as a precursor to [130]anthraquinone
   by reaction with butadiene followed by oxidation. Nitration gives
   5-nitro-1,4-naphthalenedione, precursor to an aminoanthroquinone that
   is used as a dye precursor.^[131][3]

Derivatives

   [[132]edit]

   Naphthoquinone forms the central chemical structure of many natural
   compounds, most notably the [133]K vitamins.
   2-Methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone, called [134]menadione, is a more effective
   coagulant than vitamin K.

   Other natural naphthoquinones include [135]juglone, [136]plumbagin,
   [137]droserone.

   Naphthoquinone [138]derivatives have significant pharmacological
   properties. They are [139]cytotoxic, they have significant
   [140]antibacterial, [141]antifungal, [142]antiviral, [143]insecticidal,
   [144]anti-inflammatory, and [145]antipyretic properties. Plants with
   naphthoquinone content are widely used in China and the countries of
   South America, where they are used to treat malignant and parasitic
   diseases.^[146][6]

   Naphthoquinone functions as a ligand through its electrophilic
   carbon-carbon double bonds.^[147][7]

   [148]Dichlone, a chlorinated derivative of 1,4-naphthoquinone, is used
   as a fungicide.
