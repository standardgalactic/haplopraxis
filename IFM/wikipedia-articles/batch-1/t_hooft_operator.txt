   (Redirected from [55]'t Hooft operator)
   Magnetic loop operator dual to the Wilson loop

   In [56]quantum field theory, the 't Hooft loop is a magnetic analogue
   of the [57]Wilson loop for which spatial loops give rise to thin loops
   of [58]magnetic flux associated with [59]magnetic vortices. They play
   the role of a [60]disorder parameter for the [61]Higgs phase in pure
   [62]gauge theory. Consistency conditions between [63]electric and
   magnetic charges limit the possible 't Hooft loops that can be used,
   similarly to the way that the [64]Dirac quantization condition limits
   the set of allowed [65]magnetic monopoles. They were first introduced
   by [66]Gerard 't Hooft in 1978 in the context of possible [67]phases
   that gauge theories admit.^[68][1]

Disorder operator

   [[90]edit]

   The 't Hooft loop is a disorder operator since it creates singularities
   in the gauge field, with their [91]expectation value distinguishing the
   disordered phase of pure Yang–Mills theory from the ordered
   [92]confining phase. Similarly to the Wilson loop, the expectation
   value of the 't Hooft loop can follow either the area law^[93][5]

   they play an important role in the center vortex scenario for
   confinement.^[101][6] In this model, it is these vortices that lead to
   the area law of the Wilson loop through the random fluctuations in the
   number of [102]topologically linked vortices.
