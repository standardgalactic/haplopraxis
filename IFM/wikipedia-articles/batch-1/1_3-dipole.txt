   From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
   Dipolar compound with electron delocalization and charge separation
   over 3 atoms
   [59][100px-Azide-2D.svg.png]
   [60][120px-Nitrone.png]
   [61][100px-Nitro-group.svg.png]
   From top to bottom,
   azides, nitrones, and
   nitro compounds are
   examples of 1,3-dipoles.

   In [62]organic chemistry, a 1,3-dipolar compound or 1,3-dipole is a
   [63]dipolar compound with [64]delocalized electrons and a
   [65]separation of charge over three [66]atoms. They are reactants in
   [67]1,3-dipolar cycloadditions.^[68][1]^[69][2]

   The dipole has at least one [70]resonance structure with positive and
   negative charges having a 1,3 relationship which can generally be
   denoted as
   ^+a−b−c^−, where a may be a [71]carbon, [72]oxygen or [73]nitrogen, b
   may be nitrogen or oxygen, and c may be a carbon, oxygen or
   nitrogen.^[74][3] [75][402px-1%2C3-dipolar_compounds.png] A
   demonstration that how some well known 1,3-dipoles like ozone, nitro
   compounds and azides can be shown to have a resonance structure having
   1,3 relationship between positive and negative formal charges

   Known 1,3-dipoles are:
     * [76]Azides (

   RN[3])

     [77]Ozone (
   O[3])

     [78]Nitro compounds (
   RNO[2])

     [79]Diazo compounds (R[2]CN[2])

     Some [80]oxides
     * [81]Azoxide compounds (RN(O)NR)
     * [82]Carbonyl oxides ([83]Criegee zwitterions)^[84][4]^[85][5]
     * [86]Nitrile oxides (

   RCN−O)

     [87]Nitrous oxide (N[2]O)

     [88]Nitrones (R[2]CN(R)O)
   [89][220px-Carbonyl_oxide-resonance.svg.png] Carbonyl oxide
     * Some [90]imines:
          + [91]Azomethine imine
          + [92]Nitrilimines (
       RCN−NR, analogous to nitrile oxide)
     * [93]Carbonyl imines

     Some [94]ylides
     * [95]Azomethine ylide
     * [96]Nitrile ylide (

   RCNCR'[2])

     [97]Carbonyl ylide

     [98]Thiosulfines (R[2]CSS)
