   In [92]enzymology, an aminobutyraldehyde dehydrogenase ([93]EC
   [94]1.2.1.19) is an [95]enzyme that [96]catalyzes the [97]chemical
   reaction

          4-aminobutanal + NAD^+ + H[2]O
          [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
          displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mo class="MJX-variant"
          stretchy="false">⇌</mo> </mstyle> </mrow> <annotation
          encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \rightleftharpoons
          }</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
          {\displaystyle \rightleftharpoons } 4-aminobutanoate + NADH + 2
          H^+

   The 3 [98]substrates of this enzyme are [99]4-aminobutanal, [100]NAD^+,
   and [101]H[2]O, whereas its 3 [102]products are [103]4-aminobutanoate,
   [104]NADH, and [105]H^+.

   This enzyme belongs to the family of [106]oxidoreductases, specifically
   those acting on the aldehyde or oxo group of donor with NAD+ or NADP+
   as acceptor. The [107]systematic name of this enzyme class is
   4-aminobutanal:NAD+ 1-oxidoreductase. Other names in common use include
   gamma-guanidinobutyraldehyde dehydrogenase (ambiguous), ABAL
   dehydrogenase, 4-aminobutyraldehyde dehydrogenase, 4-aminobutanal
   dehydrogenase, gamma-aminobutyraldehyde dehydrogenase, 1-pyrroline
   dehydrogenase, ABALDH, and YdcW. This enzyme participates in the
   [108]urea cycle and the metabolism of [109]amino groups and
   [110]beta-alanine.
