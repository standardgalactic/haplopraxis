   Chemical compound

   10-Methacryloyloxydecyl dihydrogen phosphate (10-MDP, MDP Monomer) is a
   chemical compound used in dental adhesive materials. This
   [79]organophosphate [80]monomer was developed in 1981 by the Japanese
   company [81]Kuraray for the preparation of dental adhesion [82]polymers
   ^[83][1]

Synthesis

   [[84]edit]

   MDP is synthesized according to the following reactions: at first,
   10-hydroxydecyl [85]methacrylate is synthesized by reaction of
   [86]methacrylic acid and [87]1,10-decanediol [[88]es]. Next,
   [89]phosphoryl chloride is added to 10-hydroxydecy methacrylate, then,
   the phosphorus-chlorine bonds in this intermediate are
   [90]hydrolyzed.^[91][2]

   [92][500px-MDP_syn.png]

Background

   [[93]edit]

   In the late 1970s, tooth adhesion phosphate monomer
   2-methacryloyloxethyl phenyl hydrogen phosphate (Phenyl-P) was
   developed for tooth-saving restoration techniques.^[94][3]
   4-Methacryloyloxyethyl trimellitic acid anhydride (4-META) that adheres
   to not only tooth structures but also dental alloys, was developed
   almost at the same time.^[95][4] In order to create adhesive monomers
   having higher performance, investigation and optimization of adhesive
   monomer molecular structure was carried out. The results of the
   experiments have provided adhesive monomers with a more suitable
   chemical structure, and one of those is MDP.^[96][5]

Research

   [[97]edit]

MDP-apatite or dentin interactions

   [[98]edit]

   The adhesive interaction of MDP with synthetic [99]hydroxyapatite was
   observed using [100]x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy and [101]atomic
   absorption spectrophotometry. MDP readily adhered to
   [102]hydroxyapatite and this bond appeared very stable, as confirmed by
   the low dissolution rate of its calcium salt in water.^[103][6]

   MDP, which effectively interacts chemically with [104]hydroxyapatite
   and the calcium salt of which is hardly soluble, showed no signs of
   degradation in bond strength. Micro-tensile bond strength (μTBS) to
   dentin of a self-etch adhesive that contains MDP were measured up to
   100,000 thermocycles. The μTBS of the MDP-based adhesive to dentin
   after 100,000 thermocycles was not significantly different from that of
   the control.^[105][7]

   Experimental primers, which were prepared by three different purity
   grade MDP monomers, were tested. Impurities and the presence of MDP
   dimer affected not only hybridization, but also reduced the formation
   of MDP_Ca salts and nano-layering. MDP in a high purity grade is
   essential to achieve durable bonding.^[106][8]

MDP-collagen interactions

   [[107]edit]

   The binding interaction between [108]collagen and MDP was studied by
   saturation transfer difference (STD) [109]NMR spectroscopy. The STD
   results imply that MDP has a relatively stable interaction with the
   [110]collagen, because of the [111]hydrophobic interactions between the
   [112]hydrophobic MDP moieties and the [113]hydrophobic [114]collagen
   surface.^[115][9]

Adhesion to dental metal

   [[116]edit]

   Tensile bond strengths to [117]titanium plates treated with 3
   experimental primers consisting of MDP in 3 concentrations were tested.
   The data obtained strongly suggest that MDP is effective to improve the
   adhesive performance of resin to [118]titanium.^[119][10]

Adhesion to zirconia

   [[120]edit]

   Tensile bond strength to [121]zirconia of [122]ethanol solutions that
   contains MDP were measured. MDP showed high bond strengths to
   [123]zirconia.^[124][11]^[125][12]

   Tensile bond strengths of MDP containing [126]resin composites to
   [127]zirconia ceramic were statistically significantly higher when
   compared with the bond strength of the conventional [128]Bis-GMA
   [129]resin composite which contains no adhesive monomer.^[130][13]

   The mechanisms of coordination between MDP and zirconium oxide were
   demonstrated by using 1H and 31P magic angle spinning nuclear magnetic
   resonance ([131]NMR) and two dimensional 1H → 31P heteronuclear
   correlation [132]NMR. The spectra indicated three possible models as
   mechanisms of interaction of MDP with zirconia.^[133][14]
