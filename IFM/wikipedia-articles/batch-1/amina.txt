   Amina bint Wahb ibn Abd Manaf al-Zuhriyya ([145]Arabic: آمِنَة بِنْت
   وَهْب, [146]romanized: ʾĀmina bint Wahb, c. 549–577) was the mother of
   the Islamic prophet [147]Muhammad.^[148][1] She belonged to the
   [149]Banu Zuhra tribe.

Early life and marriage

   [[150]edit]

   Aminah was born to [151]Wahb ibn Abd Manaf and [152]Barrah bint 'Abd
   al-'Uzzā ibn 'Uthmān ibn 'Abd al-Dār in [153]Mecca. Her tribe, Quraysh,
   were said to be descendants of [154]Ibrahim ([155]Abraham) through his
   son [156]Isma'il (Ishmael). Her ancestor Zuhrah was the elder brother
   of [157]Qusayy ibn Kilab, an ancestor of [158]Abdullah ibn
   Abdul-Muttalib, and the first Qurayshi custodian of the [159]Kaaba.
   [160]Abd al-Muttalib proposed the marriage of Abdullah, his youngest
   son, to Aminah. Some sources state that Aminah's father accepted the
   match; others say that it was Aminah's uncle, Wuhaib, who was serving
   as her guardian.^[161][2]^[162][3] The two were married soon
   after.^[163][3] Abdullah spent much of Aminah's pregnancy away from
   home as part of a merchant caravan and died of disease before the birth
   of his son in Medina.^[164][3]^[165][4]

Birth of Muhammad and later years

   [[166]edit]
   See also: [167]Mawlid and [168]Muhammad in Mecca
   [169][220px-Perpustakaan_Makkah_al-Mukarramah.jpg] [170]Makkah Al
   Mukarramah Library ([171]21°25′30″N 39°49′48″E﻿ / ﻿21.42500°N
   39.83000°E﻿ / 21.42500; 39.83000﻿ (Bayt al-Mawlid / Makkah Al
   Mukarramah Library)) is believed to stand on the spot where Aminah gave
   [172]birth to Muhammad, so it is also known as [173]Bayt al-Mawlid.

   Three months after Abdullah's death, in 570–571 CE, Muhammad was born.
   As was tradition among all the great families at the time, Aminah sent
   Muhammad to live with a [174]milk mother in the [175]desert as a baby.
   The belief was that in the desert, one would learn self-discipline,
   nobility, and freedom. During this time, Muhammad was nursed by
   [176]Halimah bint Abi Dhuayb, a poor [177]Bedouin woman from the tribe
   of [178]Banu Sa'ad, a branch of the [179]Hawāzin.^[180][5]

   When Muhammad was six years old, he was reunited with Aminah, who took
   him to visit her relatives in Yathrib (later [181]Medina). Upon their
   return to Mecca a month later, accompanied by her slave, [182]Umm
   Ayman, Aminah fell ill. She died around the year 577 or
   578,^[183][6]^[184][7] and was buried in the village of [185]Al-Abwa'.
   Her grave was destroyed in 1998.^[186][8]^[187][9] The young Muhammad
   was taken in first by his paternal grandfather, Abd al-Muttalib, in
   577, and later by his paternal uncle [188]Abu Talib ibn Abd
   al-Muttalib.^[189][3]

Religious belief

   [[190]edit]
   [191][220px-%D8%B6%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AD_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%
   A9_%D8%A2%D9%85%D9%86%D8%A9_%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%87%D8%A7_%D8%A7%D9%84
   %D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-2.JPG] The alleged grave of Aminah bint Wahb
   in [192]Al-Abwa'. It was [193]destroyed in 1998.

   Islam rebukes the idea that lineage plays a part in one's [194]final
   destination, as each person is responsible for themselves and their
   deeds. This is proven through multiple authentic hadiths that mention
   that both Muhammad's mother and father are in Hellfire:

     "Verily, a person said: Messenger of Allah, where is my father? He
     said: (He) is in the Fire. When he turned away, he (the Holy
     Prophet) called him and said: Verily my father and your father are
     in the Fire."

   — [195]Sahih Muslim, [196]1:406

   Additionally, another hadith states:

     "The Apostle of Allah (ﷺ) visited the grave of his mother and he
     wept, and moved others around him to tears, and said: I sought
     permission from my Lord to beg forgiveness for her but it was not
     granted to me, and I sought permission to visit her grave and it was
     granted to me so visit the graves, for that makes you mindful of
     death."

   — Sahih Muslim, [197]11:135

   Despite this, Islamic scholars have long been divided over the
   religious beliefs of Muhammad's parents and their fate in the
   [198]afterlife.^[199][10] One transmission by [200]Abu Dawud and
   [201]Ibn Majah states that [202]God refused to forgive Aminah for her
   [203]kufr (disbelief). Another transmission in [204]Musnad al-Bazzar
   states that Muhammad's parents were brought back to life and accepted
   Islam before returning to the [205]Barzakh.^[206][11]^: 11  Some
   [207]Ash'ari and [208]Shafi'i scholars argued that neither would be
   punished in the afterlife as they were [209]Ahl al-fatrah, or "people
   of the interval" between the prophetic messages of [210]'Isa
   ([211]Jesus) and [212]Muhammad.^[213][12] The concept of Ahl al-fatrah
   is not universally accepted among Islamic scholars, and there is debate
   concerning the extent of salvation available for active practitioners
   of [214]Shirk ([215]Polytheism).^[216][13] The majority of scholars
   have come to agree with it and disregard the [217]ahadith stating that
   Muhammad's parents were condemned to [218]Hell.^[219][10]

   While a work attributed to [220]Abu Hanifa, an early [221]Sunni
   scholar, stated that both Aminah and Abdullah died condemned to Hell
   (Mata 'ala al-fitrah),^[222][14] some later authors of [223]mawlid
   texts related a tradition in which Aminah and Abdullah were temporarily
   [224]revived and embraced Islam. Scholars such as [225]Ibn Taymiyya
   stated that this was a lie (though [226]Al-Qurtubi stated that the
   concept did not disagree with Islamic theology).^[227][12] According to
   [228]Ali al-Qari, the preferred view is that both the parents of
   Muhammad were Muslims.^[229][11]^: 28  According to [230]Al-Suyuti,
   [231]Ismail Hakki Bursevi, and other Islamic scholars, all of the
   ahadith indicating that the parents of Muhammad were not forgiven were
   later abrogated when they were brought to life and accepted
   Islam.^[232][11]^: 24  [233]Shia Muslims believe that all of Muhammad's
   ancestors—Aminah included—were [234]monotheists and therefore entitled
   to [235]Paradise. A Shia tradition states that God forbade the fires of
   Hell from touching either of Muhammad's parents.^[236][15]
