   Chemical compound

   1,2-Bis(diphenylphosphino)ethane (dppe) is an [111]organophosphorus
   compound with the formula (Ph[2]PCH[2])[2] (Ph = [112]phenyl). It is a
   common symmetrical [113]bidentate ligand in [114]coordination
   chemistry. It is a white solid that is soluble in organic solvents.

Preparation

   [[115]edit]

   The preparation of dppe entails the [116]alkylation of
   [117]NaP(C[6]H[5])[2] with 1,2-dichloroethane:^[118][1]^[119][2]

          2 NaP(C[6]H[5])[2] + ClCH[2]CH[2]Cl →
          (C[6]H[5])[2]PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])[2] + 2 NaCl

Reactions

   [[120]edit]

   The reduction of dppe by [121]lithium give the disecondary
   phosphine:^[122][3]

   (C[6]H[5])[2]PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])[2] 4 Li →
   Li(C[6]H[5])PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])Li + 2 C[6]H[5]Li

   Hydrolysis gives the bis(secondary phosphine).

   Li(C[6]H[5])PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])Li + 2 H[2]O →
   H(C[6]H[5])PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])H + 2 LiOH
   [123][220px-HFeCl_dppe_2.svg.png] The bis(dppe) complex
   [124]HFeCl(dppe)[2] is one of the most accessible transition metal
   hydrides.

   Treatment of dppe with [125]hydrogen peroxide produces the
   [126]phosphine oxides
   (C[6]H[5])[2]P(O)CH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])[2] and
   (C[6]H[5])[2]P(O)CH[2]CH[2]P(O)(C[6]H[5])[2].^[127][4] Selective
   mono-oxidation of dppe can be achieved by benzylation followed by
   hydrolysis:

   (C[6]H[5])[2]PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])[2] + C[6]H[5]CH[2]Br →
   (C[6]H[5])[2]PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])[2](CH[2]C[6]H[5])]Br
   (C[6]H[5])[2]PCH[2]CH[2]P(C[6]H[5])[2](CH[2]C[6]H[5])]Br + NaOH →
   (C[6]H[5])[2]PCH[2]CH[2]P(O)(C[6]H[5])[2] + C[6]H[5]CH[3] + NaBr

   Hydrogenation of dppe gives the ligand
   [128]bis(dicyclohexylphosphino)ethane.

Coordination complexes

   [[129]edit]

   Many coordination complexes of dppe are known, and some are used as
   homogeneous catalysts. Dppe is almost invariably [130]chelating,
   although there are examples of monodentate (e.g., W(CO)[5](dppe)) and
   of bridging behavior.^[131][5] The natural [132]bite angle is
   86°.^[133][6]
