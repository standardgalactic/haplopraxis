   Chemical compound

   The [88]chemical [89]compound 1,2-dioxetanedione, or
   1,2-dioxacyclobutane-3,4-dione, often called peroxyacid ester, is an
   unstable [90]oxide of [91]carbon (an [92]oxocarbon) with formula
   C[2]O[4]. It can be viewed as a double [93]ketone of [94]1,2-dioxetane
   (1,2-dioxacyclobutane), or a cyclic [95]dimer of carbon
   dioxide.^[96][1]

   In ordinary conditions, it quickly decomposes to [97]carbon dioxide
   (CO[2]) even at 180 K (−93.1 °C), but can be detected by [98]mass
   spectrometry and other techniques.^[99][2]^[100][3]

   1,2-Dioxetanedione is an intermediate in the [101]chemoluminescent
   [102]reactions used in [103]glowsticks.^[104][4]^[105][5] The
   decomposition proceeds via a [106]paramagnetic [107]oxalate
   [108]biradical intermediate.^[109][6]

   It is an intermediate in a reaction (between [110]oxalyl chloride and
   [111]hydrogen peroxide in [112]ethyl acetate, accumulating in solution
   at room temperature up to a few [113]micromoles, provided that the
   activating [114]dye and all traces of metals and other [115]reducing
   agents are removed from the system, and the reactions are carried out
   in an inert atmosphere.^[116][7]
