   Chemical compound

   1-Propanol (also propan-1-ol, propanol, n-propyl alcohol) is a
   [201]primary alcohol with the formula
   CH[3]CH[2]CH[2]OH and sometimes [202]represented as PrOH or n-PrOH. It
   is a colourless liquid and an [203]isomer of [204]2-propanol.
   1-Propanol is used as a [205]solvent in the pharmaceutical industry,
   mainly for [206]resins and [207]cellulose esters, and, sometimes, as a
   [208]disinfecting agent.

Occurrence

   [[209]edit]

   [210]Fusel alcohols like 1-Propanol are grain fermentation byproducts,
   and therefore trace amounts of 1-Propanol are present in [211]many
   alcoholic beverages.

Chemical properties

   [[212]edit]

   [213][450px-Propanol_reactions.png] Some example reactions of
   1-propanol

   1-Propanol shows the normal reactions of a [214]primary alcohol. Thus
   it can be converted to [215]alkyl halides; for example red
   [216]phosphorus and [217]iodine produce [218]n-propyl iodide in 80%
   yield, while PCl[3]
   with catalytic ZnCl[2] gives [219]n-propyl chloride. Reaction with
   [220]acetic acid in the presence of an H[2]SO[4] catalyst under
   [221]Fischer esterification conditions gives [222]propyl acetate, while
   refluxing propanol overnight with formic acid alone can produce propyl
   formate in 65% yield. Oxidation of 1-propanol with Na[2]Cr[2]O[7] and
   H[2]SO[4] gives a 36% yield of [223]propionaldehyde, and therefore for
   this type of reaction higher yielding methods using [224]PCC or the
   [225]Swern oxidation are recommended. Oxidation with [226]chromic acid
   yields [227]propionic acid.

Preparation

   [[228]edit]

   1-Propanol is manufactured by catalytic [229]hydrogenation of
   [230]propionaldehyde. Propionaldehyde is produced via the [231]oxo
   process by [232]hydroformylation of [233]ethylene using [234]carbon
   monoxide and [235]hydrogen in the presence of a catalyst such as
   [236]cobalt octacarbonyl or a [237]rhodium complex.^[238][5]

   H[2]C=CH[2] + CO + H[2] → CH[3]CH[2]CH=O

   CH[3]CH[2]CH=O + H[2] → CH[3]CH[2]CH[2]OH

   A traditional laboratory preparation of 1-propanol involves treating
   [239]n-propyl iodide with moist Ag[2]O
   .

Safety

   [[240]edit]

   1-Propanol is thought to be similar to [241]ethanol in its effects on
   the human body, but 2 to 4 times more potent according to a study
   conducted on rabbits. Many toxicology studies find oral acute LD[50]
   ranging from 1.9 g/kg to 6.5 g/kg (compared to 7.06 g/kg for ethanol).
   It is metabolized into [242]propionic acid. Effects include
   [243]alcoholic intoxication and [244]high anion gap metabolic acidosis.
   As of 2011, one case of lethal poisoning was reported following oral
   ingestion of 500mL of 1-propanol.^[245][6] Due to a lack of long term
   data, the carcinogenicity of 1-propanol in humans is unknown.

1-Propanol as fuel

   [[246]edit]

   1-Propanol has a high [247]octane number and is suitable for use as
   [248]engine fuel. However, propanol is too expensive to use as a motor
   fuel. The [249]research octane number (RON) of propanol is 118, and the
   [250]anti-knock index (AKI) is 108.^[251][7]
