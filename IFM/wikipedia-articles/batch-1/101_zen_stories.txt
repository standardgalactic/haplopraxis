   101 Zen Stories is a 1919 compilation of [195]Zen [196]koans^[197][1]
   including 19th and early 20th century [198]anecdotes compiled by
   [199]Nyogen Senzaki,^[200][2] and a translation of
   [201]Shasekishū,^[202][1]^[203][3] written in the 13th century by
   [204]Japanese Zen master [205]Mujū (無住) (literally,
   "non-dweller").^[206][3] The book was reprinted by [207]Paul Reps as
   part of [208]Zen Flesh, Zen Bones.^[209][4]^[210][3] Well-known koans
   in the collection include A Cup of Tea (1), The Sound of One Hand (21),
   No Water, No Moon (29), and Everything is Best (31).
