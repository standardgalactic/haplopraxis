   Chemical compound

   1-Octene is an [114]organic compound with a formula CH[2]CHC[6]H[13].
   The [115]alkene is classified as a higher [116]olefin and
   [117]alpha-olefin, meaning that the [118]double bond is located at the
   alpha (primary) position, endowing this compound with higher reactivity
   and thus useful chemical properties. 1-Octene is one of the important
   [119]linear alpha olefins in industry. It is a colourless liquid.

Synthesis

   [[120]edit]

   In industry, 1-octene is commonly manufactured by two main routes:
   [121]oligomerization of [122]ethylene and by [123]Fischer–Tropsch
   synthesis followed by purification. Another route to 1-octene that has
   been used commercially on a small scale is [124]dehydration of
   [125]alcohols. Prior to the 1970s, 1-octene was also manufactured by
   thermal cracking of [126]waxes, whereas linear internal [127]octenes
   were also manufactured by [128]chlorination/[129]dehydrochlorination of
   [130]linear alkanes.

   There are five commercial processes that [131]oligomerize [132]ethylene
   to 1-octene. Four of these processes produce 1-octene as a part of a
   wide distribution of alpha-olefins. In typical circumstances,
   [133]1-hexene content of the entire distribution of alpha-olefins
   ranges from about 25% of the distribution in the [134]Ethyl
   ([135]Innovene) process to about 8% of distribution in some modes of
   the [136]Gulf (CP Chemicals) and [137]Idemitsu processes.

   The only commercial process to isolate 1-octene from a wide mixture of
   C[8] hydrocarbons is practiced by [138]Sasol, a [139]South African oil
   and gas and petrochemical company. For commercial purposes, Sasol
   employs [140]Fischer–Tropsch synthesis to make fuels from
   [141]synthesis gas derived from coal and recovers 1-octene from these
   fuel streams, where the initial 1-octene concentration in a narrow
   [142]distillation cut may be 60%, with the remainder being vinylidenes,
   linear and branched internal olefins, linear and branched paraffins,
   [143]alcohols, [144]aldehydes, [145]carboxylic acids, and [146]aromatic
   hydrocarbons.

   Another route to 1-octene involves butadiene [147]telomerization of
   butadiene. This technology was commercialized by Dow in a facility in
   [148]Tarragona. 1-Methoxy- 2,7-octadiene is an intermediate in this
   process.^[149][2]

   Yet another route converts 1-heptene to 1-octene plant based on a
   Fischer-Tropsch-derived C[7] olefin stream (Sasol, Secunda).

   Other 1-octene technologies exist based on selective tetramerisation of
   ethylene.^[150][3]

Applications

   [[151]edit]

   The main use of 1-octene is as a [152]comonomer in production of
   polyethylene. [153]High-density polyethylene (HDPE) and [154]linear
   low-density polyethylene (LLDPE) use approximately 2–4% and 8–10% of
   comonomers, respectively.

   Another significant use of 1-octene is for production of linear
   [155]aldehyde via oxo synthesis ([156]hydroformylation) to give the C9
   aldehyde ([157]nonanal). Oxidation of this aldehyde gives the
   short-chain [158]fatty acid [159]nonanoic acid. Hydrogenation of the
   same aldehyde gives the [160]fatty alcohol [161]1-nonanol, which is
   used as a [162]plasticizer.
