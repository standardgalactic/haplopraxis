   Generalization of a category

   In mathematics, more specifically [62]category theory, a quasi-category
   (also called quasicategory, weak Kan complex, inner Kan complex,
   infinity category, ∞-category, Boardman complex, quategory) is a
   generalization of the notion of a [63]category. The study of such
   generalizations is known as [64]higher category theory.

   Quasi-categories were introduced by [65]Boardman & Vogt (1973).
   [66]André Joyal has much advanced the study of quasi-categories showing
   that most of the usual basic [67]category theory and some of the
   advanced notions and theorems have their analogues for
   quasi-categories. An elaborate treatise of the theory of
   quasi-categories has been expounded by [68]Jacob Lurie ([69]2009).

   Quasi-categories are certain [70]simplicial sets. Like ordinary
   categories, they contain objects (the 0-simplices of the simplicial
   set) and morphisms between these objects (1-simplices). But unlike
   categories, the composition of two morphisms need not be uniquely
   defined. All the morphisms that can serve as composition of two given
   morphisms are related to each other by higher order invertible
   morphisms (2-simplices thought of as "homotopies"). These higher order
   morphisms can also be composed, but again the composition is
   well-defined only up to still higher order invertible morphisms, etc.

   The idea of higher category theory (at least, higher category theory
   when higher morphisms are invertible) is that, as opposed to the
   standard notion of a category, there should be a mapping space (rather
   than a mapping set) between two objects. This suggests that a higher
   category should simply be a [71]topologically enriched category. The
   model of quasi-categories is, however, better suited to applications
   than that of topologically enriched categories, though it has been
   proved by Lurie that the two have natural model structures that are
   [72]Quillen equivalent.

The homotopy category

   [[76]edit]

   Given a quasi-category C, one can associate to it an ordinary category
   hC, called the [77]homotopy category of C. The homotopy category has as
   objects the vertices of C. The morphisms are given by homotopy classes
   of edges between vertices. Composition is given using the horn filler
   condition for n = 2.

   For a general simplicial set there is a functor

Examples

   [[79]edit]
     * The [80]nerve of a category is a quasi-category with the extra
       property that the filling of any inner horn is unique. Conversely a
       quasi-category such that any inner horn has a unique filling is
       isomorphic to the nerve of some category. The homotopy category of
       the nerve of C is isomorphic to C.
     * Given a topological space X, one can define its [81]singular set
       S(X), also known as the fundamental ∞-groupoid of X. S(X) is a
       quasi-category in which every morphism is invertible. The homotopy
       category of S(X) is the [82]fundamental groupoid of X.
     * More general than the previous example, every [83]Kan complex is an
       example of a quasi-category. In a Kan complex all maps from all
       horns—not just inner ones—can be filled, which again has the
       consequence that all morphisms in a Kan complex are invertible. Kan
       complexes are thus analogues to groupoids - the nerve of a category
       is a Kan complex iff the category is a groupoid.

Variants

   [[84]edit]
     * An (∞, 1)-category is a not-necessarily-quasi-category ∞-category
       in which all n-morphisms for n > 1 are equivalences. There are
       several models of (∞, 1)-categories, including [85]Segal category,
       [86]simplicially enriched category, [87]topological category,
       [88]complete Segal space. A quasi-category is also an
       (∞, 1)-category.
     * Model structure There is a model structure on sSet-categories that
       presents the (∞,1)-category (∞,1)Cat.
     * Homotopy Kan extension The notion of homotopy Kan extension and
       hence in particular that of homotopy limit and homotopy colimit has
       a direct formulation in terms of Kan-complex-enriched categories.
       See homotopy Kan extension for more.
     * Presentation of (∞,1)-topos theory All of (∞,1)-topos theory can be
       modeled in terms of sSet-categories. (ToënVezzosi). There is a
       notion of sSet-site C that models the notion of (∞,1)-site and a
       model structure on sSet-enriched presheaves on sSet-sites that is a
       presentation for the ∞-stack (∞,1)-toposes on C.
