   From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
   (Redirected from [122]-phobia)
   List of fear of different things or objects
   "-phobia" redirects here. For the class of psychological disorders, see
   [123]Phobia.

   The [124]English suffixes -phobia, -phobic, -phobe (from [125]Greek
   φόβος phobos, "fear") occur in technical usage in [126]psychiatry to
   construct words that describe irrational, [127]abnormal, unwarranted,
   persistent, or disabling [128]fear as a mental disorder (e.g.
   [129]agoraphobia), in [130]chemistry to describe chemical aversions
   (e.g. [131]hydrophobic), in [132]biology to describe organisms that
   dislike certain conditions (e.g. [133]acidophobia), and in
   [134]medicine to describe hypersensitivity to a stimulus, usually
   sensory (e.g. [135]photophobia). In common usage, they also form words
   that describe dislike or hatred of a particular thing or subject (e.g.
   [136]homophobia). The suffix is [137]antonymic to [138]-phil-.

   For more information on the psychiatric side, including how psychiatry
   groups phobias such as agoraphobia, social phobia, or simple phobia,
   see [139]phobia. The following lists include words ending in -phobia,
   and include fears that have acquired names. In some cases, the naming
   of phobias has become a [140]word game, of notable example being a 1998
   humorous article published by [141]BBC News.^[142][1] In some cases, a
   word ending in -phobia may have an [143]antonym with the suffix
   [144]-phil-, e.g. [145]Germanophobe/[146]Germanophile.

   Many -phobia lists circulate on the Internet, with words collected from
   indiscriminate sources, often copying each other. Also, a number of
   psychiatric websites exist that at the first glance cover a huge number
   of phobias, but in fact use a standard text to fit any phobia and reuse
   it for all unusual phobias by merely changing the name. Sometimes it
   leads to bizarre results, such as suggestions to cure "prostitute
   phobia".^[147][2] Such practice is known as [148]content spamming and
   is used to attract [149]search engines.

   An article published in 1897 in [150]American Journal of Psychology
   noted "the absurd tendency to give Greek names to objects feared
   (which, as [151]Arndt says, would give us such terms as klopsophobia –
   fear of thieves, triakaidekaphobia [[152]sic] – fear of the number
   13....)".^[153][3]

   Specialists may prefer to avoid the suffix -phobia and use more
   descriptive terms such as [165]personality disorders, [166]anxiety
   disorders, and [167]avoidant personality disorder. Terms should
   strictly have a Greek prefix although many are irregularly formed with
   Latin or even English prefixes. Many use inaccurate or imprecise
   prefixes, such as aerophobia (fear of air) for fear of flying.

Ethnic/national/religious prejudices and discrimination

   The suffix -phobia is used to coin terms that denote a particular
   anti-ethnic or anti-demographic sentiment, such as
   [676]Americanophobia, [677]Europhobia, [678]Francophobia,
   [679]Hispanophobia, and [680]Indophobia. Often a synonym with the
   prefix "anti-" already exists (e.g. [681]Polonophobia vs.
   [682]anti-Polonism). Anti-religious sentiments are expressed in terms
   such as [683]Christianophobia and [684]Islamophobia.

-phobia in the natural sciences

   In the natural sciences, words with the suffix -phobia/-phobic
   generally describe a predisposition for avoidance and/or exclusion. For
   antonyms, see [780]here
   Phobia Condition
   [781]Acidophobia preference for non-acidic conditions
   [782]Heliophobia aversion to [783]sunlight
   [784]Hydrophobia the property of being repelled by [785]water
   [786]Lipophobicity the property of [787]fat rejection (sometimes also
   called lipophobia)
   [788]Oleophobicity the property of [789]oil rejection
   [790]Photophobia (biology) a negative [791]phototaxis or
   [792]phototropism response, or a tendency to stay out of the [793]light
   [794]Ultrahydrophobicity the property given to materials that are
   extremely difficult to get [795]wet
   [796]Thermophobia aversion to [797]heat

Jocular and fictional phobias

     * Aibohphobia – a humorous term for the fear of [798]palindromes,
       which is a palindrome itself. The term is a piece of [799]computer
       humor entered into the 1981 [800]The Devil's DP
       Dictionary.^[801][47]
     * Anatidaephobia – the fictional fear that one is being watched by a
       duck. The word comes from the name of the family [802]Anatidae, and
       was used in [803]Gary Larson's [804]The Far Side.^[805][48]
     * Anoraknophobia – a [806]portmanteau of "[807]anorak" and
       "[808]arachnophobia". It was used in the [809]Wallace and Gromit
       comic book Anoraknophobia. Also the title of an [810]album by
       [811]Marillion.
     * Arachibutyrophobia – fear of [812]peanut butter sticking to the
       roof of the mouth, from Latin [813]arachis "peanut" and
       [814]butyrum "butter".^[815][49] The word is used by [816]Charles
       M. Schulz in a 1982 installment of his [817]Peanuts comic
       strip,^[818][50] and by [819]Peter O'Donnell in his 1985
       [820]Modesty Blaise [821]adventure novel [822]Dead Man's Handle.
     * Charlophobia – the fictional fear of any person named Charlotte or
       Charlie, mentioned in the comedic book A Duck is Watching Me:
       Strange and Unusual Phobias (2014), by Bernie Hobbs. The phobia was
       created to mock [823]name bias, a form of discrimination studied by
       researchers at the [824]University of California, Berkeley and the
       [825]University of Chicago.^[826][51]
     * [827]Hippopotomonstrosesquipedaliophobia – fear of long
       words,^[828][52] from the root word [829]sesquipedalophobia
       combined with monstrum and [830]hippopotamus. This was mentioned on
       the first episode of [831]Brainiac Series Five as a [832]Tickle's
       Teaser.
     * Keanuphobia – fear of [833]Keanu Reeves, portrayed in the [834]Dean
       Koontz book, [835]False Memory, where a woman has an
       [836]irrational fear of Reeves and has to see her psychiatrist,
       Mark Ahriman, each week, unaware that she only has the fear in the
       first place because Ahriman implanted it via [837]hypnotic
       suggestion to amuse himself. He calls her "Keanuphobe" in his head.
     * Luposlipaphobia – fear of being pursued by timber wolves around a
       kitchen table while wearing socks on a newly waxed floor. Coined
       humorously by cartoonist [838]Gary Larson for his comic [839]The
       Far Side.
     * Nihilophobia – fear of nothingness, from Latin nihil and "nothing,
       none", as described by [840]the Doctor in the [841]Star Trek:
       Voyager episode "[842]Night". Voyager's morale officer and chef
       Neelix has this condition, having [843]panic attacks while the ship
       was traversing a dark expanse of space known as the Void. It is
       also the title of a 2008 album by [844]Neuronium.
     * [845]Robophobia – irrational fear of robots and/or androids, also
       known as "[846]Grimwade's Syndrome". It was first used in "[847]The
       Robots of Death",^[848][53] the fifth serial of the [849]14th
       season of the British [850]science fiction television series
       [851]Doctor Who.
     * Semaphobia – fear of average web developers to use [852]Semantic
       Web technologies.^[853][54]
     * Venustraphobia – fear of beautiful women, according to a 1998
       [854]humorous article published by [855]BBC News.^[856][1]
