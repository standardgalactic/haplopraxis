   {\displaystyle T_{g}} in
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle

   {\displaystyle 0\leq T\leq 1} in the operator order. This is a version
   of the [107]Radon–Nikodym theorem.

   For such
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>g</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle g}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle g} , one can write
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle

   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle f}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle f} as a sum of positive linear functionals:
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>f</mi> <mo>=</mo> <mi>g</mi>

   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \pi
   }</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]

   is a pure state if and only if it is extremal in the convex set of
   states.

Generalizations

   [[110]edit]

   The [111]Stinespring factorization theorem characterizing
   [112]completely positive maps is an important generalization of the GNS
   construction.

History

   [[113]edit]

   Gelfand and Naimark's paper on the Gelfand–Naimark theorem was
   published in 1943.^[114][3] Segal recognized the construction that was
   implicit in this work and presented it in sharpened form.^[115][4]

   In his paper of 1947 Segal showed that it is sufficient, for any
   physical system that can be described by an algebra of operators on a
   Hilbert space, to consider the irreducible representations of a
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msup> <mi>C</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mo>∗</mo> </mrow> </msup> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   C^{*}}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle C^{*}} -algebra. In quantum theory this means that the
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msup> <mi>C</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mo>∗</mo> </mrow> </msup> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle
   C^{*}}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle C^{*}} -algebra is generated by the observables. This,
   as Segal pointed out, had been shown earlier by [116]John von Neumann
   only for the specific case of the non-relativistic
   Schrödinger-Heisenberg theory.^[117][5]
