   Chemical compound

   1,6-Dichloro-1,6-dideoxyfructose
   (dichlorodideoxyfructose)^[85][1]^[86][2]^[87][3] is [88]chlorinated
   derivative of the [89]sugar [90]fructose. It is one of the two
   components believed to comprise the [91]disaccharide
   [92]sucralose,^[93][4] a commercial [94]sugar substitute.

Metabolism

   [[95]edit]

   In mammals, 1,6-dichloro-1,6-dideoxyfructose is metabolized in the
   [96]liver and [97]erythrocytes by a reaction with [98]glutathione that
   replaces one of the chlorine atoms, forming [99]6-chlorofructos-1-yl
   glutathione (or chlorofructosyl glutathione).^[100][5]
