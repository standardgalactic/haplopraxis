   "100 Years Ago" is a song by the English rock band [79]the Rolling
   Stones featured on their 1973 album [80]Goats Head Soup.

Background

   [[81]edit]

   Credited to [82]Mick Jagger and [83]Keith Richards, guitarist [84]Mick
   Taylor said at the time of its release, "Some of the songs we used (for
   the album) were pretty old. '100 Years Ago' was one that Mick [Jagger]
   had written two years ago and which we hadn't really got around to
   using before."^[85][2] The song is described by Tom Maginnis in his
   review as having a, "wistful air with a country lilt... before making
   several tempo shifts into a funky, sped-up groove..."^[86][3] The
   song's lyrics see Jagger reflect on aging:

     Now all my friends is wearing worried smiles, Living out a dream of
     what they was; Don't you think it's sometimes wise not to grow up?

     Went out walkin' through the wood the other day; Can't you see the
     furrows in my forehead? What tender days, we had no secrets hid
     away; Now it seems about a hundred years ago

   The song then veers into a distinctive breakdown, slowing considerably
   before Jagger begins singing a verse in a noticeable drawl (beginning
   with the lyrics, "Call... Me... Lazybones... Ain't got no time to waste
   away"), before speeding back-up and turning into a [87]funk jam of
   sorts.^[88][3]

   Recording took place at [89]Dynamic Sounds studio in [90]Kingston,
   Jamaica, in November and December, 1972, with a final mix conducted in
   June 1973. Jagger performs lead vocals and is accompanied by Taylor on
   backing. Taylor performs the song's guitars while Keith Richards and
   [91]Charlie Watts perform bass and drums, respectively. [92]Nicky
   Hopkins provides piano while [93]Billy Preston performs
   [94]clavinet.^[95][4]

Live performances

   [[96]edit]

   "100 Years Ago" was only played on the first two performances of
   [97]European Tour of 1973, and has not been performed live
   since.^[[98]citation needed]
