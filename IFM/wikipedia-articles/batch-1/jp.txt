   Internet country-code top level domain for Japan

   CAPTION: .jp

   [155][120px-Dotjp.svg.png]
   Introduced August 5, 1986; 38 years ago (1986-08-05)
   [156]TLD type [157]Country code top-level domain
   Status Active
   [158]Registry [159]Japan Registry Services
   Sponsor [160]JPNIC
   Intended use Entities connected with [161]Japan
   Actual use Used in Japan
   Registered domains 1,742,261 (2023-07-01)^[162][1]
   Registration restrictions Second-level registrations require a Japanese
   mailing address; third-level registrations have varying rules depending
   on which second-level name they are beneath
   Structure Registrations permitted at second level and at third level
   beneath various second-level labels
   Documents [163]ICANN sponsorship agreement
   [164]DNSSEC yes
   Registry website [165]jprs.co.jp/en/

   .jp is the [166]Internet [167]country code [168]top-level domain
   ([169]ccTLD) for [170]Japan. It was established in 1986 and is
   administered by the [171]Japan Registry Services.

History

   [[172]edit]

   At the establishment of the .jp domain, the domain was administered by
   the [173]JPNIC, as part of their role as an overseeing technical body
   for the Internet in Japan. It was originally proposed by [174]Jun Murai
   for the [175]Information Sciences Institute at the [176]University of
   Southern California on August 5, 1986. Handling of the domain was first
   managed within the "junet-admin" admin group, which was responsible for
   the operations of [177]JUNET, an early computer network in which Murai
   was a part of.^[178][2]

   In April 1989, the junet-admin group began formally registering .jp
   domain names.^[179][3] However, due to the growing importance and size
   of the .jp registry, it was decided at the 11th General Meeting of
   JPNIC in December 2000 to create a new corporation that would manage
   the .jp domain. Thus, the Japan Registry Service was created, and on
   June 30, 2003, officially assumed the duties of the .jp [180]registry.

   .jp registrations are only allowed if the registrant has a physical
   address in Japan. Registrations are processed via [181]accredited
   registrars and [182]domain names with Japanese characters ([183]kanji,
   [184]hiragana or [185]katakana) may be registered at the second level.

Second-level domains

   [[186]edit]

   While any party with a Japanese mailing address can get a second-level
   domain (example.jp) there are several restricted-use second-level
   domains, listed below.^[187][4]
     * ac.jp: higher level academic institutions, such as universities
     * ad.jp: [188]JPNIC members
     * co.jp: most forms of incorporated companies, including foreign
       companies registered in Japan
     * ed.jp: educational institutions for individuals under 18
     * go.jp: Japanese government ministries and their endeavours
     * gr.jp: groups of two or more people, or groups of registered
       companies
     * lg.jp: local government authorities
     * ne.jp: network service providers
     * or.jp: registered organizations and non-profit organizations

Geographical type jp domain names

   [[189]edit]
     * (organization).(cityname).(prefecturename).jp^[190][5]

   Domains listed below are reserved for the local governments in Japan:
     * metro.tokyo.jp: reserved for the government of [191]Tokyo
       Metropolis
     * pref.(prefecturename).jp: reserved for the prefectural government
     * city.(cityname).jp: reserved for [192]cities designated by
       government ordinance
     * city.(cityname).(prefecturename).jp: reserved for non-designated
       cities and, wards and cities within Tokyo
     * town.(townname).(prefecturename).jp: reserved for towns
     * vill.(villagename).(prefecturename).jp: reserved for villages

Internationalized top-level domains

   [[193]edit]

   Japan has considered registering an [194]internationalized country code
   top-level domain, .日本.^[195][6] In 2008, a preliminary application was
   made.^[196][7] As of 2022,^[197][update] no such domain has been
   registered.^[198][8]

   Private companies have registered the following [199]internationalized
   generic top-level domains using Japanese script:
     * minna (.みんな, everyone)
     * sēru (.セール, sale)
     * fasshon (.ファッション, fashion)
     * sutoa (.ストア, store)
     * pointo (.ポイント, [200]point)
     * kuraudo (.クラウド, [201]cloud)
     * komu (.コム, com), the most used Japanese script top-level
       domain^[[202]citation needed]

   Cities or regions in Japan have registered these geographic top-level
   domains:
     * [203].kyoto ([204]Kyoto)
     * .nagoya ([205]Nagoya)
     * .okinawa ([206]Okinawa)
     * .osaka ([207]Osaka)
     * .ryukyu ([208]Ryukyu, a former kingdom, often used as a traditional
       alias of Okinawa)
     * [209].tokyo ([210]Tokyo)
     * .yokohama ([211]Yokohama)
