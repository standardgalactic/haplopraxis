   100 Thieves, LLC (abbreviated 100T) is an American [112]lifestyle brand
   and [113]gaming organization based in [114]Los Angeles, California,
   founded in 2017 by [115]Matthew "Nadeshot" Haag. The organization
   competes in several [116]video games, including [117]Apex Legends,
   [118]Call of Duty, [119]League of Legends and [120]Valorant. They
   currently operate three franchise teams, in the [121]League of Legends
   Championship of The Americas (LTA), [122]Valorant Americas League and
   [123]Call of Duty League (branded as [124]Los Angeles Thieves); their
   LTA team is currently a "provisional guest partner" in the North
   Conference.

History

   [[125]edit]

   100 Thieves was founded in 2017 by [126]Matthew "Nadeshot" Haag, a
   former OpTic Gaming [127]Call of Duty team captain, (MLG) [128]X Games
   gold medalist, and 2014 Esports Athlete of the year.^[129][2] In
   November 2017, 100 Thieves received a multimillion-dollar investment
   from [130]Cleveland Cavaliers and [131]Quicken Loans owner [132]Dan
   Gilbert allowing the company to expand into a full-fledged esports
   organization.^[133][3]

   On October 23, 2018, 100 Thieves announced that they finished their
   Series A funding round co-led by [134]Scooter Braun and [135]Drake, who
   both became co-owners when they raised $25 million. Included in this
   round was [136]Sequoia Capital, Ludlow Ventures, Courtside Ventures,
   WndrCo, [137]Marc Benioff, [138]Drew Houston, Green Bay Ventures,
   [139]Tao Capital and [140]Advancit Capital.^[141][4] Their Series B
   funding round on July 16, 2019, was led by Artist Capital Management
   which raised $35 million. Included in this round is Aglae Ventures,
   [142]Groupe Arnault (controlling shareholder of [143]Louis Vuitton Moet
   Hennessy).^[144][5]

   In March 2021, 100 Thieves signed a sponsorship deal with Truly Seltzer
   and [145]Twisted Tea.^[146][6] They acquired gaming peripheral company
   Higround in October 2021, marking their first acquisition.^[147][7] On
   December 2, 2021, 100 Thieves announced their Series C funding round,
   which raised $60 million.^[148][8]

   In May 2022, 100 Thieves announced its intentions to publish a video
   game. It hired former [149]Telltale Games CEO Pete Hawley as its Chief
   Product Officer to set up a studio.^[150][9] In July, more than 10
   members of 100 Thieves' content team were laid off.^[151][10] Nadeshot
   launched his own energy drink, called Juvee, on October 4.^[152][11]

   In January 2023, around 30 employees were laid off, including several
   lead staff members.^[153][12] That November, 100 Thieves announced it
   was laying off 20% of its workforce, affecting employees at its game
   studio and energy drink brand. These business units would also be spun
   off as separate companies in order to focus on esports and
   apparel.^[154][13]

   On May 6, 2024, the Esports World Cup Foundation, funded by the
   [155]Saudi Arabia [156]Public Investment Fund and organizers of the
   Esports World Cup tournament series, announced the 30 organizations
   (known in the ESWC as Clubs) who would make up the Club Support
   Program, with 100 Thieves being one of them. This program gives teams a
   one-time six-figure stipend if an organization is willing to enter new
   esports as well as additional funding each year if they drive
   viewership and fan engagement to the Esports World Cup.^[157][14]

Divisions

   [[158]edit]

League of Legends

   [[159]edit]

Inaugural season

   [[160]edit]

   On November 20, 2017, 100 Thieves was accepted as a franchise
   organization for the [161]2018 NA LCS season.^[162][15] The team signed
   Neil "pr0lly" Hammad as its head coach and [163]Kim "Ssumday" Chan-ho,
   [164]William "Meteos" Hartman, Ryu "Ryu" Sang-wook, [165]Cody Sun, and
   [166]Zaqueri "Aphromoo" Black for its starting roster.^[167][16] The
   team placed first in the spring split [168]regular season with a 12–6
   record, securing a bye into the semifinals. Additionally, Aphromoo was
   voted as [169]MVP of the spring split.^[170][17] The first team they
   faced in the [171]playoffs was [172]Clutch Gaming, and 100 Thieves won
   3–2,^[173][18] moving on to the finals, where they were [174]swept 0–3
   by [175]Team Liquid in the finals.^[176][19]

   100 Thieves' second-place finish qualified them for [177]Rift Rivals
   2018, an international tournament between the top three spring teams
   from [178]Europe and North America. The team elected to use substitute
   player Đỗ "Levi" Duy Khánh in place of Meteos for the
   tournament.^[179][20] [180]Team Liquid, 100 Thieves, and [181]Echo Fox
   competed against Europe's [182]Fnatic, [183]G2 Esports, and
   [184]Splyce, going a combined 4–5 in the double round-robin group stage
   with 100 Thieves with a 1–2 record after a single win against
   [185]Splyce. In the best-of-five "relay race" finals, the team lost
   their game against [186]Fnatic, contributing to North America's
   combined 1–3 loss to Europe.^[187][21]

   Before the summer split, 100 Thieves traded Meteos to [188]Flyquest, in
   exchange for their jungler, Andy "AnDa" Hoang.^[189][22] Following this
   roster move, the team placed third in the regular season with a 10–8
   record. The team won their first match 3–0 over FlyQuest,^[190][23]
   before losing 1–3 to [191]Team Liquid in the semi-finals,^[192][24]
   then losing 2–3 in the [193]third place match to [194]Team
   SoloMid.^[195][25]

   The team's performance across both splits allowed them to qualify for
   the [196]2018 League of Legends World Championship in [197]South Korea
   as North America's second seed.^[198][26] The team was drawn to
   [199]Group D with Europe's [200]Fnatic, China's [201]Invictus Gaming,
   and Hong Kong's [202]G-Rex.^[203][27] Shortly after qualifying for the
   world championship, they replaced Cody Sun with substitute player
   Richard "Rikara" Oh.^[204][28] 100 Thieves finished third in their
   group with a 2–4 record, and 12th overall, not qualifying for the
   knockout stage.^[205][29]

2019–2022

   [[206]edit]

   In 2019, the team extended top laner Ssumday's contract, while Ryu
   moved to an assistant position.^[207][30]^[208][31] Cody Sun and Rikara
   opted to leave the team, and they were replaced by [209]Choi "huhi"
   Jae-hyun and Bae "Bang" Jan-sik. Partway through the spring split, Huhi
   was benched in favor for Max "Soligo" Soong, but despite attempts at
   change, the team finished the spring split in dead last, with a 4–14
   record, and elected to make additional roster changes for summer. Huhi
   left the team, and Maurice "Amazing" Stückenschneider joined to replace
   AnDa, who was moved to the academy roster along with Ryu.^[210][32] A
   month into the split, the team replaced Soligo with Ryu from the
   academy roster, and also swapped Ssumday with Aaron "FakeGod" Lee, as
   LCS rules required at least 3 North American [211]residents on the
   starting roster.^[212][33] The team finished the summer split in eighth
   place and did not qualify for the post–season.^[213][34]

   100 Thieves began the [214]2020 season with the announcement that Chris
   "PapaSmithy" Smith would be joining the team as the new general
   manager.^[215][35] Tony "Zikz" Gray was the next addition to the
   roster, replacing pr0lly as head coach,^[216][36] and both Meteos and
   Cody Sun rejoined the team for the second time,^[217][37] alongside new
   additions William "Stunt" Chen and Tommy "Ryoma" Le. Ryu, Amazing, Bang
   and Aphromoo all left the team, with FakeGod rejoining Academy and
   Ssumday rejoining the main roster.^[218][38] In spring, the team
   finished third in the regular season with a 10–8 record, and qualified
   for post–season for the first time since 2018.^[219][39] However once
   in the playoffs,^[220][40] they were swept 0–3 by Cloud9,^[221][41] and
   then lost 2–3 to Team SoloMid in the losers' bracket.^[222][42] After
   an 1–5 start in the summer split, the team parted ways with players
   Meteos and Stunt, and called up academy players Juan "Contractz" Garcia
   and Philippe "Poome" Lavoie-Giguere to replace them.^[223][43] The team
   finished in seventh at the end of the summer split with a 7–11 record,
   and were seeded into the loser's bracket of the playoffs, where they
   would be swept 0–3 by Evil Geniuses to finish their 2020
   season.^[224][44]

   The [225]2021 season started with the team signing Can "Closer" Çelik,
   Victor "FBI" Huang, [226]Tanner "Damonte" Damonte, and a returning Huhi
   from [227]Golden Guardians.^[228][45] Ry0ma (renamed from Ryoma) and
   Poome were moved to the academy roster,^[229][46] while Cody Sun and
   Contractz both left the team.^[230][47]^[231][48] The team also added
   Aleš "Freeze" Kněžínek and Ham "Lustboy" Jang-sik to the coaching
   staff.^[232][49]^[233][50] The season started off with the preseason
   Lock−In tournament,^[234][51] and the team starting strong, placing
   first in their group, before sweeping [235]Immortals 2–0 in the first
   round of the knock−out stage. In the semifinals they faced Cloud9, and
   despite winning the first two games, were reverse swept to lose 2–3 in
   the series, and finish 3rd/4th in the tournament.^[236][52] In week 5
   of the spring split, the team decided to bring back Ry0ma as their
   starter and sent Damonte to academy.^[237][53] They finished the spring
   split in third place, and in the Mid-Season Showdown,^[238][54] were
   swept 0–3 by Cloud9,^[239][55] before picking up a 3–0 win of their own
   against [240]Dignitas in the losers bracket.^[241][56] The team faced
   Team SoloMid next, and fell 1–3, and were eliminated from
   playoffs.^[242][57]

   After a fourth place finish in the spring split, the team parted ways
   with head coach Zikz and hired Bok "Reapered" Han-gyu as his
   replacement.^[243][58] Prior to the start of the summer split, 100
   Thieves signed Felix "Abbedagge" Braun from the [244]LEC, and Ry0ma was
   subsequently sent back to academy,^[245][59] while Damonte was dropped
   by the organization.^[246][60] The revamped roster would go on to
   finish second in the regular season, with a record of 29–16. The team
   won their first playoff match 3–2 against Evil Geniuses, before falling
   2–3 to Team Liquid. In the losers' bracket, 100 Thieves defeated Cloud9
   3–1 to set up a finals rematch against Team Liquid. This time around,
   100 Thieves defeated Team Liquid in a 3–0 sweep, to win the 2021 LCS
   Championship^[247][61]^[248][62]^[249][63] and qualify for the
   [250]2021 League of Legends World Championship.^[251][64] The team's
   performance secured them a bye into [252]Group B alongside China's
   [253]Edward Gaming, Korea's [254]T1, and Japan's [255]DetonatioN
   FocusMe.^[256][65] The team finished third in their group, with a 3–3
   record, and were eliminated from the tournament, placing 9th–12th
   overall and ending their season.^[257][66]

   In 2022, the team announced all five players would be
   returning,^[258][67] with academy top laner Milan "Tenacity" Oleksij as
   a sixth man.^[259][68] [260]Alfonso "Mithy" Rodriguez also joined the
   coaching staff in the off−season.^[261][69] Once again, 100 Thieves
   were atop their group in the Lock−In tournament,^[262][70] however they
   were upset 0–2 by Dignitas in the quarterfinals of the Knock−Out stage,
   and eliminated early.^[263][71]^[264][72] Through the first round robin
   of the spring split, 100 Thieves accumulated a 5–4 record, putting them
   in a three way tie for third place.^[265][73] 100 Thieves ultimately
   ended the spring split with a record of 12–6, securing themselves third
   place. In the first round of Playoffs, they swept Cloud 9 3–0 to
   advance to the winner bracket finals, where they came back from a 0–2
   deficit to reverse sweep Team Liquid and advance to Grand
   Finals.^[266][74] There, they lost 0–3 to Evil Geniuses, denying 100
   Thieves back to back championships.^[267][75]

   Following a group stage exit at the [268]2022 World Championship,
   General manager PapaSmithy and coaches Repeared, Mithy, and Freeze
   parted ways with the team.^[269][76]^[270][77]

2023

   [[271]edit]

   For the [272]2023 season, 100 Thieves released the entire roster except
   Closer, signing veterans [273]Yiliang "Doublelift" Peng and [274]Søren
   "Bjergsen" Bjerg and promoting Alan "Busio" Cwalina from the academy,
   and Tenacity to full-time top laner.^[275][78] The team also hired
   Christopher "Kaas" Oudheusden as their new head coach with Erlend
   "Nukeduck" Holm joining as an assistant and promoting Joseph
   "JungleJuice" Jang as general manager..^[276][79] After a 5-8 start to
   the Spring Split, Kaas and 100 Thieves parted ways and Nukeduck was
   promoted to interim head coach with Danny "Dan Dan" Comte joining as an
   assistant from the challengers team. Following a quick exit in Spring
   playoffs, Bjergsen announced his retirement.^[277][80]

   After Bjergsen's retirement, the team announced their new head coach in
   Jang "Cain" Nu-ri and the signing of Lim "Quid" Hyeon-seung as their
   new mid laner. The team also brought back Ssumday as Tenacity decided
   to move to content creation. The team finished with a 7-11 record and
   placed 7th-8th in the summer split championship.

2024

   [[278]edit]

   For the 2024 season, the team parted ways with the whole roster and
   coaching staff except for Quid, signing Rayan "Sniper" Shoura, Kim
   "River" Dong-woo, Brandon "Meech" Choi, and Bill "Eyla" Nguyen. The
   team also announced [279]Greyson "Goldenglue" Gilmer as their new head
   coach. The team finished 5th-8th in the Spring playoffs with a 10-4
   record in the regular season.

   During the Summer split, Meech was benched and the team signed Frank
   "Tomo" Lam. The team finished the regular season with a 3-4 record and
   finished 3rd in the Summer playoffs and qualified for the [280]2024
   World Championship play-in, where they would be eliminated by [281]PSG
   Talon. In October 2024, Meech was released. On October 31, 100 Thieves
   announced their intention to sell their LCS franchise spot for the 2025
   season, however, after close collaboration with Riot Games, 100 Thieves
   would continue to take part in the LCS (now reformed as the [282]League
   of Legends Championship of The Americas (LTA) as the LCS merged with
   both [283]CBLOL and [284]LLA) as a "guest partner".^[285][81] In
   November 2024, Tomo was released and FBI was re-signed as the new
   starting bot laner.

Call of Duty

   [[289]edit]

2019 season

   [[290]edit]

   The team started the season off signing the team of Kenneth "Kenny"
   Williams, Maurice "Fero" Henriquez, Sam "Octane" Larew, Austin
   "SlasheR" Liddicoat, and Ian "Enable" Wyatt.^[291][82] They placed
   9th–12th at CWL Las Vegas 2019, qualifying for the Pro League
   Qualifiers and for the Pro League. After this, they had to re-evaluate
   the roster, and acquired Preston "Priestahh" Greiner and head coach
   James "Crowder" Crowder on loans from [292]FaZe Clan, with Fero getting
   benched.^[293][83] At CWL Fort Worth 2019, the team finished 4th losing
   to Team Reciprocity, citing medical issues with player Priestahh and
   being replaced by Fero.^[[294]citation needed] At CWL London 2019, the
   team won the organization's first trophy.^[295][84] At CWL Anaheim
   2019, the team won the organization's second trophy.^[296][85] After
   the Pro League, 100 Thieves finished second in Division B, qualifying
   for Pro League playoffs. At the Pro League Playoffs, the team placed
   5th–6th, losing to [297]Gen.G esports. In the 2019 Call of Duty World
   Championship, 100 Thieves finished second, with a loss to
   eUnited.^[298][86]

Valorant

   [[308]edit]

   On June 4, 2020, 100 Thieves Esports announced their entrance into the
   Valorant competitive scene through the signing of Spencer "Hiko"
   Martin. Within three weeks of signing Hiko, the team was finalized. The
   team also signed Hector "FrosT" Rosario as their head coach but he was
   announced until months later.^[309][88]^[310][89] The original team
   consisted of Keane "Valliate" Alonso, Diondre "YaBoiDre" Bond, Zachary
   "Venerated" Roach, and Alfred "Pride" Choi. This team, however, did not
   last long; on August 14, 2020, the entire team, apart from Hiko, was
   released.^[311][90]^[312][91] Shortly thereafter, the team went on to
   sign [313]Counter-Strike: Global Offensive veterans Nick "nitr0"
   Cannella and Joshua "steel" Nissan.^[314][92]^[315][93] 2 months later,
   the team was once again finalized after signing Peter "Asuna" Mazuryk
   and Quan "dicey" Tran from [316]Immortals.^[317][94] The team went on
   to win the very first Valorant First Strike tournament, defeating
   [318]TSM in the grand finals.^[319][95]

   On January 26, 2021, the team brought in Jonathan "silenx" Huntington
   as a substitute in place of nitr0, due to nitr0's wife going into
   labor.^[320][96] On February 28, 2021, the team benched dicey in favor
   of another veteran CS:GO player, Ethan "Ethan" Arnold.^[321][97] After
   the Masters 3 tourney, the team decided to call up their substitute
   Aaron "b0i" Thao to the starting roster and moved steel to the bench.
   The team also added Johann "seven" Hernandez as a substitute.^[322][98]
   Their 2021 season ended after falling to Cloud9 in the NA Last Chance
   Qualifier for Valorant Champions. On November 12, the team fired
   FrosT.^[323][99] On November 23, dicey and b0i were dropped from the
   team.^[324][100] Soon after, both steel and nitr0 departed the
   team.^[325][101]

   They started 2022 with the signing of Adam "ec1s" Eccles and Hunter
   "BabyJ" Schline and promoting analyst Jovanni "Jovi" Vera to head
   coach.^[326][102] Shockingly, after two losses in the 2022 NA VCT
   Challengers Stage 1 group stage, ec1s and BabyJ were released; the team
   then acquired Sean "bang" Bezerra and Noah "jcStani" Smith on
   loans.^[327][103] After being eliminated in the 2022 NA VCT Challengers
   group stage, the team parted ways with Jovi and hired [328]Sean
   "sgares" Gares as head coach with Michael "Mikes" Hockom joining him as
   an assistant, and hiring Daniel "ddk" Kapadia as general
   manager.^[329][104] On March 27, jcStani announced his free agency
   after his loan period ended with the team.^[330][105] On April 12, Hiko
   announced his retirement from competitive Valorant but remained with
   the organization as a content creator.^[331][106] A couple days later,
   bang was fully bought out and the team then signed Derrek "Derrek" Ha,
   Brenden "stellar" McGrath, and William "Will" Cheng, while Ethan was
   moved to the bench and later released.^[332][107] The team was able to
   qualify for [333]2022 Valorant Champions after beating The Guard in the
   grand finals of the NA Last Chance Qualifier.^[334][108] The team was
   then placed Group D alongside [335]DRX, FURIA Esports, and
   [336]Fnatic.^[337][109] The team finished the tournament
   9-12th.^[338][110] Following the end of the regular 2022 season, 100
   Thieves released Will and signed [339]XSET star player Matthew
   "Cryocells" Panganiban,^[340][111] and Mikes was promoted to head
   coach, following Sgares's departure.^[341][112] 100 Thieves ended the
   year by winning the [342]Red Bull Home Ground tournament #3 in
   [343]Manchester with a 3–0 win over [344]Cloud9 in December
   2022.^[345][113]

   In September 2022, 100 Thieves was selected as one of the thirty teams
   to be part of Riot Games' partnership program for the 2023
   season.^[346][114] During the season, they failed to qualify to
   Americas League playoffs and were also eliminated in their first match
   in the Americas Last Chance Qualifier. On September 12, 2023, 100
   Thieves announced the release of Derrek and
   stellar.^[347][115]^[348][116]

   On September 25, 2023, 100 Thieves signed Tony "Zikz" Gray as head
   coach, who had previously been head coach for their [349]League of
   Legends team.^[350][117] Mikes was moved back to assistant coach. On
   November 1, 2023, 100 Thieves announced Daniel "eeiu" Vucenovic to the
   starting roster.^[351][118] 100 Thieves competed in the main event of
   Red Bull Home Ground #4 in [352]Tokyo with Nicholas "NaturE" Garrison
   as a stand-in, and placed fifth.^[353][119]

   On January 8, 2024, 100 Thieves rounded out their roster with in-game
   leader, Kelden "Boostio" Pupello. Boostio, along with Zikz, previously
   won the [354]2023 Valorant Champions with [355]Evil
   Geniuses.^[356][120] The season started off poorly with 100 Thieves
   losing all their games in the Kickoff tournament and being
   eliminated.^[357][121] In Americas Stage 1, they placed second in their
   group and qualified for the playoffs. With a clean sweep of their
   playoff games including a 3–0 win over [358]G2 Esports in the grand
   final, they became Stage 1 champions and qualified to [359]Masters
   Shanghai as the first seed from Americas, earning a bye to the playoffs
   and 9 circuit points for the [360]2024 Valorant Champions.^[361][122]
   At Shanghai, they placed 4th after beating FUT and [362]Paper Rex but
   losing to both eventual finalists [363]Gen.G and Team Heretics. With a
   2–3 record in Americas Stage 2, the team made it into playoffs but
   placed 4th, ending their year one win away from qualifying to
   Champions.^[364][123] On October 3, it was announced that Bang would be
   leaving after two and a half years with the team.^[365][124] On October
   8, Alexander "Zander" Dituri was announced as the new fifth member of
   the roster.

Apex Legends

   [[368]edit]

   In February 2019, 100 Thieves announced their entrance in the Apex
   Legends competitive scene, signing Connor "Gigz" White, Justin
   "Teenage" Phipps, and Isiah "Lifted" Slowik as their first roster. In

   "Vein" Hobbs. In February 2022, the team released Vein and signed Alan
   "Vaxlon" Gonzalez, with Tyler "Sickks" Clark joining later on as their
   new head coach. The team placed 3rd in the [369]2022 Apex Legends
   Global Series World Championship. In September 2023, 100 Thieves
   released their entire roster.

   Gardner, [370]Noyan "Genburten" Ozkose, and Timothy "iiTzTimmy" An,

Former divisions

Clash Royale

   [[376]edit]

   In April 2018, 100 Thieves was announced as member of the Clash Royale
   League. In the following months, 100 Thieves announced the roster of
   Eduadro "Eddie" Rojas-Torras, Jacob "Frost" Horsch, James "Pt105"
   Keresey, and Hoan "PureZuhn" Le, with Davis "Gilgamesh" Huang as head
   coach and William "Woody" Hix as the team's general manager.

   In December 2018, 100 Thieves announced their departure from
   competitive Clash Royale.

Counter-Strike: Global Offensive

   [[377]edit]

   In December 2017, 100 Thieves announced that they signed the former
   roster of [378]Immortals which included players Bruno "BIT" Lima,
   Lincoln "fnx" Lau, Henrique "HEN1" Teles, Vito "kNgV-" Giuseppe, and
   Lucas "LUCAS1" Teles with Rodolfo "bLecker" Blecker as head
   coach.^[379][126] However, the organization had issues with visas,
   resulting in the team being disbanded.^[380][127]

   In late October 2019, 100 Thieves announced the signing of the former
   [381]Renegades roster which included Aaron "AZR" Ward, Sean
   "Gratisfaction" Kaiwai, Joakim "jkaem" Myrbostad, Justin "jks" Savage,
   and Jay "Liazz" Tregillgas with Aleksandar "kassad" Trifunović as head
   coach.^[382][128] At IEM Beijing 2019, the team placed second in the
   tournament, losing to [383]Astralis.^[384][129] The team placed 7th–8th
   at the ESL Season 10 Pro League Finals, losing to
   [385]Fnatic.^[386][130] On October 12, 2020, 100 Thieves announced
   their departure from the competitive CS:GO scene, citing complications
   with travel, a focus on European events, and COVID-19 as the main
   reasons behind the move.^[387][131]

Fortnite: Battle Royale

   [[388]edit]

   In July 2018, 100 Thieves announced their entrance into the Fortnite:
   Battle Royale competitive scene, announcing the signings of Kenith,
   Archie "Parallax" Sharder, and Alex "Risker" Biamonte, with
   SirDimetrious joining a month later.

   At the beginning of 2019, Risker and Parallax were released and the
   team signed Maurilio "Blind" Gramajo, Davis "Ceice" McClellan, Hayden
   "Elevate" Krueger, Brandon "Klass" Weaver, and Gabriel "Kyuzi" Harlos.
   At the end of 2019, Blind, Kenith, and SirDimetrious were released,
   with the team signing Diego "Arkhram" Lima, Brendan "Falconer"
   Facloner, and Brett "grandmateets" Squires. At the [389]Fortnite World
   Cup, Ceice and Elevate placed 3rd in the duos competition, while
   Arkhram and Falconer placed 5th.

   In 2020, the team signed Martin "MrSavage" Andersen and Brodie "rehx"
   Franks, with Grandmateets, Klass, and Kyzui leaving the team.

   In February 2021, Ceice and Elevate left the team. The next year, 100
   Thieves exited the Fortnite: Battle Royale competitive scene, releasing
   the remaining roster members.

100 Thieves Compound

   [[390]edit]

   The 100 Thieves Compound is a 15,000 square foot esports,
   entertainment, and apparel hub, located in [391]Culver City,
   California.^[392][132] It has four sports training rooms: the
   [393]Rocket Mortgage League of Legends training room, the [394]AT&T
   Valorant training room, the [395]Totino's Fortnite training room, and
   the League of Legends Academy training room. It also has a content
   studio, which is worth around half a million dollars.^[396][133] Other
   areas include the Cash App Lounge, the [397]Totino's basketball court,
   many business operation areas and four streaming pods. Chairs,
   catering, and PCs are provided by Secretlab, [398]Chipotle^[399][134]
   and [400]NZXT^[401][135] respectively. It was formerly known as the 100
   Thieves [402]Cash App Compound.

   The compound served as an LA County [403]vote center for the [404]2020
   United States presidential election.^[405][136]
