   + h.c. is an abbreviation for "plus the [63]H ermitian c onjugate"; it
   means is that there are additional terms which are the [64]Hermitian
   conjugates of all of the preceding terms, and is a convenient shorthand
   to omit half the terms actually present.^[65][1]^[66][2]

Context and use

   [[67]edit]

   The [68]notation convention "+ h.c." is common in [69]quantum mechanics
   in the context of writing out formulas for [70]Lagrangians and
   [71]Hamiltonians, which conventionally are both required to be
   [72]Hermitian operators.^[73][2]

   The mathematics of quantum mechanics is based on [75]complex numbers,
   whereas almost all observations (measurements) are only [76]real
   numbers. Adding its own [77]conjugate to an operator guarantees that
   the combination is [78]Hermitian, which in turn guarantees that the
   combined operator's eigenvalues will be real numbers, suitable for
   predicting values of observations / measurements.^[79][1]

Dagger and asterisk notation

   [[80]edit]

   In the expressions above,
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msup> <mi>A</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mo>†</mo> </mrow> </msup> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle A^{\dagger
   }}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle A^{\dagger }} is used as the symbol for the
   [81]Hermitian conjugate (also called the [82]conjugate transpose) of
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>A</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle A}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle A} , defined as applying both the [83]complex conjugate
   and the [84]transpose transformations to the operator
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>A</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle A}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle A} , in any order.

   The dagger (
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mo>†</mo> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \dagger
   }</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \dagger } ) is an old notation in mathematics, but is
   still widespread in quantum-mechanics. In mathematics (particularly
   [85]linear algebra) the Hermitian conjugate of
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mi>A</mi> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle A}</annotation>
   </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle A} is commonly written as
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <msup> <mi>A</mi> <mrow
   class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mo>∗</mo> </mrow> </msup> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle A^{\ast
   }}</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle A^{\ast }} , but in quantum mechanics the asterisk (
   [MATH: <semantics> <mrow class="MJX-TeXAtom-ORD"> <mstyle
   displaystyle="true" scriptlevel="0"> <mo>∗</mo> </mstyle> </mrow>
   <annotation encoding="application/x-tex">{\displaystyle \ast
   }</annotation> </semantics> :MATH]
   {\displaystyle \ast } ) notation is sometimes used for the [86]complex
   conjugate only, and not the combined [87]conjugate transpose
   ([88]Hermitian conjugate).
