   In a [185]quantum field theory with [186]fermions, (−1)^F is a
   [187]unitary, [188]Hermitian, [189]involutive [190]operator where F is
   the [191]fermion [192]number operator. For the example of particles in
   the [193]Standard Model, it is equal to the sum of the lepton number
   plus the baryon number, F = B + L. The action of this operator is to
   multiply [194]bosonic states by 1 and [195]fermionic states by −1. This
   is always a global [196]internal symmetry of any quantum field theory
   with fermions and corresponds to a rotation by 2π. This splits the
   [197]Hilbert space into two [198]superselection sectors. Bosonic
   operators [199]commute with (−1)^F whereas fermionic operators
   [200]anticommute with it.^[201][1]

   This operator really shows its utility in [202]supersymmetric
   theories.^[203][1] [204]Its trace is the [205]spectral asymmetry of the
   fermion spectrum, and can be understood physically as the [206]Casimir
   effect.
