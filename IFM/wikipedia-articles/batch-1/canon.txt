   Canon Inc. ([193]Japanese: キヤノン株式会社;^[194][note 1] [195]Hepburn: Kyanon
   [196]kabushiki gaisha) is a Japanese [197]multinational corporation
   headquartered in [198]Ōta, [199]Tokyo, specializing in optical,
   imaging, and industrial products, such as [200]lenses, [201]cameras,
   medical equipment, [202]scanners, [203]printers, and semiconductor
   manufacturing equipment.^[204][4]

   Canon has a primary listing on the [205]Tokyo Stock Exchange and is a
   constituent of the [206]TOPIX Core 30 and [207]Nikkei 225 indexes. It
   used to have a secondary listing on the [208]New York Stock Exchange.

Name

   [[209]edit]

   The company was originally named Seikikōgaku kenkyūsho ([210]Japanese:
   精機光学研究所, [211]lit. 'Precision Optical Laboratory'). In 1934, it
   produced the Kwanon, a prototype for Japan's first-ever 35 mm camera
   with a focal-plane-based shutter.^[212][5] In 1947, the company name
   was changed to Canon Camera Co., Inc.,^[213][5] shortened to Canon Inc.
   in 1969. The name Canon comes from Buddhist [214]bodhisattva Kannon
   ([215]Japanese: 観音, [216]lit. '[217]Guanyin'), previously
   transliterated as Kuanyin, Kwannon, or Kwanon in
   English.^[[218]citation needed]

History

   [[219]edit]

1933–1970

   [[220]edit]

   The origins of Canon date back to the founding of Precision Optical
   Instruments Laboratory in Japan in 1933^[221][5] by [222]Takeshi
   Mitarai, [223]Goro Yoshida [[224]jp], [225]Saburo Uchida [[226]jp] and
   [227]Takeo Maeda [[228]jp]. It became the company Precision Optical
   Instruments, Co., Ltd. in 1937.^[229][5] During its early years, the
   company did not have any facilities to produce its own optical glass,
   and its first cameras incorporated Nikkor lenses from Nippon Kogaku
   K.K. (the later [230]Nikon Corporation).^[231][6]

   Between 1933 and 1936, 'The Kwanon', a copy of the [232]Leica design,
   Japan's first 35 mm focal-plane-shutter camera, was developed in
   prototype form.^[233][7] In 1940 Canon developed Japan's first indirect
   X-ray camera. Canon introduced a field [234]zoom lens for
   [235]television broadcasting in 1958, and in 1959 introduced the Reflex
   Zoom 8 and the [236]Canonflex.

   In 1961, Canon introduced the Rangefinder camera, [237]Canon 7, and
   50mm 1:0.95 lens in a special bayonet mount. In 1964 Canon introduced
   the 'Canola 130', the first Japanese made 10-key [238]calculator, a
   substantial improvement on the design of the British [239]Bell Punch
   company, which introduced the first fully electronic calculator two
   years earlier with the Sumlock Anita Mark 8 unit. In 1965 Canon
   introduced the [240]Canon Pellix, a [241]single lens reflex (SLR)
   camera with a semi-transparent stationary mirror which enabled the
   taking of pictures through the mirror.

1970–2009

   [[251]edit]

   In 1971, Canon introduced the [252]Canon F-1, a high-end SLR camera,
   and the [253]FD lens range. In 1976, Canon launched the [254]Canon
   AE-1, the world's first camera with an embedded
   micro-computer.^[255][8]

   Canon introduced their [256]Inkjet printer using bubble-jet technology
   in 1985, one year after Hewlett-Packard. In 1987, Canon introduced
   their [257]Canon Electro-Optical System (EOS), named after the goddess
   of the dawn, along with the [258]Canon EOS 650 autofocus SLR camera.
   Also in 1987, the Canon Foundation was established. In 1988, Canon
   introduced 'Kyosei philosophy'.^[259][9] The EOS 1 Flagship
   Professional SLR line was launched in 1989.^[260][10] In the same year
   the EOS RT, the world's first AF SLR with a fixed, semi-transparent
   pellicle mirror, was unveiled.

   In 1992, Canon launched the [261]Canon EOS 5, the first-ever camera
   with eye-controlled AF, and the PowerShot 600, its first digital
   camera.^[262][11] In 1995, Canon introduced the first commercially
   available SLR lens with internal image stabilization, [263]Canon EF
   75-300mm lens f/4-5.6 IS USM. The Canon EOS-RS was the world's fastest
   AF SLR camera with a continuous shooting speed of 10 frame/s at the
   time. Based on the EOS-1N, the EOS-1N RS had a fixed, semi-transparent
   pellicle mirror with a hard coat. In 1996, Canon introduced a
   pocket-sized digital camera with the [264]Advanced Photo System, named
   ELPH in America and IXUS in Europe. Canon entered the digital video
   camcorder market in 1997.^[265][12]

   In 2004, Canon introduced the XEED SX50 LCD projector.^[266][13] Canon
   introduced its first high-definition [267]camcorder in 2005.^[268][14]

   In November 2009, Canon made a €730 million (US$1.1 billion) all-cash
   offer for the Dutch printer maker [269]Océ.^[270][15] Canon had
   acquired majority ownership of Océ by March 2010,^[271][16] and
   completed the acquisition of 100% of shares in Océ by the end of
   2011.^[272][17]

2010–2019

   [[278]edit]

   In 2010, Canon acquired Tereck Office Solutions, Inc.^[279][18]

   On 16 March 2010, Canon announced that it was seeking to acquire a new
   .canon [280]generic top-level domain, acquiring it in February 2015 and
   using it for the first time on its global website in May
   2016.^[281][19]^[282][20]

   In the third quarter of 2012, Canon's global market share in the sale
   of printers, copiers and multifunction devices was 20.90%.^[283][21]

   In early 2013, Canon USA moved into a new US$500 million headquarters
   in [284]Melville, New York.^[285][22]^[286][23]

   In February 2014, Canon announced it would acquire [287]Texas-based
   Molecular Imprints Inc., a developer of nanoprint lithography systems,
   for an amount speculated to be around US$98 million.^[288][24]

   On 13 June 2014, Canon announced it had acquired Danish IP Surveillance
   VMS software company Milestone Systems. Milestone provides
   open-platform software to allow video management from various vendors
   in a single interface; therefore the company will operate as a separate
   entity.^[289][25]

   On 10 February 2015, Canon announced that it had intentions to buy
   Swedish Security Camera maker [290]Axis Communications for US$2.83
   billion.^[291][26] On 23 February 2015, Axis Communications reacted to
   this news and confirmed that it had received a purchase proposal from
   Canon. The purchase was effectively completed in April 2015.^[292][27]

   On 24 April 2015, Canon Europe announced it had acquired the
   London-based family photo sharing startup Lifecake.^[293][28]^[294][29]

   In November 2015, in an effort to avoid the selling of gray-market
   camera gear, Canon USA filed litigation against a number of camera gear
   retailers. Retailers include Get It Digital, All New Shop and F&E
   Trading.^[295][30]

   In March 2016, Canon acquired [296]Toshiba Medical Systems Corporation
   for US$5.9 billion.^[297][31]

   On 28 March 2017, Canon Europe announced it had acquired the
   London-based printing startup Kite.^[298][32]^[299][33]

   On 2 April 2019, Canon introduces two new UHDgc 2/3-inch Portable Zoom
   Lenses designed For 4K UHD Broadcast Cameras.^[300][34]

2020–present

   [[301]edit]

   In July 2020, Canon recorded its first ever quarterly loss due to the
   [302]COVID-19 pandemic.^[303][35]

   In September 2020, [304]Fujitsu announced that it would provide Canon
   with a [305]Fujitsu Supercomputer PRIMEHPC FX1000 unit, to assist with
   its no-prototype development manufacturing initiative.^[306][36]

   In December 2020, Canon concluded its photographic-equipment print-ad
   series named "Wildlife as Canon Sees It". This series of ads began in
   1981 in [307]National Geographic magazine.^[308][37]

   In October 2023, Canon introduced its new [309]nanoimprint lithography
   manufacturing systems, which it claims are simpler and more affordable
   than [310]ASML's [311]extreme ultraviolet lithography systems. The
   system prints the desired circuit pattern onto the silicon wafer
   bypassing photolithography and can produce circuits equivalent to
   5 [312]nm scale.^[313][38]^[314][39]

Flash units

   [[371]edit]

   Main article: [372]Canon EOS flash system

   Canon produces a range of high-output flash units for its DSLR cameras,
   including the 270EX II, 320EX, 430EX II, 430EX III-RT, 470EX-AI, 580EX,
   580EX II, 600EX-RT, 600EXII-RT, EL-1, and EL-5 Speedlites. Canon also
   produces macro flash units, including the Macro Twin Lite and the Macro
   Ring Lite.

CMOS image sensor

   [[375]edit]

   Canon designs and manufactures [376]CMOS image sensors in-house for its
   imaging products and it has three dedicated fabs in [377]Japan. In
   2016, Canon, the fifth-largest [378]image sensor manufacturer in the
   world, decided to start selling the sensors to other
   companies.^[379][43] However, it does not plan to sell [380]smartphone
   image sensors to focus on the niche markets such as industrial and
   space observation.

   Although Canon had withdrawn from the so-called '[381]pixel count race'
   in the 2000s, it has been on the cutting edge as to the image sensor
   resolution in recent years. A demo of a 250MP image sensor was revealed
   in 2015^[382][44] and reported to be launched in 2020. In 2018, Canon
   launched a 120MP image sensor as a part of its latest BtoB
   offerings.^[383][45]

Printers

   [[384]edit]

   For many years, Canon was the principal maker of the print engines
   found in industry-standard laser printers. The first models of
   [385]Apple LaserWriter and the equivalent products made by HP used the
   Canon LBP-CX engine. The next models (LaserWriter II series, LaserJet
   II series) used the Canon LBP-SX engine. Later models used the Canon
   LBP-LX, LBP-EX, LBP-PX engines and many other Canon print engines.

   Following Canon's acquisition of the Dutch digital printing
   manufacturer [386]Océ in 2010, Canon continued to develop and
   manufacture printing systems, initially under the Océ brand name. On
   1.1.2020 the company Océ was officially renamed [387]Canon Production
   Printing.^[388][46]

   Canon has been sued over intentionally designing all-in-one printers
   that cannot scan when the printer is low on ink.^[389][47] Canon
   settled the lawsuit in 2023 without admitting guilt.^[390][47]

Digital copiers

   [[394]edit]

   Canon's largest division in terms of revenue is its multifunction
   copier division. Canon distributes its consumer and home office
   imageCLASS line though retail outlets and professional-grade
   imageRUNNER series through subsidiary Canon Solutions America and
   independent distributors. The professional-grade series ranges from
   small table tops to large digital presses.

Scanners

   [[395]edit]

   Canon manufactures a wide range of [396]flatbed scanners, [397]film
   scanners and [398]document scanners for home and business use,
   including the [399]Canon CanoScan 8800F. Some of its scanners employ
   LED inDirect Exposure (LiDE) technology, such that USB port is
   sufficient to power the scanner, and no additional power is required.

   Current printers use the proprietary BJNP protocol (USB over IP port
   8611).

Calculators

   [[402]edit]

   Canon produced a range of calculators in various applications,
   including handheld calculators, desktop calculators, printing
   calculators and scientific calculators. One model was the 1964 Canola
   130. It had 13 digits, a result of marketing research. The reason for
   the odd number of figures was based on selling it to the Japanese
   central bank. Given the low value of the Japanese Yen, 13 digits was a
   requirement of the banks.

   The calculator was built by germanium transistors and the display was a
   light pipe which gave an odd format.^[403][48]

Projectors

   [[404]edit]

   Canon produces a range of projectors.^[405][49]

Presenters

   [[406]edit]

   Canon offers a range of wireless presenters, from advanced green
   [407]laser presenters with back-lit screen display to basic red laser
   presentation clickers.

Virtual reality headset

   [[408]edit]

   Canon is developing a prototype [409]virtual reality headset (Canon
   VR). The headset offers a wider viewing angle (120°) than other VR
   devices but requires handles rather than a head strap. The headset is
   not yet available on the market.^[410][50] As of 2020, Canon produces
   and sells high-end AR ([411]augmented reality) headsets for enterprise
   users.^[412][51]

Manufacturing equipment

   [[413]edit]

   Canon is one of the world's top producers of semiconductor and display
   manufacturing equipment. Its subsidiary [414]Canon Tokki dominates the
   market of material deposition equipment, and instruments for
   manufacturing [415]OLED displays. Canon is also the leading
   manufacturer of display [416]photolithography equipment and one of the
   top 3 in the semiconductor lithography machine market. Once a leader of
   semiconductor lithography along with [417]Nikon, it has been dwarfed by
   [418]ASML, and as of 2017 its share in the overall market was less than
   5%. Still, Canon maintains a great presence in the i-line stepper
   market.^[[419]citation needed]

Discontinued products

   [[420]edit]

Computers

   [[421]edit]

   Main article: [422]Canon Computer Systems

   Canon introduced two [423]MSX home computer models in 1983, the V-10
   and the [424]V-20. Both offered just the minimum range of the MSX
   standards without any additional features. The V-20 was able to receive
   shooting data from the T90 Canon camera with the Data Memory Back T90
   expansion.

   Canon also sold a Canon AS100 PC^[425][52] for which you could get a
   color or monochrome display computer, shortly after the release of the
   [426]IBM PC. It was based on the [427]Intel 8088 processor and used
   [428]CP/M or [429]MS-DOS. Options included an 8 MB hard
   drive.^[430][53]

Operations

   [[431]edit]

   As of 2020, Canon is organized into four principal business
   segments:^[432][54]
    1. the Office Business Unit (the products of which include copying
       machines, digital production printers, large format inkjet
       printers, laser printers and multi-function devices);
    2. the Imaging System Business Unit (the products of which include
       broadcasting equipment, calculators, compact digital cameras,
       digital SLR cameras, digital video camcorders, image scanners,
       interchangeable lenses, inkjet [433]multifunction printers and
       single function inkjet printers);
    3. the Medical System Business Unit (the products of which include a
       broad range of medical equipment, such as [434]ophthalmic
       equipment, [435]CT, [436]ultrasound scanners, and [437]MRI); and,
    4. the Industry and Others Business Unit (the products of which
       include computers, handy terminals, magnetic heads, micromotors,
       flat panel display lithography equipment, semiconductor lithography
       equipment, and network cameras).

   Canon Inc. has 383 subsidiaries as of 31 June 2017. The number includes
   second-generation subsidiaries, for example, [438]Canon IT Solutions
   Inc.

   Canon's world headquarters is located at 30-2 Shimomaruko 3-chome,
   Ota-ku, Tokyo 146–8501, Japan. Canon has regional headquarters in
   America, Europe, Middle East, Africa, Japan, Asia and Oceania
   (including Australia & New Zealand). Canon Europe has two principal
   subsidiaries: Canon Europa NV (based in [439]Amstelveen, Netherlands)
   and Canon Europe Ltd. (based in [440]Uxbridge, UK).

   On 26 December 2003, Canon Inc. announced restructuring plans for three
   domestic Canon Group companies. The restructuring involved the merger
   of two companies and the spinning off of one.^[441][55]

   Canon generated total revenues of US$45,608 million in 2011, of which
   53.9% was by the Office Business Unit, 36.9% by the Consumer Business
   Unit and 11.8% by the Industry and Others Business
   Unit.^[442][56]^[443][note 2] In the same year, 31.3% of revenues were
   generated in Europe, 27.0% in the Americas, 22.2% in Asia and Oceania
   (excl. Japan) and 19.5% in Japan.^[444][56]

   Canon invested a total of US$3,946 million in research and development
   in 2011, equivalent to 8.7% of sales.^[445][56] In 2011, Canon was
   granted 2,813 patents in the United States, the third-highest number of
   any company (after [446]IBM and [447]Samsung Electronics).^[448][56]

Environmental record

   [[453]edit]

   A report by the environmental organization Clean Air-Cool Planet puts
   Canon at the top of a list of 56 companies the survey conducted on
   climate-friendly companies.^[454][57]
   [455][220px-AE-1_Film_Camera.jpg] AE-1 Film Camera. AE stood for
   Automatic Exposure.

   Canon has also launched three new calculators in Europe, called "Green
   Calculators", which are produced in part from recycled Canon
   copiers.^[456][58]

   The Canon Group has an environmental charter which looks at "offering
   products with a lower environmental burden through improvements in
   [457]resource efficiency, while eliminating anti-social activities that
   threaten the health and safety of mankind and the
   environment".^[458][59] In 2020, Canon joined [459]WIPO GREEN as an
   official partner in an effort to address climate change.^[460][60]

Climate scepticism

   [[461]edit]

   Meanwhile, the Canon Institute for Global Studies, a thinktank
   affiliated with Canon, has published many articles of [462]denying the
   climate crisis.^[463][61] Canon said in a statement to the Guardian
   that “the thinktank is not a Canon business, so we are not in a
   position to answer about its research or activities.”^[464][61]

   In response, the [465]Action Speak Louder, a corporate watchdog group,
   held a contest called "Cameras Don't Lie".^[466][62]

Charitable activities

   [[467]edit]

   In 2008, Canon donated financial support to help the estimated 5
   million people displaced by the earthquake that hit China's Sichuan
   province in May 2008.^[468][63] RMB 1 million was donated to the Red
   Cross Society of China shortly after the earthquake. Canon Inc., Japan,
   soon followed with a donation of RMB 10 million.^[469][63]

Sponsorships

   [[470]edit]

   [471][250px-Williams_FW14_front-right_2017_Williams_Conference_Centre.j
   pg] Canon sponsored [472]Williams FW14.

   In 1983, Canon came as the first title sponsors of the English football
   league [473]The Football League, which was named The Canon League from
   1983 to 1986, when the sponsorship was taken over by the [474]Today
   newspaper. Canon also sponsored Italian football club [475]Hellas
   Verona FC between 1982 and 1986^[476][64] including during the
   [477]1984–85 Serie A which they won.

   From 1967 to 2003 Canon sponsored the Greater Hartford Open, now
   [478]Travelers Championship.

   In [479]Formula One, Canon sponsored [480]Williams between 1985 and
   1993, while they won World Drivers Championships for [481]Nelson Piquet
   (1987), [482]Nigel Mansell (1992) and [483]Alain Prost (1993) and Four
   World Constructors Championships (1986, 1987, 1992, 1993). In the
   [484]2009 Singapore Grand Prix, Canon sponsored [485]Brawn GP.

   Between 1994 and 1997 they also sponsored the [486]South Sydney
   Rabbitohs.

   Since 2006, Canon has been helping the Red Cross provide support to 13
   Red Cross National Societies across Europe, with focus on youth
   projects. Support from Canon includes financial contributions and
   donations of imaging equipment, including cameras, copying machines and
   digital radiography devices, as well as volunteer activities.^[487][65]

   Canon Europe has been a partner of [488]World Press Photo for 16 years.
   World Press Photo promotes the professional standards in photography;
   organises the largest international contest for professional
   photojournalists; and acts as a worldwide platform for press
   photography.^[489][66]

   Canon Asia sponsored many competitions such as [490]Canon Photomarathon
   and reality TV show [491]Photo Face-Off. The latter is a reality TV
   show in which professional photographer [492]Justin Mott is the judge
   and competes against amateur photographers. [493]Justin started filming
   [494]season 3 in April 2016 and that season aired at the end of that
   same year.^[495][67]

       name is written as Kiyanon (キヤノン). This is due to the consideration

       by using Kiyanon.^[503][3]
