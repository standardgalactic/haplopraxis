   Chemical compound

   3-Methylhistidine (3-MH) is a [99]post-translationally modified
   [100]amino acid which is excreted in human
   [101]urine.^[102][1]^[103][2] Urinary concentration of
   3-methylhistidine is a [104]biomarker for [105]skeletal muscle
   [106]protein breakdown in humans who have been subject to muscle
   injury.^[107][1]^[108][3]^[109][4] Urinary 3-methylhistidine
   concentrations are also elevated from consumption of [110]soy-based
   products and meat, particularly chicken.^[111][1]

Biochemistry

   [[112]edit]

   3-Methylhistidine is a [113]metabolic product that is produced in the
   body via the [114]enzymatic [115]methylation of [116]histidine during
   [117]peptide bond synthesis and the methylation of [118]actin and
   [119]myosin.^[120][1]^[121][2]

Detection in body fluids

   [[122]edit]

   The normal concentration of 3-methylhistidine in the urine of healthy
   adult humans has been detected and quantified in a range of
   3.63–69.27 micromoles per millimole (μmol/mmol) of [123]creatinine,
   with most studies reporting the average urinary concentration between
   15–20 μmol/mmol of [124]creatinine.^[125][1] The average concentration
   of 3-methylhistidine in human [126]blood plasma has been detected and
   quantified at 2.85 [127]micromolar (μM) with a range of
   0.0–5.9 μM.^[128][1] The average concentration of 3-methylhistidine in
   human [129]cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) has been detected and quantified
   at 3.82 μM with a range of 1.39–6.25 μM.^[130][1]
