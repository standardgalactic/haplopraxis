   [289][220px-East-Hem_1100ad.jpg] The Eastern Hemisphere in 1100

   Year 1100 ([290]MC) was a [291]leap year starting on Sunday in the
   [292]Julian calendar, the 1100th year of the [293]Common Era (CE) and
   [294]Anno Domini (AD) designations, the 100th year of the [295]2nd
   millennium, the 100th and last year of the [296]11th century, and the
   1st year of the [297]1100s decade. In the [298]proleptic Gregorian
   calendar, it was a [299]non-leap century year starting on Monday (like
   [300]1900).

Events

   [[301]edit]

By place

   [[302]edit]

Levant

   [[303]edit]

     * [304]January – The Seljuk ruler [305]Mahmud I is expelled from
       [306]Baghdad by his brother [307]Barkiyaruq, but Mahmud manages to
       retake the city, during his spring offensive.^[308][1]
     * [309]May or [310]June – [311]Raymond IV (Saint-Gilles) sails to
       [312]Constantinople to obtain the support of Emperor [313]Alexios I
       (Komnenos), in his attempt to seize [314]Tripoli.^[315][2]
     * [316]August 1 – A [317]Genoese fleet leaves [318]Italy, to support
       the [319]Crusaders' efforts to conquer the coastal cities; the
       ships reach [320]Latakia on [321]September 25.^[322][3]
     * [323]August – [324]Battle of Melitene: [325]Bohemond I is captured
       by the [326]Danishmends, leaving [327]Tancred as regent of the
       [328]Principality of Antioch for two years.
     * [329]August 20 – With the [330]support of a [331]Venetian fleet,
       the Crusaders under Tancred capture the coastal city of [332]Haifa.
     * [333]December 25 – French Crusader [334]Baldwin I is crowned first
       [335]King of Jerusalem at the [336]Church of the Nativity in
       [337]Bethlehem, by [338]Daimbert, the new [339]Latin Patriarch of
       Jerusalem, following the death of the previous ruler, Baldwin's
       brother [340]Godfrey of Bouillon, on [341]July 18.^[342][4]
     * After a success over the Armenians of [343]Cilicia and the Emirate
       of [344]Aleppo, [345]Baldwin of Bourcq becomes [346]Count of
       Edessa, with the support of Daimbert.
     * [347]Genoa, [348]Venice and [349]Pisa gain trading privileges from
       the [350]Crusader states, in return for their service during the
       conquest of the coastal cities.

Europe

   [[351]edit]

   [352][150px-Death_of_William_Rufus.jpg] 2 August: death of [353]William
   II of England during a hunt, killed by an arrow of [354]Walter Tirel.
     * [355]August 2 – King [356]William II (or William Rufus) dies in a
       [357]hunting accident in the [358]New Forest. Sir [359]Walter Tirel
       is accused of having shot the arrow, but flees the country to avoid
       a trial. [360]Henry I claims the throne.
     * [361]August 5 – Henry I is crowned [362]King of England, at
       [363]Westminster Abbey. The power of the new monarch is
       ill-assured, and to mollify the barons he has to grant them the
       [364]Charter of Liberties, one of the first examples of a written
       [365]constitution in [366]Europe.^[367][5]
     * [368]August 30 – After the failure of the [369]Council of Liubech
       in [370]1097, the Congress of Vytechev establishes peace and the
       feudal system in [371]Kievan Rus; the princes come to an agreement
       to share the country between them. [372]Sviatopolk II of Kiev
       becomes the first Grand Prince.
     * [373]September 16 – [374]Battle of Malagon: The Almoravid army
       defeats the Castellan troops.^[375][6]
     * [376]September 23 – [377]Anselm, archbishop of [378]Canterbury
       returns from [379]exile, at the invitation of Henry I.
     * [380]October 18 – [381]Peter I of Aragon conquers [382]Barbastro
       (modern [383]Spain) from the hands of the
       [384]Almoravids.^[385][7]^[386][8]
     * [387]November 11 – Henry I marries [388]Matilda of Scotland, the
       daughter of King [389]Malcolm III and a direct descendant of the
       Saxon king [390]Edmund Ironside.
     * [391]November 18 – The Council of [392]Poitiers opens, but is soon
       forcibly closed by [393]William IX, duke of [394]Aquitaine, as the
       bishops are about to excommunicate King [395]Philip I once
       more.^[396][9]
     * [397]December 25 – Philip I elevates his son [398]Louis VI as
       co-ruler to the government of the realm.
     * Philip I conquers the [399]Vexin area, and adds the city of
       [400]Bourges and the province of [401]Berry to his
       estate.^[402][10]
     * In [403]Iceland, the [404]Althing decides that the laws should be
       transferred to a written form (approximate date).
     * Intense urban activity in north and central Europe: [405]Kalmar
       ([406]Kungälv) and [407]Varberg ([408]Sweden) are chartered; The
       cities of [409]Aach (southern [410]Germany) and Nakléřov in
       [411]Bohemia are created. The castle of [412]Burg Eppstein is built
       in central Germany.
     * Henry I grants the ownership of [413]Carisbrooke Castle on the
       [414]Isle of Wight to [415]Richard de Redvers, a Norman nobleman.

Africa

   [[416]edit]

     * A collective of [417]Tuareg trading clans decide to permanently
       settle the city of [418]Timbuktu (modern [419]Mali) north of
       [420]Djenné along the [421]Niger River. Timbuktu will later achieve
       fame as a center of Islamic learning. The [422]Sankore,
       [423]Djinguereber and [424]Sidi Yahya mosques are among Timbuktu's
       most famous religious and scholarly institutions (approximate
       date).

China

   [[425]edit]

     * [426]February 23 – [427]Emperor Zhezong dies after a 15-year reign.
       He is succeeded by his 17-year-old brother [428]Huizong as ruler of
       the [429]Song dynasty. At about this date, the Chinese population
       reaches around 100 million and in [430]Kaifeng, his capital, the
       number of registered citizens within the walls is about 1,050,000
       with the army stationed here boosting the overall populace to some
       1.4 million people.
     * The [431]Liao dynasty crushes the [432]Zubu, a tribute state of the
       [433]Khitan Empire, and takes their [434]khan prisoner.

Americas

   [[435]edit]

     * [436]Oraibi, a [437]Hopi village in [438]Navajo County, becomes the
       oldest populated settlement in modern-day [439]Arizona (modern-day
       [440]United States).
     * The [441]Ancestral Puebloans culture, located in the modern-day
       [442]Four Corners (United States), rises (approximate date).
     * The city of [443]Cusco (modern [444]Peru) is founded (approximate
       date).

Religion

   [[446]edit]

     * [447]September 8 – Antipope [448]Clement III dies at [449]Civita
       Castellana after a 20-year reign in opposition to the legitimate
       popes [450]Gregory VII, [451]Victor III and [452]Urban II.
       Supporters of Emperor [453]Henry IV in [454]Rome choose
       [455]Theodoric as his successor.
     * November – The council of Poitiers decrees that the followers of
       [456]Robert of Arbrissel have to settle down and live under a rule,
       leading to the foundation of [457]Fontevraud Abbey.^[458][11]
     * [459]Frederick I becomes archbishop of [460]Cologne, and begins the
       construction of the castle of Volmarstein.
     * The [461]Stift St. Georgen Abbey is founded near [462]Sankt Georgen
       am Längsee (modern [463]Austria).
     * The Diocese of [464]Faroe is founded (approximate date).
