   The 110-vertex Iofinova–Ivanov graph is, in [73]graph theory, a
   [74]semi-symmetric [75]cubic graph with 110 vertices and 165 edges.

Properties

   [[76]edit]

   Iofinova and Ivanov proved in 1985 the existence of five and only five
   semi-symmetric cubic bipartite graphs whose automorphism groups act
   [77]primitively on each partition.^[78][1] The smallest has 110
   vertices. The others have 126, 182, 506 and 990.^[79][2] The 126-vertex
   Iofinova–Ivanov graph is also known as the [80]Tutte 12-cage.

   The [81]diameter of the 110-vertex Iofinova–Ivanov graph, the greatest
   distance between any pair of vertices, is 7. Its radius is likewise 7.
   Its [82]girth is 10.

   It is [83]3-connected and 3-edge-connected: to make it disconnected at
   least three edges, or at least three vertices, must be removed.

Coloring

   [[84]edit]

   The [85]chromatic number of the 110-vertex Iofina-Ivanov graph is 2:
   its vertices can be 2-colored so that no two vertices of the same color
   are joined by an edge. Its [86]chromatic index is 3: its edges can be
   3-colored so that no two edges of the same color met at a vertex.

Semi-symmetry

   [[91]edit]

   Few graphs show semi-symmetry: most edge-transitive graphs are also
   vertex-transitive. The smallest semi-symmetric graph is the [92]Folkman
   graph, with 20 vertices, which is 4-regular. The three smallest cubic
   semi-symmetric graphs are the [93]Gray graph, with 54 vertices, this
   the smallest of the Iofina-Ivanov graphs with 110, and the
   [94]Ljubljana graph with 112.^[95][4]^[96][5] It is only for the five
   Iofina-Ivanov graphs that the symmetry group acts primitively on each
   partition of the vertices.
