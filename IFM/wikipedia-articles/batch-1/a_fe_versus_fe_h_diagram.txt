   From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
   (Redirected from [53](a/Fe) versus (Fe/H) diagram)
   Graph used in astrophysics
   The correct title of this article is [α/Fe] versus [Fe/H] diagram. The
   substitution of any [54]brackets is due to [55]technical restrictions.
   [56]The diagram shows the iron abundance on the x-axis and the
   abundance of alpha process elements on the y-axis. The diagram shows
   the iron abundance, [Fe/H], on the x-axis and the abundance of alpha
   process elements, [α/Fe], on the y-axis. The data from [57]Wallerstein
   (1962) is shown as red circles on top of the 2020 public data from the
   large scale stellar spectroscopic surveys [58]APOGEE (DR16) and
   [59]GALAH (DR3).

   The [α/Fe] versus [Fe/H] diagram is a type of graph commonly used in
   stellar and galactic astrophysics. It shows the [60]logarithmic ratio
   number densities of diagnostic elements in [61]stellar atmospheres
   compared to the [62]solar value. The x-axis represents the abundance of
   iron (Fe) vs. hydrogen (H), that is, [Fe/H]. The y-axis represents the
   combination of one or several of the [63]alpha process elements ([64]O,
   [65]Ne, [66]Mg, [67]Si, [68]S, [69]Ar, [70]Ca, and [71]Ti) compared to
   iron (Fe), denoted as [α/Fe].

   These diagrams enable the assessment of [72]nucleosynthesis channels
   and [73]galactic evolution in samples of stars as a first-order
   approximation. They are among the most commonly used tools for Galactic
   population analysis of the Milky Way. The diagrams use abundance ratios
   normalised to the Sun, (placing the Sun at (0,0) in the diagram). This
   normalisation allows for the easy identification of stars in the
   Galactic stellar high-alpha disk (historically known as the Galactic
   stellar [74]thick disk), typically enhanced in [α/Fe], and stars in the
   Galactic stellar low-alpha disk (historically known as the Galactic
   stellar [75]thin disk), with [α/Fe] values as low as the Sun.
   Furthermore, the diagrams facilitate the identification of stars that
   are likely born in times or environments significantly different from
   the stellar disk. This includes metal-poor stars (with low [Fe/H] <
   -1), which likely belong to the [76]stellar halo or [77]accreted
   features.^[78][1]

History

   [[79]edit]

   [80]George Wallerstein and [81]Beatrice Tinsley were early users of the
   [α/Fe] vs. [Fe/H] diagrams. In 1962, [82]George Wallerstein noted,
   based on the analysis of a sample of 34 Galactic field stars, that "the
   [α/Fe] distribution seems to consist of a normal distribution about
   zero, plus seven stars with [α/Fe] > 0.20. These may be called
   [α/Fe]-rich stars."^[83][2]

   In 1979, [84]Beatrice Tinsley used the interpretation of these
   observations with the theory throughout her work on Stellar lifetimes
   and abundance ratios in chemical evolution. While discussing oxygen as
   one of the alpha-process elements, she wrote, 'As anticipated, the
   observed [O/Fe] excess in metal-poor stars can be explained
   qualitatively if much of the iron comes from SN I. [...] The essential
   ingredient in accounting for the [O/Fe] excess is that a significant
   fraction of oxygen must come from stars with shorter lives than those
   that make much of the iron.'^[85][3] In 1980, in Evolution of the Stars
   and Gas in Galaxies, she said, 'Relative abundances of elements heavier
   than helium provide information on both nucleosynthesis and galactic
   evolution [...].'^[86][4]

   These relative abundances and the diagrams depicting different relative
   abundances are now among the most commonly used diagnostic tools of
   Galactic Archaeology. Bensby et al. (2014) used them to explore the
   Milky Way disk in the solar neighbourhood.^[87][5] Hayden et al. (2015)
   used them for their work on the chemical cartography of our Milky Way
   disk.^[88][6] It has been suggested that the diagram be named for
   Tinsley and Wallerstein.^[89][7]

   abundance relative to hydrogen [Fe/H] - a tracer of [91]stellar
   metallicity - and the enrichment of [92]alpha process elements relative
   to iron, [α/Fe].
