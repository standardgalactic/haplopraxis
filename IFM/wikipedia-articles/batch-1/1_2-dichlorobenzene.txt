   Chemical compound

   1,2-Dichlorobenzene, or orthodichlorobenzene (ODCB), is an [138]aryl
   chloride and isomer of [139]dichlorobenzene with the formula
   C[6]H[4]Cl[2]. This colourless liquid is poorly soluble in water but
   miscible with most organic solvents. It is a derivative of
   [140]benzene, consisting of two adjacent [141]chlorine atoms.

   It is mainly used as a [142]precursor chemical in the synthesis of
   [143]agrochemicals, as a preferred solvent for dissolving and working
   with [144]fullerenes, as an [145]insecticide, and in softening and
   removing carbon-based contamination on metal surfaces.

Production and uses

   [[146]edit]

   1,2-Dichlorobenzene is obtained as a side-product of the production of
   [147]chlorobenzene:

          C^
          [6]H^
          [5]Cl + Cl^
          [2] → C^
          [6]H^
          [4]Cl^
          [2] + HCl

   The reaction also affords the 1,4- and small amounts of the 1,3-isomer.
   The 1,4- isomer is preferred over the 1,2- isomer due to [148]steric
   hindrance. The [149]1,3- isomer is uncommon because it is a meta-
   compound, while chlorine, like all halogens, is an [150]ortho/para-
   director in terms of [151]electrophilic aromatic substitution.

   It is mainly used as a precursor to [152]1,2-dichloro-4-nitrobenzene,
   an intermediate in the synthesis of [153]agrochemicals.^[154][5] In
   terms of niche applications, 1,2-dichlorobenzene is a versatile,
   high-boiling solvent. It is a preferred solvent for dissolving and
   working with [155]fullerenes. It is an [156]insecticide for termites
   and locust borers, historically used by the [157]United States Forest
   Service to combat widespread bark beetle outbreaks.^[158][6]

   1,2-Dichlorobenzene is also used in softening and removing carbon-based
   contamination on metal surfaces.^[159][7]

Safety

   [[160]edit]

   Data from human exposure to 1,2-dichlorobenzene shows that
   concentrations of 100 ppm have been reported to cause sporadic
   irritation of the eyes and respiratory tract.^[161][8] The
   [162]Occupational Safety and Health Administration and the
   [163]National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health have set
   occupational exposure limits at a ceiling of 50 ppm, over an eight-hour
   workday.^[164][9]
