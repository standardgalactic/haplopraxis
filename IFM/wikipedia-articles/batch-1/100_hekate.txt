About

   [[163]edit]
   [164][220px-000100-asteroid_shape_model_%28100%29_Hekate.png] 3D convex
   shape model of Hekate

   This is a stony [165]S-type asteroid with a diameter of 87+5
   −4 km and a sidereal [166]rotation period of 27.07 h.^[167][6] It
   orbits in the same region of space as the [168]Hygiea asteroid family,
   though it is actually an unrelated interloper. However, its geometric
   [169]albedo of 0.22±0.03^[170][6] is too high, and it is of the wrong
   spectral class to be part of the dark [171]carbonaceous Hygiea family.
   It is listed as a member of the [172]Hecuba group of asteroids that
   orbit near the 2:1 [173]mean-motion resonance with
   [174]Jupiter.^[175][7]

   Hekate was the 100th asteroid to be discovered, by Canadian-American
   astronomer [176]J. C. Watson (his fourth discovery) on July 11,
   1868.^[177][8] It is named after [178]Hecate, the goddess of
   [179]witchcraft in [180]Greek mythology, but its name also commemorates
   it as the hundredth asteroid, as ἑκατόν (hekaton) is Greek for
   'hundred'.

   A Hekatean [181]occultation of a star was observed on July 14, 2003,
   from [182]New Zealand.
