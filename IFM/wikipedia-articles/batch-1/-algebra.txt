   In [124]mathematics, and more specifically in [125]abstract algebra, a
   *-algebra (or involutive algebra; read as "star-algebra") is a
   mathematical structure consisting of two involutive rings R and A,
   where R is commutative and A has the structure of an [126]associative
   algebra over R. Involutive algebras generalize the idea of a number
   system equipped with conjugation, for example the [127]complex numbers
   and [128]complex conjugation, [129]matrices over the complex numbers
   and [130]conjugate transpose, and [131]linear operators over a
   [132]Hilbert space and [133]Hermitian adjoints. However, it may happen
   that an algebra admits no [134]involution.^[135][a]

   Look up [136]* or [137]star in Wiktionary, the free dictionary.

   In [203]mathematics, a *-ring is a [204]ring with a map * : A → A that
   is an [205]antiautomorphism and an [206]involution.

   More precisely, * is required to satisfy the following
   properties:^[207][1]
     * (x + y)* = x* + y*
     * (x y)* = y* x*
     * 1* = 1
     * (x*)* = x

   for all x, y in A.

   This is also called an involutive ring, involutory ring, and ring with
   involution. The third axiom is implied by the second and fourth axioms,
   making it redundant.

   Elements such that x* = x are called [208]self-adjoint.^[209][2]

   Archetypical examples of a *-ring are fields of [210]complex numbers
   and [211]algebraic numbers with [212]complex conjugation as the
   involution. One can define a [213]sesquilinear form over any *-ring.

   Also, one can define *-versions of algebraic objects, such as
   [214]ideal and [215]subring, with the requirement to be
   *-[216]invariant: x ∈ I ⇒ x* ∈ I and so on.

   *-rings are unrelated to [217]star semirings in the theory of
   computation.

*-algebra

   [[218]edit]

   A *-algebra A is a *-ring,^[219][b] with involution * that is an
   [220]associative algebra over a [221]commutative *-ring R with
   involution ′, such that (r x)* = r′ x*  ∀r ∈ R, x ∈ A.^[222][3]

   The base *-ring R is often the complex numbers (with ′ acting as
   complex conjugation).

   It follows from the axioms that * on A is [223]conjugate-linear in R,
   meaning

          (λ x + μ y)* = λ′ x* + μ′ y*

   for λ, μ ∈ R, x, y ∈ A.

   A *-homomorphism f : A → B is an [224]algebra homomorphism that is
   compatible with the involutions of A and B, i.e.,
     * f(a*) = f(a)* for all a in A.^[225][2]

Philosophy of the *-operation

   [[226]edit]

   The *-operation on a *-ring is analogous to [227]complex conjugation on
   the complex numbers. The *-operation on a *-algebra is analogous to
   taking [228]adjoints in complex [229]matrix algebras.

Notation

   [[230]edit]

   The * involution is a [231]unary operation written with a postfixed
   star glyph centered above or near the [232]mean line:

          x ↦ x*, or
          x ↦ x^∗ ([233]TeX: x^*),

   but not as "x∗"; see the [234]asterisk article for details.

Examples

   [[235]edit]

     * Any [236]commutative ring becomes a *-ring with the trivial
       ([237]identical) involution.
     * The most familiar example of a *-ring and a *-algebra over
       [238]reals is the field of complex numbers C where * is just
       [239]complex conjugation.
     * More generally, a [240]field extension made by adjunction of a
       [241]square root (such as the [242]imaginary unit √−1) is a
       *-algebra over the original field, considered as a
       trivially-*-ring. The * [243]flips the sign of that square root.
     * A [244]quadratic integer ring (for some D) is a commutative *-ring
       with the * defined in the similar way; [245]quadratic fields are
       *-algebras over appropriate quadratic integer rings.
     * [246]Quaternions, [247]split-complex numbers, [248]dual numbers,
       and possibly other [249]hypercomplex number systems form *-rings
       (with their built-in conjugation operation) and *-algebras over
       reals (where * is trivial). None of the three is a complex algebra.
     * [250]Hurwitz quaternions form a non-commutative *-ring with the
       quaternion conjugation.
     * The [251]matrix algebra of n × n [252]matrices over R with * given
       by the [253]transposition.
     * The matrix algebra of n × n matrices over C with * given by the
       [254]conjugate transpose.
     * Its generalization, the [255]Hermitian adjoint in the algebra of
       [256]bounded linear operators on a [257]Hilbert space also defines
       a *-algebra.
     * The [258]polynomial ring R[x] over a commutative trivially-*-ring R
       is a *-algebra over R with P *(x) = P (−x).
     * If (A, +, ×, *) is simultaneously a *-ring, an [259]algebra over a
       ring R (commutative), and (r x)* = r (x*)  ∀r ∈ R, x ∈ A, then A is
       a *-algebra over R (where * is trivial).
          + As a partial case, any *-ring is a *-algebra over
            [260]integers.
     * Any commutative *-ring is a *-algebra over itself and, more
       generally, over any its [261]*-subring.
     * For a commutative *-ring R, its [262]quotient by any its
       [263]*-ideal is a *-algebra over R.
          + For example, any commutative trivially-*-ring is a *-algebra
            over its [264]dual numbers ring, a *-ring with non-trivial *,
            because the quotient by ε = 0 makes the original ring.
          + The same about a commutative ring K and its polynomial ring
            K[x]: the quotient by x = 0 restores K.
     * In [265]Hecke algebra, an involution is important to the
       [266]Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial.
     * The [267]endomorphism ring of an [268]elliptic curve becomes a
       *-algebra over the integers, where the involution is given by
       taking the [269]dual isogeny. A similar construction works for
       [270]abelian varieties with a [271]polarization, in which case it
       is called the [272]Rosati involution (see Milne's lecture notes on
       abelian varieties).

   [273]Involutive Hopf algebras are important examples of *-algebras
   (with the additional structure of a compatible [274]comultiplication);
   the most familiar example being:
     * The [275]group Hopf algebra: a [276]group ring, with involution
       given by g ↦ g^−1.

Additional structures

   [[280]edit]

   Many properties of the [281]transpose hold for general *-algebras:
     * The [282]Hermitian elements form a [283]Jordan algebra;
     * The skew Hermitian elements form a [284]Lie algebra;
     * If 2 is invertible in the *-ring, then the operators ⁠1/2⁠(1 + *)
       and ⁠1/2⁠(1 − *) are [285]orthogonal idempotents,^[286][2] called
       symmetrizing and anti-symmetrizing, so the algebra decomposes as a
       direct sum of [287]modules ([288]vector spaces if the *-ring is a
       field) of symmetric and anti-symmetric (Hermitian and skew
       Hermitian) elements. These spaces do not, generally, form
       associative algebras, because the idempotents are [289]operators,
       not elements of the algebra.

Skew structures

   [[290]edit]

   Given a *-ring, there is also the map −* : x ↦ −x*. It does not define
   a *-ring structure (unless the [291]characteristic is 2, in which case
   −* is identical to the original *), as 1 ↦ −1, neither is it
   antimultiplicative, but it satisfies the other axioms (linear,
   involution) and hence is quite similar to *-algebra where x ↦ x*.

   Elements fixed by this map (i.e., such that a = −a*) are called skew
   Hermitian.

   For the complex numbers with complex conjugation, the real numbers are
   the Hermitian elements, and the imaginary numbers are the skew
   Hermitian.
