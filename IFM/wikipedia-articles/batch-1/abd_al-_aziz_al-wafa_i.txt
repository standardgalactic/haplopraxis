   From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
   15th-century Egyptian astronomer and mathematician
   Abd al-'Aziz ibn Mahammad al-Qahiri al-Shafi' al-Wafa'i
   Born 811 AH /1408
   [57]Cairo, [58]Egyptian Mamluk Sultanate
   Died 876 AH/ 1471 (aged 63)
   Nationality [59]Egyptian
   Occupation(s) Astronomer, mathematician, time keeper
   Era [60]Mamluk era
   Notable work
     * Risalah al-'Amal bi al-Rub' al-Mujayyab

   'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Mahammad al-Qahiri al-Shafi' al-Wafa'i known as Ibn
   al-Aqba'i (1408–1471) was an [61]Egyptian astronomer and mathematician
   in the 15th century. He was born in 811 H.E. (1408 AD) and died in 876
   H.E (1471 AD). Some sources say that he died in 874 or 879 H.E. He was
   a student of [62]Ibn al-Majdi and [63]Nur al-din al-Naqqash. He became
   a time keeper at the [64]Mosque of Sultan al-Muayyad in [65]Cairo. He
   also became the chief of the time keepers of the [66]Azhar mosque and
   the Maridani Mosque. He was interested in the calculation of the local
   times of cities based on their latitude and longitude. He invented an
   astronomical device called Da'irah al-Mu'addal.^[67][1]^[68][2]

   He wrote about 40 books and 20 treatises on [69]mathematics and
   [70]astronomy, none of them published. Among his works is the
   manuscript Risalah al-'Amal bi al-Rub' al-Mujayyab
